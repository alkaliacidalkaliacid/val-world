## A. Contradictions with canon / settings

- **A1 (medium-high, main point) - The count reaches 414, but no 414th man exists yet.** In the "Open / swallow" section Nia counts: "Four hundred and twelve. Four hundred and thirteen. Four hundred and fourteen. Each one a name in the ledger, and yours is at the top." The established arithmetic (ch004 review, ch006, migration resolution, MEMORY) is strict: 412 men before Bog, Bog is the 413th, and a 414th man belongs to the Mississippi arc, far later. Read as a count of men, 414 is impossible at this point in the story. A reconciliation exists inside the same chapter - a later section says "Four hundred and twelve. And then the new ones, every day. A different ledger now" - i.e. a second ledger counting Bog's repeated meals, not men. But the 414 sentence itself says "each one a name in the ledger", which pulls it back to the men-ledger. Needs a ruling: (a) the numbers after 413 are the new daily ledger (then the wording should be clarified), or (b) 414 is an error and should be cut. See also B1.
- **A2 (medium) - The litany gains a fourth ingredient, once.** Nia's private litany is fixed on her card and used seven other times in this chapter as "Tomatoes. Scotch bonnet. Thyme." In the "Hold it. Move it. Stir it." section it appears as "Tomatoes. Scotch bonnet. Thyme. Bay leaf." - and she immediately repeats it in the three-item form. Two readings: (a) a slip - bay leaf should be removed for consistency with the card and the chapter's own other occurrences; (b) a deliberate memory beat (she is reconstructing Daniel's jollof from memory in this very section, and the recipe may genuinely include bay leaf while the mantra does not). If (b), no prose change, but the distinction (recipe = 4 items, mantra = 3) should be recorded so later chapters stay consistent.
- **A3 (medium) - Slipstream's brother is a character who exists nowhere else.** "Slipstream's brother told me that once, before the corridor, over a bad cup of coffee. 'Every cage has a door, Nia...'" Slipstream's card (Elena Marquez, 29, Mexican, rescue specialist) mentions no brother; no brother appears in the minor cast, the roster, or any other chapter (verified by search - the only other Slipstream mention is ch054's list of the Bound). This is prose from the original draft, not a review-pass invention, but under the style guide's Florence convention (new characters only when the user specifies them) he should either be recorded (minor cast: name unknown, pre-corridor acquaintance of Nia's) or the attribution changed (e.g. to Slipstream herself). The quoted advice itself is load-bearing for Nia's patience strategy, so the beat should stay; only its source needs a ruling.
- **A4 (medium) - "I could break this thing. One flick." vs. collar suppression.** Nia's interior: "I could break this thing. One flick. One finger, the way I used to snap a car door off its hinges. A Val's body is armor everywhere but the places he forgot to guard... The collar says no." Canon: the collar "suppresses or sharply limits" powers; the ch003 ruling locked her collared capability at mundane-but-exceptional strength (enough to make a Val bleed with a knee). Whether one flick, collared, could actually maim Bog is a bigger claim. Readings: (a) it is fact - mundane strength plus a genuinely unarmored point, and the collar's suppression / pain response is the only restraint (then lock it on her card, because later chapters' tension depends on whether Bog is actually in physical danger at every meal); (b) it is Nia's revenge fantasy / self-reassurance, true only uncollared (then no card change, but later reviews should not treat Bog as one flick from death). Needs a ruling; no prose change proposed either way.

Everywhere else the household has exactly one robot: Bog's card ("a helper robot (squeaky wheel, tactful, obeys Bog)"), the minor cast (singular), and ch014-ch017, where a single household robot with the squeaky wheel serves towels, water and tactful exits. chapters/ch007.md already records "Bog owns three helper robots he had not been using", so the prose and the chapter summary agree; the cards are the odd ones out. ## B. Internal consistency and character voice

- **B1 (linked to A1) - Two ledgers, one vocabulary.** Within one chapter Nia keeps: the count of men (412 + Bog = 413), a "different ledger" of daily repetitions, a pebble-count of Bog's praises ("So I count the pebbles instead"), and a debt ledger ("yours is at the top"). The multiplicity is thematically right - counting is her resistance - but the 414 sentence blurs which ledger is speaking. Whatever A1's ruling, this is the sentence a careful reader will trip on.
- **B2 (observation, pattern from ch006) - Bog's decisive lines again arrive reported, not shown.** The ritual's key instructions ("Don't stop", "Don't swallow", "Hold it. Move it. Stir it.", "Open") exist only inside Nia's interior, as remembered / ongoing voice; and the final section's trigger - Bog calling her gift "a true calling", the tomato-plant speech about Daniel ("What a waste." "A shame he won't get another chance." "That's life.") - is paraphrased by Nia, never quoted at length. The montage form licenses this (ch006's B1 was the same pattern, and there the user chose to surface one line), and the paraphrase arguably hurts more than quotation would. Flagged for awareness only: the chapter's climax (the retaliatory ***Slurrrrrrrrrrrrrrp***) answers a speech the reader never actually hears.
- **B3 (minor) - The kitchen scene's offer is left hanging.** Bog offers to do the chores with her and turn on the robots; Nia's reaction (furious color, contempt, turning back to her meal) closes the scene without the offer being accepted, refused, or seen again - later chapters show the single robot active but no trio, and Bog still watching her work. Montage logic covers the cut, but combined with A5 the robots thread is the chapter's loosest end.
- Voice: Nia is fully consistent with her card - blunt arithmetic, ledger-keeping, compliance reframed as reconnaissance ("A hunter holds still because she's learning the animal", the smile reframed as a trap, "Surely. ...Surely so."). Bog, in the two external scenes, is the card's Bog: the shy, delighted teacher of the prayer scene ("Perfect... You're a clever girl, aren't you?") and the deadpan of "I thought it looked homey" - devotion presented as appetite, cruelty entirely without malice ("I only wanted to watch you work because you looked so cute in the apron").
- Refrain density (measured): "Amara. Chidi. Daniel." x9, the ingredient litany x8, "Hold the line" x7, "Four hundred and twelve" x7 in 3,895 words. This is the ritual's music and the chapter's stated subject (repetition as both erosion and resistance), but it is also the densest refrain use so far - noted for the anti-repetition watch, not as a defect.
- Figurative-language policy: observed. The prayer "rang through the little kitchen like a bell struck cleanly" is an explicit simile for her delivery, not an effect; the bells throughout are physical sound; the spirits are not invoked as actors in this chapter at all.

## C. Craft, as a work

- Function: ch006 was the first meal as an event; ch007 is the meal become a system. The chapter's real plot is erosion measured in small surrenders - her voice breaking because her body "waits for the pat on the head instead", forgetting Daniel's jollof, catching herself smiling and renaming the smile a ruse. "That's what's trembling. Not my courage. My expectation." is the book's thesis advancing, exactly where ch006 left it ("not with the pain").
- Structure: ten numbered insults as a overture (a table of contents of her defiance, several recycled from ch006 - correctly, since this is literally the same insult set becoming routine), then interior movements keyed to the ritual's stages, broken twice by external scenes. The two breaks are well chosen: the prayer scene (the only place Bog's delight gets full dialogue, and the chapter's most painful beat - she is praised for perfect gratitude) and the kitchen scene (the only slapstick, and the only place her domestic grievance - the mismatched, miswired kitchen - gets spoken aloud).
- The kitchen scene also quietly does world work: Bog's all-Earth mismatched appliances, the hum of bad wiring, and "My husband would've fixed it in an afternoon" set Daniel up as the anti-Bog (competent, unasked, present) more efficiently than any flashback - and Bog's quiet "He sounds like a good man" before he recovers into brightness is his most decent moment so far, which is what makes the apron confession land as a gut-punch right after.
- Middle-section risk: the three hold / stir / open movements are deliberate variations on one beat (endure the instruction, notice what it asks of her, reassert the litany). As ritual depiction this is the point; as reading, it is the stretch most likely to be skimmed, and the chapter's length makes it felt. Noted as an observation - the split / trim questions are settled (see header) and no cut is proposed.
- Ending: the retaliatory slurp is the perfect button for this chapter - her one available weapon, fired in the only language Bog respects, after a section spent swallowing a speech about Daniel. Comic, obscene and genuinely defiant at once: the tone promise in a single sound effect.
- Tone vs. the promise: the fullest mix since ch003 - erotic charge as subject and sensation throughout, slapstick (prize marrow at a country fair, the kitchen inventory, the final slurp), deranged (a liturgy of petition and thanksgiving built around the meal, taught by collar punishment). The derangement here is systemic, not decorative, which is the book at its most distinctive.
