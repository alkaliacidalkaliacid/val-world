## B. Internal consistency and voice

- B1 (observation, keep recommended) — "a man who… had been dead for four days and eaten by a tentacle monster". The four days are Nia's private reckoning ("according to the most reasonable half of her mind"), and no earlier text fixes the day she gave him up for dead; the monster detail is what she would reasonably assume from the fall (ch039–040). Unanchored but unfalsifiable, and it is doing emotional, not chronological, work. Noted so it is a known quantity.
- Nia's jealousy is entirely interior and immediately self-mocked ("It's just a strange thing… to carry. That's all."), exactly her card's pattern — genuinely in love, refusing to acknowledge it, framing attachment as anything else.
- "in a voice with grass in it" for her doubled "Wait" — her nature-connection colouring her voice under stress; consistent with her power's description, and it is Rin she is stopping from clobbering Bog, i.e. protecting him before she has admitted he is hers to protect.

Her one spoken contribution is stopping Rin from attacking him.
- The closing beat inverts ch073–075's sound design: the bells that sang through three chapters go "absolutely silent" under Priya's crimson. Sound, not exposition, marks the power shift.
