## A. Contradictions with canon / settings
No clear (hard) contradictions. Items, in order of weight:

- **A1 (possible tension, needs ruling) - The purchase oath is missing from the purchase scene.** Per the user's ch01 ruling, now in canon: at a purchase the buyer swears an oath before a registrar under the glowing seal. In ch03 the whole transaction is "He sighed, paid the man, and hoisted her onto his shoulder." No registrar, no oath, no formality - and ch04 then opens with Bog submitting the reserve application from home and having "read the registrar's manual twice". So the registrar exists in this story's paperwork, but the oath the user just established is nowhere on the page. Options: (a) the oath is sworn off-page as part of the ch04 application process - record that interpretation and no prose change; (b) add a short oath beat to ch03 (at the merchant's stall or a registrar's booth in the market); (c) rule that black-market purchases skip the oath (which would make Bog's purchase irregular - a possible deliberate wrinkle, but it would need to be a decision, not an accident).
- **A2 (possible tension) - Nia's knee vs. collar suppression.** Canon: the null-collar "suppresses or sharply limits" powers; Nia's card: "Collared, her strength is suppressed; her resilience expresses itself as endurance, defiance, and counting." In ch03, the moment the field drops she drives a knee at Bog's face hard enough to draw blood, and the narration rules on it: "she had enough raw strength left to make a Val bleed even without any powers." chapters/ch03.md already records this as an established fact ("retained enough raw strength, even collared, to make a Val bleed"). Reconciliation available: the knee is mundane strength - a big, trained, dense body striking a soft target (the nose), with Bog catching most of it on the cheek. If the user confirms that reading, lock it on Nia's card ("even with powers suppressed, her mundane physical strength is exceptional") so later chapters stay consistent. If instead some power leaks through, that is a bigger canon decision.
- **A3 (possible tension, low-medium) - The curse that lands.** Final lines: through the aircar glass Nia "screamed a curse at him, one of the genuinely effective kind. Bog felt it land somewhere between his shoulder blades and stay there." Read literally, an effect reaches Bog despite the collar - and Nia's gifts do include a spiritual dimension (her spirits, the mate-recognition protection). Read figuratively, it is Bog's unease in folk language. The text does not disambiguate. Needs a ruling only about how later chapters should treat it: literal spiritual residue that the collar cannot fully suppress, or Bog's superstition. No prose change proposed either way.
- **A4 (style-guide tension) - Hearts on other captives.** "The curses that followed came out sweet and breathless... and a few were punctuated by hearts." The style guide and the character cards give the "♥" in speech as a signature pattern for Nia (arousal/fluster, growing affection) and for Sonya. Here unnamed captives in the row produce hearts too, which dilutes the signature - unless hearts are a general effect of Val domestication/conditioning on captive women, in which case Nia's pattern is an instance of a general phenomenon and the cards should say so. Needs ruling; prose change optional (e.g. keep hearts only on the growling one - who is Nia - if the signature reading wins).
- Naming scan: clean. The merchant's "a trader I met on one of the worlds we conquered" is a generic lowercase trader from another conquered world, not the Traders network - no violation.
- Timeline: the purchase falls inside the three-day deadline from ch02. Consistent.
