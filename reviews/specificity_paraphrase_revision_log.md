## The two rules being applied

- **Background specificity** (`04_style_guide.md`, ruled
  2026-11-20, scope broadened 2026-11-21 to cover every
  domain a scene touches — vegetation, animals, geology
  and landforms, the built environment, weather and light;
  concise form in
  `reviews/style_guide_concise_draft.md` §A/B, as amended
  2026-11-21): Name specifics, not generics — a shagbark
  hickory with shaggy, weathered bark, not "a tree". Fit the
  detail to its place, person, time, season, weather and
  situation; keep it atmosphere, not exposition.
- **Paraphrase / varied wording** (standing, 2026-11-21;
  general form in the concise draft's "Wording", erotic
  application in `reviews/erotic_description_memo.md` §7):
  Do not repeat the same word or image through a chapter
  where a fitting paraphrase or metaphor exists. Rotate
  nouns, descriptions and metaphor vehicles — e.g. black as
  night's colour, as a raven's feather; buttocks as bouncing
  balls of flesh, as a rippling futon of flesh (user's
  examples). Variety must fit the moment; it is not random
  synonym-swapping.

## Operation A — Specificity

Replace a generic, symbolic placeholder noun in narration
with a specific, seen thing, only when ALL of these hold:

- It is background / set dressing / a prop / flora / fauna /
  geology / a background extra — not a plot fact, not a
  reveal, not dialogue where the generic is natural speech.
- The chapter's place is known (from `chapters/chXXX.md`
  and the manuscript itself) and the replacement is correct
  for that place: e.g. northern Alabama / Appalachia /
  Florence — shagbark hickory, tulip poplar, oak, kudzu,
  red-tailed hawk, Carolina wren, northern cardinal (the
  ch122 fixed list); Mobile / Gulf, Vesper, the Burrow,
  a Val interior each take their own correct specifics.
  If the correct specific cannot be verified for that
  place, leave the generic and log `[flag: unverified]`
  — do not invent an exotic species or object.
- It fits time of day, the season band in `05_timeline.md`
  (arc01 late winter→spring, ch013→014 late spring,
  arc02–07 early summer→summer, arc08 summer), the scene's
  weather, and the situation. Note `00_canon.md`: the war
  has made the climate abnormal, so off-season weather is
  allowed — but a species/object that could not be there
  at all is not excused by that rule.
- It fits the person whose space / viewpoint it is (a jazz
  lover's record, a mechanic's tool), where relevant.
- The specific carries at most one visible trait in the
  ch122 manner (named thing *seen*, not just named), and
  stays a subordinate background beat — never a new
  paragraph of exposition, never a new character, plot
  thread, or named extra (Florence-session convention).

Target examples (upper-bound scan 2026-11-21, manuscript/):

## Operation B — Paraphrase

- Scan each chapter for a concrete noun / description /
  image repeated in narration to the point of monotony
  (as a guide: the same content word 3+ times in close
  succession, or the same body-part / colour / object word
  running through the chapter — the user's "お尻、お尻、
  お尻" case).
- Keep the first / strongest occurrence as written; vary
  later occurrences with a fitting paraphrase or metaphor
  that preserves the referent, scale, register and POV.
  A character's own repeated word in dialogue is voice,
  not monotony — leave it unless narration repeats it.
- **Exempt — never paraphrase away:** intentional verbatim
  motifs (the feeding litany, the 413 count, bells as a
  motif, "Не сдамся"), canon terms (consort, General
  Control System, faction and device names), proper names,
  and any repetition that is itself the joke / rhythm.

## Exclusions — do NOT touch

- **Similes and character shorthand for known people:**
  "like a boy who's just watched a fireworks show" (Bog),
  "a girl who fixed machines…" / "a girl with a ponytail"
  (Rin), "a girl signing a form she has not read" (Rin) —
  these are characterisation / voice, not background
  placeholders. The bulk of "a boy" (7) / "a girl" (24)
  hits are of this kind.
- A generic whose vagueness is the point (an unidentified
  man/woman before a reveal; a crowd figure the prose
  deliberately does not individuate).
- Dialogue and interior monologue, except where narration
  inside it repeats a background generic and Operation A
  clearly applies — log those as [interior] and be
  conservative.
- `archive/` — never rewritten. Chapter summaries,
  beat sheets, reviews and cards are not prose targets;
  only the manuscript and its part volume change.
