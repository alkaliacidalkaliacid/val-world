## B. Internal consistency and voice

- B1 (observation, keep recommended) — the grievance montage's chronology. "Four days. She had spent four days in a bunny costume, a collar and a string of Val beads, being *counted*… sat in a tent with the sound of a queue outside" packs the Mississippi tent night (ch49) into the four days, although that night predates the parting at ch54 and Bog reached that camp himself by ch53. Read strictly, the itinerary does not fit; read as what it is — an associative litany of everything she endured, re-lit by four days of mourning since the parting (her denied grief was planted at ch54, T04) — it is true to her interior accounting and to how the litany itself works in her head. Flagged so the compression is a known quantity, not a discovered error; no change proposed.
- Her litany detail is exact: the spices, the names, and *him* put "in the place where a good Christian woman would have put an Amen" — the count chapters' litany carried forward correctly, and it is the chapter's real accusation, which she does not say aloud.
- Bog's deflection is in full character: the grin, the wave, the missed-bus framing, and "With the toolkit. I put a lot of thought into the toolkit" — wounded pride about the one part of the pod decision he can defend. His one honest beat is involuntary: "Oh. You do look different." (He does not mean the overalls — the bells, the rings, what six years and four days have made audible.)
- The fist she closes anyway, knowing the collar leaves her "a pilot light" and it will hurt her more, matches the collar's established suppression and her established refusal to be sensible about him.
