## A. Contradictions with canon / settings
No hard contradictions. The chapter's main factual job - planting T11 - is done exactly per the card. Items:

- **A1 (medium-low, main point) - "His people have turned entire oceans into fuel."** This is the largest world claim in the chapter and it exists nowhere else: not in canon, not in lore, not in any other chapter (verified by search). Read literally it cannot stand - the Earth of this book still has its oceans (the monster falls into the Gulf in ch039 and walks its seafloor; the occupation's geography is coasts, corridors and a fall of the eastern seaboard, not a drained planet), and a Val program that converted whole oceans to fuel would be a defining fact of the setting, not a throwaway line. Read as what it almost certainly is - Nia's contemptuous interior hyperbole, in a paragraph whose real subject is the absurdity of Bog worrying about a water bill next to a processor that "could fill a lake" - it is in voice and harmless. Needs a ruling only so the pass treats it consistently: (a) hyperbole - do not record in canon, do not treat as fact in later reviews (the ch002 Tau Ceti treatment); or (b) if some version is intended as fact (e.g. the Val strip-mined oceans elsewhere, or process seawater at scale), say so and it will be recorded in a form that does not drain Earth. No prose change proposed under (a).
- **A2 (low, grouped with A1) - "A thing that could crush a tank."** Same family: interior hyperbole about Bog's strength. Canon's scaling is precise - an ordinary Val infantryman overpowers several humans; it is uncollared *heroes* who threaten warships (and Nia who put a tank on a satellite). A rank-and-file Val who could crush a tank in his hands would break that scaling. In context ("shivering because a woman is washing his back") it is her awed shorthand for his grip and bulk, not a feat claim. Awareness only; A1's ruling should cover the pattern (Nia's interior superlatives are rhetoric, not power-scaling data).
