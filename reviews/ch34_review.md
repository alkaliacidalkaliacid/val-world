## A. Canon / setting contradictions

None. Verified:

- Sonya's exhaustion follows her card exactly: strain symptoms are nosebleeds, headaches and dizziness after heavy cumulative use (~a hundred discs in this battle, per ch32's count, plus continuous cutting since). Here: temples throbbing, vision swimming at the edges, dried blood crusted on her lip, and her discs coming "smaller each time, and slower" until she cannot open one at all. The limit is physiological, not a new rule.
- The creature's corelessness is the tactical point and matches its card: no heart, no brain, no core to cut; severed limbs regrow almost instantly. Her portals are "useless" not because they fail but because there is nothing decisive to cut - the correct kind of counter.
- Geography matches the ch32 fix: she moves between the absorbed pod and the creature's hollow; discs take her only to "the hollow of the creature's own body, among more cables", which is where this fight happens.
- Bog's voice issues from "a gray band clamped around" a tentacle's middle - a collar of the monster's absorbed stock used as a speaker, consistent with the monster speaking through collars. It is a different collar from the pulsing-blue control collar in Bog's pod (ch33); the depot's stock makes both available.
- Her résumé details (FSB Alpha Group, the four-hundred-metre headshot) are colour consistent with her card (Alpha Group veteran, assassin); nothing contradicts them.

## B. Internal consistency and voice

- The escalation is clean cause-and-effect: cut, cut, smaller and slower discs, a fifth coil she cannot answer. Her surrender of the foot is physiological before it is anything else, which is what makes the comedy humiliating rather than arbitrary.
- Bog's "your business partner" is deliberate needling from the man she betrayed, and the magazine-cover line ("a role instead... the kind you used to see promised on the covers of old Earth magazines") is his skewed Earth-culture knowledge doing double duty: the reader gets the pulp joke, Sonya - who has never read an American SF magazine - gets none of it, and her blank *What?* is the punchline.
- Her interior self-mythology ("a woman who had never once laughed in the presence of an enemy") is placed exactly where the first squeak can destroy it.

## C. Craft

- "Entertaining?" as the closing line hands the arc's thesis to Bog in one word: ch33's "different way" made audible.
