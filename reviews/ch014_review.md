## A. Canon / setting contradictions

No hard contradictions. Verified in detail:

- The Baltimore line is card-exact: "She had held a Baltimore corridor for nine days" matches `characters/nia_okonkwo.md` (held the Baltimore evacuation corridor, nine days, 30,000 escaped).
- Her interior roll call - "Amara. Chidi. Daniel. Hold the line." - is the card's order, used here as a battle mantra against a humiliatingly small enemy, which is the joke and the pathos at once.
- The diet logic is consistent with her card (sole nourishment = Bog's protein) and with the ch013 clarification: Bog himself names the missing pieces - "They also say drink water and eat fiber. Which, ah. Isn't really in the cards for us." The constipation is the protein diet's cost, and ch015 ("The Long Push") continues the same episode.
- The rendezvous thread is continuous: ch008's plan to deal with a supervillain-backed outfit -> ch013's 7:30 PM rumor -> this chapter's rendezvous day, the transponder, and the postponement. The contact's uncertain identity ("The supervillain, or villainess, nobody had been sure which") is left open in the text and pays off later; no conflict with the Broker sequence in ch018-021.
- A1 (low; records gap) - "The bells on her ankles and everywhere else shivered." Ankle bells are not in the piercing list on Nia's card (nipples, labia, clitoris; chest and hips per ch004), but they recur in ch054 ("the bells at her ankles gave a single, treacherous chime"), so the prose is self-consistent across parts and the card is what is incomplete. **Ruled and applied (user, 2026-11-17):** resolved by deletion instead - "The bells on her ankles and everywhere else shivered" became "The bells shivered" (manuscript + part02). The parallel instance in ch054 ("the bells at her ankles gave a single, treacherous chime") was fixed the same day at the user's direction: "at her ankles" deleted there too (ch054 manuscript + part05). No ankle bells remain anywhere in the manuscript, so the card needs no addition.
- A2 (low; first appearance) - The household robot rolls in with a tray, beeps, and speaks ("Which family?"), then relays Bog's message. In ch001-013 the helper robots exist only as a mention (ch007: three owned, one in regular use, per the ch007 ruling on Bog's card) and never act or speak. Nothing in canon forbids a speaking helper unit, and its literal-minded question fits the book's machine humor, but this is quietly the robots' debut as characters. No text change proposed; optionally record on Bog's card that the regular-use unit speaks and can relay messages.

## B. Internal consistency and voice

- **B1 (the chapter's one real question) - Bog calls Nia "pickle".** "Hold still, pickle." Every earlier use of the word runs the other way: it is Nia's insult for Bog (ch004 "you overgrown pickle", ch005 "perverted pickle", ch006-012 passim), and the ch011 ruling treated "pickle" as hers (narration names him Bog). Two readings: (a) a slip; (b) a deliberate throwback - in ch004, hearing it for the first time, "Bog repeated it, and filed it away", and this chapter is the first time he spends what he filed: her own insult returned as a teasing endearment while he tends her, which she protests ("Don't you dare call me that") precisely because it is hers. Reading (b) is strongly supported by the ch004 setup. **Ruled and applied (user, 2026-11-17):** the user judged Bog's "pickle" most likely a Claude hallucination and replaced it with a newly coined endearment: "Hold still, squeeze candy." (manuscript + part02). "Pickle" stays one-directional - Nia's insult for Bog - and "squeeze candy" is recorded as Bog's endearment for Nia on his character card (Voice).
- Bog's competence here is in character: the warm towel, the clockwise circles from "a stray page of a prenatal pamphlet", the water-and-fiber aside - his patchwork Earth knowledge applied with total sincerity, and for once exactly what is needed. His delight ("Best thing I ever did") at causing the problem he is now tenderly solving is the couple in one beat.
- Her surrender is physical before it is verbal: the hand that "closed around his wrist, not to stop him, only to hold on" continues the holding motif from ch012 / ch013, and her one request is for secrecy ("Don't tell anyone that you saw this") - pride, not gratitude, is what she can afford.

## C. Craft

- The chapter's event is a decision, not the ailment: Bog looks at the blinking transponder, looks at her, and invents "Family emergency" on the spot - then has to define "family" to his own robot and lands on "The one that's lying on my rug." After ch012's proposal (still unanswered) and ch013's "Not yet", this is his framing moving again, in action rather than words, at real cost to his plan (the summary's Facts note the postponement's cost).
- The comedy is the book's signature ratio: a woman who held a corridor for nine days, defeated by her own bowels; a warlord-grade problem solved with a towel and patience. The slapstick never undercuts her dignity for long - the narration keeps her hatred "pure" and her curses fluent.
- Structure: single scene, single room, two interruptions (the gurgle, the transponder) that divide it into tenderness / choice / surrender. Economical and complete.
