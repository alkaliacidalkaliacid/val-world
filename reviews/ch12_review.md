## C. Craft

- Structure is four clean movements: the proposal and her conditions; the "seeds" negotiation (erotic slapstick — "Write that on your little form" / "I'll frame it"); the spirits' criterion, the chapter's emotional core; and the seven-days confrontation ending on the hook — Bog, voluble for a week, with nothing to say. His silence lands because the chapter has made him talk constantly.
- The criterion scene works because Daniel is rendered in concrete acts (bad coffee at 3 a.m., badly braided hair, dishes after double shifts) rather than virtues; Bog's stillness and faltering rhythm register the hit before his line does. This is effectively the thesis scene for Bog's whole arc: what he would have to become.
- Her interior rationalization peaks here — "It's not fondness. It's agriculture." — and the counter-beat ("Then why did my mouth water when he said 'seeds'?") is the chapter in miniature: every denial immediately audited by her own body.
- The ♥-saturated dialogue is at its densest in the book so far, which is calibrated to her state (per the style guide the ♥s surface against her will when she is aroused or flustered); the third scene thins them out as the talk turns serious, which keeps the device from going flat. No trim proposed, but this is the chapter where a reader's tolerance for the device is tested most.
- Title: "Seven Days" names the chapter's second half and its real subject — not the proposal but what a week of unbroken closeness does to her defenses. The roll-call passage ("He's filling every silence until I can't hear the roll call") makes the siege metaphor explicit without leaving her interior voice.
