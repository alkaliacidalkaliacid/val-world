# THE ASHEN DOMINION
## A Post-Apocalyptic Superhero Universe
 
---
 
## 1. The World After the Fall
 
In the year **2041**, Earth was invaded by an extraterrestrial military civilization known as the **Val Dominion**. Their arrival was sudden and overwhelming: vast carrier-ships appeared over major population centers, orbital weapons disabled communications and power grids, and ground forces seized strategic infrastructure before the world's governments could coordinate a response.
 
Humanity's last organized defense was the **Earth Accord**, an alliance of national militaries and independent superheroes. The United States led the coalition, coordinating its command structure, logistics, and remaining strategic forces. Other nations contributed troops, intelligence networks, weapons, and enhanced individuals of their own.
 
For a brief period, the Accord held. Superheroes destroyed landing craft, rescued millions, and inflicted losses the invaders had not anticipated. But the Val possessed reserves of ships, troops, and weapons that humanity could not match. Their commanders adapted. Their occupation forces learned to isolate superheroes from civilians, overwhelm them with coordinated attacks, and use orbital fire to erase entire defensive positions.
 
The decisive defeat came at the **Battle of the Meridian Line**. The Accord's command network collapsed, its remaining air forces were destroyed, and the last major superhero strike team disappeared beneath a curtain of alien bombardment.
 
The war lasted eleven months. The occupation has lasted six years.
 
### What remains
 
- **Cities:** Many are shattered shells. Some have been rebuilt as Val garrisons, labor hubs, or administrative centers.
- **Governments:** National governments have either dissolved, fled into exile, or survive as tightly controlled local administrations.
- **Infrastructure:** Electricity, medicine, transport, and communications are scarce outside occupation zones. The invaders maintain their own networks but rarely repair systems they do not understand.
- **The population:** Most survivors live in ration districts, agricultural compounds, underground settlements, or scattered communities beyond regular patrol routes.
- **The wilderness:** Abandoned suburbs and industrial belts have become dangerous corridors of feral machines, desperate scavengers, and unpatrolled territory.
Humanity is not extinct. It is occupied, fragmented, and treated as a resource.

## 2. The Val Dominion
 
The Val are a humanoid, all-male species. Adult males commonly stand between **7 feet 2 inches and 8 feet 6 inches** tall, with dense musculature, broad shoulders, heavy bone structure, and green skin ranging from deep olive to bright mineral green. Their faces are blunt and imposing, with pronounced brows, wide mouths, and sharp teeth.
 
They are physically formidable. Even an ordinary Val infantryman can overpower several unenhanced humans. Their elite soldiers are trained from youth in close-quarters combat, weapons handling, and formation tactics. Even with a tenfold numerical advantage, it would be difficult for human military forces to defeat Val’s army. On the other hand, superheroes—provided they have not been neutralized by means such as the collars mentioned later—could potentially even shoot down Val’s space warships. Val views humanity’s nuclear weapons, as well as armaments devised by super-genius-level superheroes, as "bold concepts" and finds them reasonably interesting.
 
They are also crude, arrogant, and frequently vulgar. Val military culture prizes dominance, public humiliation of enemies, and loud displays of strength. Their soldiers use coarse humor and insults as social currency. Their commanders are not uniformly stupid, however: some are disciplined strategists who understand logistics and military organization even when they do not understand the underlying science of their equipment.
 
### The reproductive crisis
 
The Val have no females and do not reproduce biologically. For generations, their population has been sustained by **artificial gestation chambers**, commonly called *cradles*. These systems combine genetic archives, synthetic nutrient environments, and automated developmental controls to grow new Val.
 
The cradles are now failing.
 
An unexplained contamination—known among Val technicians as **the Quiet Blight**—has spread through the gestation network. Embryos fail at different stages, viable genetic lines are becoming scarce, and entire cradle-vaults have gone dormant. The Dominion has been unable to identify the contaminant or reverse its effects.
 
The crisis is slow rather than immediately catastrophic. The Val still possess enormous armies and occupied territories, but their population is aging without a reliable means of replacement. Their leaders fear a long, inevitable extinction. However, many of their lower-ranking members do not take this very seriously, preferring instead to enjoy their day-to-day acts of brutality.
 
### The values of humanity for Val
 
A source of labor, and a form of entertainment. And the Val elites were astonished to discover that humans reproduce without artificial gestation systems. Human pregnancy, birth, and family formation are biological processes that the Val regard with a mixture of disbelief, envy, and desperate curiosity.
 
the Val elites have consequently classified humanity as a **reproductive resource**. For the lower-ranking members of Val who roam the field, following this policy is akin to a new form of play. However, they have been strictly warned by the elites against taking the lives of the "reproducible individuals" (i.e., females) of humanity. they generally abide that instruction.
 
### The powerful but bit idiotic empire
 
The Val have formidable weapons, armored carriers, orbital platforms, and large occupation forces. Their individual soldiers are dangerous, and their armies can devastate human settlements.
 
Yet their technological culture has a weakness: **they operate systems they no longer understand**. Their artificial cradles and power-suppression collars are inherited technologies maintained through ritualized procedures, inherited manuals, and a narrow caste of technicians. Few Val can explain the principles behind them.
 
The Dominion's military might is real. Its scientific confidence is largely theater. And the lower-ranking members of Val don’t seem to care much about that.

## 3. The Val Tools
 
Every captured superhero is fitted with a **null-collar**, a heavy band of alien alloy locked around the neck. The collars interfere with the biological, neurological, or metaphysical processes that allow superhuman abilities to function.
 
The Val call them *taming rings*. The prisoners call them *dead weight*.
 
### What the collars do
 
- Suppress or sharply limit superhuman abilities.
- Monitor basic vital signs and broadcast a wearer's location.
- Deliver painful incapacitating pulses when triggered by authorized control units.
- Resist ordinary tools and conventional weapons.
- Make escape difficult without making it physically impossible.
### Piercing-rings and bells
 
Some captives are also fitted with **piercing-rings and bells**, ornamental devices worn at designated body piercings. Their small bells produce a clear, unusually beautiful tone, while the rings themselves can vibrate in carefully controlled patterns.
 
The Val present them as decorative accessories, but they serve several practical purposes:
 
- Their vibrations provide persistent sensory stimulation, distracting the wearer and making sustained concentration more difficult.
- Their placement and construction can restrict certain movements, especially when combined with a collar or other restraints.
- Their bells make the wearer easier to locate by sound, even in crowded compounds or poorly lit corridors.
- The devices can be tuned or remotely activated by authorized handlers.
The sound is deliberately pleasant to Val ears. To many human captives, the contrast between the lovely tone and the coercive purpose makes the bells especially unsettling. Their use is not universal; some Val consider them fashionable, while others regard them as unnecessary ornamentation.
 
### Intestinal bead-chain
 
A more invasive device used in some Val compounds is a **string of anal beads**, each bead approximately the size of a human fist. The Val describe the apparatus as a sanitary and disciplinary appliance, though its use is deeply intrusive and is often imposed without meaningful consent.
 
The beads contain multiple mechanical and sensory systems. Depending on the model, they can rotate, vibrate, undulate, alter their surface texture, expand and contract under control, change temperature, and generate electrical currents. Their operation can be remotely controlled or set to automated cycles.
 
The Val claim that the device also processes waste matter within the intestines, breaking it down and converting it into a clean, transparent, fragrant gel that can be collected through the apparatus. The Dominion treats this function as proof of its technological sophistication, although its technicians cannot fully explain the underlying biological and chemical processes.
 
Some models can be fitted with a **faux tail** made from the wearer's own shorn hair. Among the Val, this is regarded as a fashionable finishing touch and a conspicuous sign that a captive has been fully incorporated into an owner's household display. Humans generally regard the practice as degrading, though attitudes vary among collaborators and those who have adopted Val customs.
 
### Leashes
 
Val leashes are made by braiding hair cut from the person wearing the collar. They have no specialized mechanical or technological functions; they are ordinary physical leads whose significance is primarily symbolic and practical.
 
Leashes made from superhero hair are especially sought after. Superhuman hair is often exceptionally durable, making these braids unusually strong. Collectors and status-conscious Val regard such leashes as prestigious trophies.
 
### Brands
 
A **brand** marks an individual as the permanent property of a specific Val. In Val custom, it functions as a binding contract comparable to marriage: it establishes an enduring household bond, recognized obligations, and exclusive ownership under Dominion law.
 
A brand is therefore not applied casually. Among the Val, receiving one is considered a profound honor, a public declaration that the recipient has been selected for permanent inclusion in an owner's household. Some humans who have embraced Val customs share this interpretation and actively seek such bonds.

## 5. The Resistance
 
Human resistance is fragmented. It has no unified command, no secure territory, and no guarantee that one cell knows the others exist. Most groups concentrate on immediate survival, local defense, or disrupting the occupation in small ways.
 
### The Old Guards
 
A loose alliance of former soldiers, local defense groups, escaped prisoners, and civilian fighters. Ash Guard units conduct ambushes, sabotage supply lines, steal ration shipments, and protect settlements from raiders and occupation patrols.
 
Some units include former crime-fighters and vigilante-style heroes who never possessed superpowers. They rely on training, investigative skills, improvised equipment, disguises, and intimate knowledge of their cities. Though individually far less powerful than the Bound, these masked survivors are a persistent nuisance to the Val: they know the streets, recognize corrupt intermediaries, and repeatedly disrupt patrols that assume all human resistance has been crushed.
 
The Old Gurads are not always noble or unified. Some commanders favor careful defense; others advocate increasingly aggressive reprisals. Their greatest strength is local knowledge, and their greatest weakness is the difficulty of coordinating beyond their own territory.
 
### The Free Clinics
 
A network of doctors, nurses, pharmacists, medical volunteers, and improvised treatment rooms operating in basements, abandoned hospitals, and hidden settlement spaces. The clinics treat injuries, deliver babies, preserve scarce medicines, and hide fugitives when they can.
 
Several doctors within the network are also attempting to analyze the null-collars. Their work has not yet produced a reliable way to disable or remove one. They have managed to document recurring symptoms, compare collar behavior, and study fragments recovered after malfunctions, but the alien devices remain poorly understood. The risk is considerable: an incorrect intervention could permanently injure or kill the wearer, while discovery by the Val could lead to the destruction of an entire clinic.
 
The Free Clinics insist that research must never come at the expense of a captive's life. This principle sometimes puts them at odds with impatient resistance fighters who see the collars as the key to liberating the Bound.
 
## 6. Third-Party Factions
 
The Val occupation has created a power vacuum in which opportunists, zealots, criminals, and former supervillains have carved out their own territories. These groups are neither a unified resistance nor reliable allies of the Dominion. Their loyalties are usually local, transactional, or ideological—and often change when the balance of power shifts.
 
### Raiders
 
They are operating along abandoned highways and freight corridors. Its members strip vehicles, attack isolated settlements, and sell captured supplies to whoever can pay. The Cinder Kings avoid direct battles with Val patrols but sometimes provide guides, prisoners, or intelligence in exchange for weapons and protection.
 
### Cults
 
Cults interprets the invasion as a divine judgment and the Val as the instruments of a new age. Its preachers teach that surrendering to Dominion rule is a sacred duty. Some chapters collaborate openly with occupation officials, identifying hidden settlements and denouncing Ash Guard members. Other chapters are less organized and focus on ritual, communal living, and apocalyptic prophecy.
 
### Traders
 
Smugglers, black-market brokers, and former corporate fixers. It trades in medicine, forged identity papers, ammunition, alien components, and information. The Gilded Hand will work with the Ash Guard one week and sell a route map to a Val quartermaster the next. Its members insist that neutrality is a survival strategy; most communities regard them as dangerous but sometimes indispensable.
 
### 
 
A loose collection of former supervillains who remained hidden throughout the war. They survived by abandoning public identities, changing territories, and allowing the Val to believe they had died during the invasion. They are not a formal organization, and their members distrust one another almost as much as they distrust the heroes.
 
Known figures include:
 
- **Mister Ruin:** A demolition expert with the ability to weaken the structural integrity of materials. He once terrorized major cities for profit and now sells sabotage services to the highest bidder.
- **Madame Morrow:** A former information broker who can extract fragments of memory through physical contact. She maintains a private archive of secrets gathered before and after the invasion.
- **Gravemind:** A telepath who can influence crowds but cannot reliably control individuals with strong mental discipline. He has built a small, fearful following in the ruins of an old industrial district.
- **The Jackal:** A non-powered master thief and escape artist who specialized in robbing other criminals. He has survived the occupation by trading stolen alien equipment and changing identities.
- **Saint Voltage:** A disgraced energy manipulator who once claimed to be a hero before turning to extortion. He has been seen supplying power cells to Val officers in return for protection.
The Uncrowned present a complicated problem for the resistance. Some possess skills or powers that could help undermine the occupation; others are predators who see the collapse of civilization as an unprecedented business opportunity. A few may genuinely want the Val driven from Earth, but none should be mistaken for dependable allies.
