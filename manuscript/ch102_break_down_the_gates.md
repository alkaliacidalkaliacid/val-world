## Chapter 102: Break Down the Gates

"Time to beg breeding," Bog said.

Priya shuddered on his lap. Her eyes squeezed shut. For a moment the whole enterprise hung there — the good wife, the doctor, the mother, at the end of her excuses —

— and then she grinned.

It was a wicked grin, a brazen grin, nothing surrendered about it at all. She rose off his lap and stood before him tall on her own two feet, and she spread her legs wide, and with her own fingers she parted the thicket of hair and opened the entrance to her gate of life for him to see, glistening, already ruined. Her other hand took up her huge left breast and kneaded it, offering and display in one gesture. Her tongue flicked out, wet, across her lips. She beckoned.

"See her, great one♥," she said, in a voice gone low and rolling and pleased with itself. "See how the *dasi* stands before her *swami*—"

"Incarnation," said the Mediatoress, from the same throat, hastily. "This pantomime will cease. You will not degrade the—"

"—and the goddess in her cries *cease, cease*♥," Priya sang over her, weaving it in without a seam, "as goddesses cry in all the old songs, which in the tongue of gods means *come closer*. Aao♥. She has seen the lord of the dance and she knows him. You are Shiva come again, green and terrible♥, and the servant girl begs what the goddess is too proud to beg: put your *linga* in her *yoni*, great one. Fill the gate of life you have been knocking at."

Bog, who had been watching with his head slightly tilted, like a man at a festival he had only ever read about, stood up.

He stood up *as* something. Chest out. Arms spread wide in welcome to an invisible crowd. Chin down, head tipped to one side, attempting a smoulder that his face had not been issued the equipment for. He held it. Somewhere in the performance was a famous man, fondly and badly remembered, filtered through whatever salvaged Earth entertainment had reached a Val barracks — Salman Khan by way of a low-ranking soldier's total commitment.

"What—" said the Mediatoress. "What is he *doing*—"

"—and the goddess asks what he is doing♥," Priya breathed, delighted, not missing a beat, "for the gods are strange when they come courting, and stranger when they dance—"

Bog crossed the space between them with the walk that went with the pose, which was also wrong, and also entirely sincere. His arm went around her waist. He tilted her back, far back, the way the pose demanded, and kissed her down there in the dip of it, deep and unhurried, while she laughed once against his mouth — a real laugh, startled out of her — and then moaned instead, because his free hand had found the bud again and the verse came back to her in pieces.

"The servant girl♥," she gasped, arms winding around his neck, "already destined to submit to her great ruler — she defies him still, only— only to gain his attention♥. Please. Please, *swami*— use your blessed battering ram♥ — break down the gates of the city that awaits its fall — defeat her, destroy her, destroy and destroy—"

Bog hoisted her.

Up she went, light as laundry to him, legs locking around his waist in the air — and he took her there, suspended, one thrust up into the opened gate as she dropped onto him, and Priya's verse broke apart into cries with ♥s in them like sparks. Both his hands gripped her thick, brown, married backside, firm and full, twisting her on him, lifting her and letting her fall, thrusting upward to meet every fall, a rhythm like the music her performance had been reaching for. The Mediatoress interjected twice more — a rebuttal, a protest, something about the dignity of the— — and both times the sounds were simply carried off by the rhythm, woven in, percussion in a song that no longer required her meaning.

"Hey!"

Above them, Rin hung scarlet to the ears, gasping, her legs flailing in the red light, one finger pointing down at the scene like a witness identifying a suspect.

"Hey! I've seen this before!" Rin shouted, scandalised and thrilled in equal measure, pointing harder. "My mom used to watch stuff like this!"
