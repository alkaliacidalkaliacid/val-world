## Chapter 25: Portals


"What in Val's name?" Bog bellowed, scrambling backward on all fours.

"She opens portals! Whatever goes into one comes out of another!" Nia shouted.

Sonya's pale eyes flickered. Her gaze seemed to say, *I knew it. Even with that collar on, you're a seasoned hero.* The discs that had swallowed the bells' chimes winked out, and Nia's body rang again, bright and clear, through the vast warehouse.

Another disc flared into being beside Bog's ear and vanished. Another opened beneath his boots, and he jumped before he'd finished thinking about it. A fourth appeared at the level of his belly and snapped shut as he twisted away, so close that the air it displaced tugged at his uniform. He kept stumbling backward, hugging the toolkit to his chest, cursing in three dialects.

"Portals that move things from one place to another," he panted. "How scary can that be? You'd think the worst you could do is, I don't know, rearrange somebody's furniture…"

A crate toppled from the stack beside him, struck by his flailing elbow. It slid across the floor toward a disc that had just opened there. The front half of the crate sank into the glowing circle, and the disc snapped shut.

The crate fell in two pieces. The cut was so clean it looked polished, and a puff of fine powder trickled from the sliced contents.

Bog stared at it. "Okay," he said weakly. "I take it back."

*Not teleportation,* Nia thought, watching Sonya's hands. *Doorways. And she can close them whenever she likes, no matter what is halfway through.* She had fought storms and walking furnaces, but never anyone whose weapon was an edge of nothing that could be drawn through anything.

"Bog! Move left, then drop!" she shouted.

He obeyed without an argument, which surprised both of them. A pair of discs shot open where his head and chest had been, and a second later two matching exits flashed out of the air behind him. Sonya had sent her own blow through the first pair and was redirecting it. Whatever she had hurled at him gouged a trench in the floor where he'd been lying. He rolled aside, wheezing.

"She's aiming at you," Nia called. "I'm collared. She knows I can't do anything. You're the one with the muscle!"

"I have muscle! I have *tremendous* muscle! I don't have any *plan*!"

"Then charge her! Get close! The portals need space around her to open. She can't fill a room with them without straining!"

*Two or three at a time,* Nia added to herself. She'd been counting since the first strike, and the number never climbed beyond four. Sonya's temple was glistening, and she kept her left foot planted when she shifted her weight, as if she were bracing against a headache. *Size, count, and stamina. The more she opens, the more she pays.* She hopes that's the case.

Bog charged. For a Val with his weight and size it was an alarming sight, and a lesser human might have fled the warehouse in terror. Sonya stepped aside without hurry. A disc yawned in the floor ahead of him, and he hurdled it at the last instant, trailing curses. A second blossomed at the height of his throat. He ducked, and the toolkit slipped from his grip.

It skidded across the floor and stopped at Nia's feet.

Both of them looked at it.

"Oh, wonderful," Bog groaned.

Sonya's eyes dropped to the toolkit, then lifted to Nia's collar. For the first time she looked amused. "Collar-calibration," she said softly. "You are the reason he came."

"He came for his own reasons," Nia said.

She snatched up the case and hugged it to her stomach. The bells shivered, and she felt the cold weight of the collar settle against her throat. She wished, for one insane second, for the strength to break the woman in half. A disc opened just beyond her left foot, a warning, not a strike, and she stood very still.

"Put it on the ground," Sonya said. "Both of you. Then we will talk."

"I'd love to," Bog said from somewhere behind a tower of crates. His voice was brittle with false cheer. "But somehow I doubt you meant 'talk.'"
