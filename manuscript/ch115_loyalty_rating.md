## Chapter 115: Loyalty Rating

Bog turned the wristband on his arm this way and that, admiring it the way a man admires a new watch, and then — because it was there, and because he was Bog — he pressed on the next question that wandered into his head.

"How do I display a 'loyalty rating'?"

"You simply issue a command," said the monster.

"Display," said Bog.

Over the collar of each of the three women, light bloomed and steadied: characters, Val characters, a short burning line of the Dominion's own script, hanging in the air above Nia's throat — and above Rin's — and above Priya's.

Bog grinned.

"What is *that*?!" Nia shot to her feet. "Get rid of it! No — wait —" Her eyes had narrowed; outrage was doing arithmetic with curiosity, and curiosity was winning. "Show me what nonsense it's displaying. But in a language from Earth!"

"Earth language," Bog said, obliging.

The holograms flickered. They struggled — characters breaking, reforming, stuttering through shapes that belonged to no alphabet any of them owned — and then, one after another, they settled.

Percentages.

Over Nia's collar: **93%**.

Rin read her own at a crane, then Nia's, then her own again, in the manner of a girl checking a scoreboard she had not known she was on. **18%**. Her eyebrows climbed. She did the subtraction with her whole face and arrived, visibly, at outrage of two entirely different kinds at once.

Priya's burned lower still, as if even the light were being polite about it. **12%**. She tilted her head back to catch it, reading her own loyalty upside down over her own throat, and said nothing at all.

And Nia stood there with ninety-three percent over her collar — the highest in the room, and not by a little — fists clenched, bells silent, wearing the expression of a woman who has just been handed a certificate for something she intends to deny in writing.

"Ninety-three," she said at last, to nobody, very quietly. "Ninety. Three."
