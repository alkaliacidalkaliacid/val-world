## Chapter 108: It's Not What It Looks Like

The kiss ended.

Nia's hand came around and struck Bog across the head — a flat, ringing crack of a blow, delivered with everything her arm had in it.

"Ouch," said Bog, with feeling.

In the green light, Rin and Priya stared. Both of them had seen what Val soldiers were. Rin had watched Bog fight a powered suit with his hands and come out of it grinning through a broken nose; Priya had seen the green folk at war and knew what their bodies shrugged off. And this woman, collared and powerless and hanging in the man's arms, had just hit him hard enough to make him say *ouch* — and mean it.

Nia cleared her throat with enormous dignity.

"Put me down."

Bog put her down. Carefully, the way you set down something valuable and breakable, which — Rin noted, in the privacy of her own head — was not how anyone else in this cavern got handled.

Nia straightened her overalls. She drew breath, arranged her face into its most forbidding configuration, and began, to the room at large:

"It's not what it looks like—"

She stopped.

She had taken in Rin. Rin, naked, glowing green at the edges, holding the light up like a lantern girl — and wearing, snug and unmistakable at her throat, a collar. A Val collar. The same make as—

Her eyes moved. Priya. Priya on Bog's shoulder, Priya blushing already under the weight of being looked at — and at Priya's throat, closing the inventory, a collar of her own.

The silence had a shape.

"Anyway," said Bog, into it, with a hint of awkwardness rattling around in his tone like a stone in a can, "let's get out of here. No telling how much longer Rin's light will last."

From his shoulder, scarlet to the ears, Priya found her voice. "P-please — put me down too. I can walk—"

"No," said Bog, pleasantly, and settled her more firmly where she was, and that was the end of that discussion.

Nia's head came around slowly. The hair on the back of her neck was standing on end. She advanced on him, bells atinkle with every step, and jabbed a black-nailed thumb hard against his green chest.

"You…"

She hissed it. She hissed it beautifully. It would have been a moment of genuine menace if the bells had not chosen that exact instant to jingle, bright and musical and completely fatal to the effect. Her face went from red to a deeper, more committed red. Her eyes squeezed shut. Her brows twitched, twice, like something in her was shorting out.

She spun on her heel.

"Let's go," she declared, to the darkness, to the mountain, to the whole assembled situation. "Hurry up."

She said it as if she had been the leader of this expedition all along — as if the leadership were a matter of long record, undisputed and unremarkable. Everything else, her posture announced, would be settled when they reached the surface. There would be a settling. Oh, there would be a settling.

Nobody said a word.

No one dared to challenge the battle-hardened Black Huntress.
