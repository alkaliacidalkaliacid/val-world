## Chapter 11: The Couch


He then requested her to straddle him while he sat on the couch, align herself, and lower her hips to impale herself upon him.

"Well, if you're not confident, that's fine too," Bog remarked.

"Don't be scared—I'll hold your hands and kiss you while you give it a....try," the overgrown cactus added.

She gritted her teeth, ready to fire back with a sharp retort.

An irritating inner monologue bubbled up from the depths of her mind.

*Not confident.*

*Not confident! He's seen me crouched on a tile floor and recited the liturgy without a stumble. He's graded my technique. He's called me a sommelière. And now he's offering a handicap.*

*Four hundred and twelve, you overgrown cactus. Four hundred and twelve, and I never once asked for a gentle introduction.*

*I twisted a Val anti-ship cannon into a napkin ring. I kicked a plasma missile back into the sky and watched it take a carrier's wing off. The spirits themselves signed off on me. Do you have any idea what I've lifted, Bog? Do you know what I've carried, and what I've shrugged off, and what I've walked away from smiling?*

*This is a couch.*

*A couch, a man, and a little geometry. I can do geometry. Align, lower, breathe. It's a stairwell. It's a tight window and a long fall, and I've done both of those with a child under each arm.*

*...All right, he is above the species average. Considerably above. I'm not blind, and I'm not stupid, and I have eyes in my head. But a good soldier assesses the terrain, and a good soldier doesn't flinch, and a good soldier does not, under any circumstances, let the opponent see her swallow.*

*And what's this? "Don't be scared." He'll hold my hands. He'll kiss me while I try.*

*While I* try.

*Look at that face, so sincere it hurts. He thinks he's coaching a nervous girl through her first dive off the board. He's reassuring me, and I'm the one who has done this four hundred times.*

*It's the kindness I can't stand. The cruelty I could take. I've taken it. I could take it in my sleep. This, with the soft voice and the thumb stroking my knuckles, goes straight through the armor and finds the one soft place I swore I'd sealed up.*

*Daniel held my hands like that. In the delivery room, with Amara, before the monitor started beeping.*

*No. Get out of my head.*

*Straddle. Align. Lower.*

*I'm not afraid. I'm not afraid. I am the Black Huntress.*

She set her jaw, the scar going white, her mouth already shaping the first word of a very sharp reply.

The bells chimed once, clear and trembling, against the rise and fall of her breath.

*Hold the line.*

---

*Ten fingers. His thick and green, mine long and dark, laced together like the teeth of two gears. He's holding on as if I might float away.*

*Don't look at them. Look at his face, the closed eyes and the blunt brow creased with concentration. He's trying so hard to be gentle that it makes me want to hit him.*

*Pecks. A bite on the lip, careful, almost shy. Another, at the corner of my mouth. He kisses me like a man tasting something he can't believe he's allowed to have.*

*All right. Align. You've done this four hundred and twelve times. You can do it once more with your eyes shut.*

*Down. Slowly. Make it look like contempt.*

*Don't breathe in yet.*

*It's not fear. It's weight, that's all. It's the sheer, stupid size of him, and the body has a natural objection to being asked for more than it was built to give, and I'm giving it anyway, because I decided to, because no one else did, because I'm lowering myself of my own free will and calling it a mission.*

*Look at me, Bog. Look. I am the one doing this. I am the one setting the pace. You want to be held? Hold on, then.*

*Tight. Damn it, tight. My hands are crushing his, and he doesn't even wince. He just squeezes back, warm and steady, and says my name against my mouth in that low, helpless voice, like a prayer he's not sure anyone is answering.*

*Nia.*

*He never uses the other name when his eyes are closed.*

*Don't let that matter. It doesn't matter. It's a trick of the light and the bells and the heat.*

*Breathe out. Slowly. The jaw stays locked. The scar stays white.*

*I am not afraid of you.*

*I am not afraid. I'm angry, and I'm tired, and I'm very, very far from home. And somewhere in the middle of all that, there's a small, stubborn part of me that's still counting.*

*Amara. Chidi. Daniel.*

*Daniel, forgive me. Daniel, I'm still here.*

*Hold the line.*

*Hold it.*

The bells rang once, sharp and clear, and then the room was quiet except for breathing.

---

*Fine. It's fine. It's fine. It's totally—*

*What was that sound?*

*That wasn't me. That was a noise from some other woman in some other kitchen, and it had a heart at the end of it, a little pink, stupid heart, like the ones Amara draws in the margins of her notebooks.*

*I didn't choose that. My throat chose it. My mouth said "it's fine" and the rest of me filed a very different report, and now the whole room has heard both.*

*And Bog heard. Of course he did. Look at him: eyes cracked open, brow raised, wearing that lazy, innocent, bottomless curiosity.*

*"Snagged?"*

*No. Not snagged. I'm not snagged. I'm a professional, I've done this four hundred and twelve times, and I'm not "snagged" by a man who reads advice columns.*

*Say something. Say it now. A sharp one, the sharpest. "Pickle." "Cactus." "You great green idiot, don't talk."*

*...Why are my hands still holding his?*

*I should let go. I should drive my knee into his ribs, collar or no collar, and hear what that does to the laid-back voice. I should do any one of ten things. I've got the training. I've got the rage.*

*Instead I'm gripping like it's the last rope off a ship.*

*It's just physiology. The body is a machine, and machines respond to inputs, and nobody ever blames a machine for the noise it makes when it's pushed. The Clinics would say so. Friction, pressure, heat. A reflex, nothing more.*

*It's not me.*

*It's not me, Daniel. You know it's not me.*

*It was never me.*

*Don't let him see your face. Keep your head down. Breathe through your nose. Count.*

*Four hundred and thirteen.*

*Amara. Chidi. Daniel.*

*Tomatoes. Scotch bonnet. Thyme.*

*Hold the line.*

*Hold—*

*Nmmh.*

*Damn it.*

---

*Oh.*

*Oh, no.*

*The ceiling. Why am I looking at the ceiling? There's a water stain in the corner shaped like Maryland, and I've been staring at it for five seconds without knowing why my neck is bent back and my mouth is hanging open and my tongue is out like a dog's in summer.*

*That wasn't a decision. He took hold of me and pulled, and every plan I had went out of my head at once, the way a room goes dark when someone throws the breaker. Align, lower, breathe. Gone. The drill. Gone. The sharp reply I was loading. Gone.*

*There's only the fullness. It reaches places I didn't know I had. And the bells, ringing and ringing on their own, because I'm trembling too hard to hold still.*

*"See? We managed it."*

*We.*

*His voice is so pleased. So proud of me. And his fingers are lacing back through mine, warm and patient, as if nothing in the world could be more natural than holding hands at a moment like this.*

*Breathe. Come back. You're on a couch. You're in a house in the Wasteland. You are the woman who held Baltimore.*

*Say something. Say "pickle." Say "cactus." Say "I hate you."*

*...I can't find the words.*

*That's the part that scares me. Four hundred and twelve times, I could go somewhere else in my head, up on the ceiling, watching the cracks, counting. Now I'm not on the ceiling. I'm here. I'm right here, all of me, in a body that is answering for itself while the one who gives the orders is out looking for her voice.*

*Daniel. Daniel, I'm not...*

*Amara. Chidi.*

*Amara. Chi...*

*Hold the line.*

*Hold the...*

*Hold on.*

The bells rang again, high and unsteady, and she tightened her fingers around his, because they were the only thing in the room that wasn't moving.

---

*Step off.*

*He's giving me the choice. Not an order, not a test, not a trap with a ribbon on it. The door is open and he's holding my hands so that I can walk through it.*

*Four hundred and twelve men, and not one of them ever said that.*

*Don't take it as a gift. It isn't a gift. It's a price tag in a prettier font. He's a Val. The collar is still on my neck. If I climb off this couch, I climb off into a kitchen that belongs to him, a house that belongs to him, a deal that I agreed to with one very long sound.*

*So it isn't a choice. It can't be. A choice needs a floor under it, and I don't have a floor.*

*...Then why does it feel like one?*

*Breathe. In. Out. His chest is rising and falling under mine, slow and enormous, and he is waiting. He isn't pushing. He isn't pulling. He just sits there like a mountain that has agreed, for the length of one breath, to stay still.*

*I could leave. Right now. He'd let me. I know it the way I know when a floor will hold.*

*That's what I can't stand. Not the heat, not the weight, not the bells ringing in time with my pulse. It's that he has given me back the one thing I'd stopped bothering to want, and now I have to decide what to do with it.*

*Daniel, I'm not asking for permission. I know you can't give it.*

*I'm asking you to understand. Whatever I do next, I'm still here. I'm still counting. I'm still going home.*

*And I am still, God help me, the one who decides.*

Her fingers tightened around his. She drew one long, uneven breath, and when she spoke, her voice came out rough and low, with no sharpness in it at all.

"Don't you dare let go of my hands."

Then she began to move, slowly, at her own pace, and the bells followed her.

---

*"Black Huntress."*

*He says it like a title, not a brand. Not the name they stamped on me at the market, not the label on the cube. He says it the way the people in Baltimore said it, with their faces turned up in the dark, when I was the thing standing between them and the sky.*

*Beautiful. Super beautiful. The words come out so plain and unguarded, like a child naming a color. He isn't flattering me. He doesn't know how. He's reporting what he sees.*

*And what he sees is a woman above him, in control of the pace, with her hands held in his. Not a woman beneath. Not a woman bent. He's looking up at me, and he's looking up like I'm something to be looked up to.*

*Don't. Don't let that in.*

*It doesn't change the collar. It doesn't change what he is, or what this house is, or what's at the end of the deal. A kind man can still hold a leash. Kindness doesn't unlock anything. Kindness is just the sweetest thing a jailer can wear.*

*Slowly. He said slowly.*

*So slow, then. Make it slow. Make him wait. If I'm the one setting the pace, then every second is mine, and he can sit there in the warm dark and learn what it costs to be patient with a woman like me.*

*Breathe in. Move. Out.*

*The kiss tastes like mint and lavender and something I have no name for. His thumbs stroke the backs of my hands. His heartbeat comes up through my palms, enormous and unhurried, and mine, traitorous and stupid, starts keeping time with it.*

*That's not surrender. A drummer follows a drummer. It's nothing.*

*Daniel, I'm still here.*

*I know what this is. I know exactly what it is. I'm watching myself do it, and I'm not looking away.*

*Amara. Chidi. Daniel.*

*Slowly.*

*Hold the line.*

The bells answered, one soft chime at a time, in the rhythm she set.

---

*Faster.*

*Who decided faster? Not the drill and not the plan. It was my hips, on their own, the way a horse breaks into a canter when the ground finally turns firm. I started slow, I swear I did. I was counting. Then somewhere around the fourth bar of that stupid, ringing music the counting stopped, and now it's only the rhythm, and the rhythm doesn't care what I think.*

*The bells. Every stroke sets them off, bright and clear and merciless, so that the whole house knows what I'm doing. A tiny chorus announcing me.*

*"Super cool."*

*Super* cool.

*I am the woman who put a Val tank on a satellite, and he looks up at me with that open, dazzled face and says* super cool, *like a boy who's just watched a fireworks show.*

*Then the hand. A flat, heavy crack across my backside that rings through the room and sends a flush of heat all the way up to my ears.*

*Don't. Don't you dare enjoy that. Don't you dare.*

*He's grinning. He's not even being cruel. It's a clap, a cheer, the way you slap the flank of a good horse at the end of a race.*

*I should be furious. I* am *furious. I'm furious in every muscle, and the fury is the engine, that's all it is, rage turned into motion, the way I used to throw a car.*

*That's the whole secret. It's all anger. If it feels like something else, it's because anger and heat are the same fire at different distances.*

*Look at his face. Look at what I'm doing to it. That wide green brow gone slack, that blunt mouth falling open, a thing that could crush a city lost in a woman it paid for. I could ruin him like this. I could make him say anything.*

*That's power. That's the closest thing to power I've had since the collar.*

*Isn't it?*

*Daniel.*

*Daniel, I can't hear you.*

*Amara. Chi...*

*Amara.*

*Hold the...*

*Hold...*

The bells ran together into one long, ringing peal, and Nia's head tipped back, and the roll call she had kept every night for six years slipped quietly out of her grasp.

---

*"Ah♥."*

*That was mine. That was my voice, with that pink little heart on the end of it, and it went out into the room and rang against the bells, and now it's out there and I can't get it back.*

*He heard it. Look at him. Eyes shut, head tipped, a smile spreading across his blunt green face like a man listening to a favorite song on a good set of speakers.*

*And the hand again. Another crack across the same place, right over the first, and heat blooms there, hard and bright. He's posing now. One brow lifted, chin up, a tusked grin. The suave leading man from an Earth magazine, all borrowed charm and no idea how it fits. He's so bad at it. He looks like a boulder doing an impression of a swan.*

*It would be funny. It would be hilarious, if I weren't the one being listened to.*

*"Sing, however you like."*

*Sing.*

*As if I were a bird he'd hung in the window. As if the bells weren't enough, and he wanted the rest of the orchestra.*

*No.*

*That's mine. Of everything in this house, the voice is the one thing I've never handed over. Not in the barracks, not in the cube, not on my knees with the liturgy in my mouth. I recited his words. I never once sang my own.*

*I won't perform for him. I won't be a good girl with a pretty noise. If I make a sound, it will be because my body is a traitor, not because he asked.*

*Clamp down. Teeth together. Nose, breathe through the nose.*

*...Don't look at his face. He's waiting. He's so patient. He's holding my hands, and he's not even pushing.*

*Damn him. It would be so much easier if he pushed.*

*All right. Here's the truth, Nia, since nobody else is listening: you can't stop all of it. You never could. The body has its own mind, and it's louder than yours right now.*

*So let the sound be what it is. A reflex. An accident. A thing that happens to you.*

*It doesn't mean yes. It doesn't mean anything. It's just noise, and noise isn't a word.*

*Daniel knows. Daniel always knew the difference.*

*Amara. Chidi. Daniel.*

*I'm still the one holding the line.*

*Even now.*

*Even if I'm...*

*Ah...*

The sound slipped out anyway, low and wavering, beneath the chiming. She turned her face into his shoulder to hide it, and kept moving.

---

*A record.*

*An old Earth record. A scratchy, warm, mildly dusty thing he'd take out of a cardboard sleeve, set on the turntable, and lower the needle onto. He probably has one. He probably found it in a ruined apartment, brushed off the ash, and carried it home like a relic.*

*And now I'm the music.*

*"Low, husky, and rich." The words go in like warm water, and that's exactly the trouble. They're not cruel. Nobody's ever said that about my voice. The barracks wanted silence, the market wanted insults, the Accord wanted orders called across a burning street, loud and fast and flat. Daniel said I sounded like honey on gravel when I was half asleep. That was the last time anyone described my voice as anything other than a tool.*

*Another crack across my backside, and the heat there is almost a signature now. He's marking me again, a third stamp over the same place, and I should feel branded. I should feel owned.*

*Why do I feel counted?*

*"Be freer."*

*Freer. He's asking me to be free, in the one room in the world where I'm not.*

*Listen to the contradiction, Nia. It's so tidy it could be a joke. Collar on my neck, bells on my body, a deal that rests on a green giant's good mood, and the word he picks is* freer. *As if there's a version of this where I open the cage door myself and call it dancing.*

*I can't be free here. But I can be honest.*

*So here's the honest part. I'm not singing for him. If a sound comes out of me, it's mine, and it goes where I send it, and he doesn't get to frame it on his wall. Let him think what he likes. Let him hum along. He doesn't own the song. He only found the record.*

*Breathe. Let it out low.*

*No performance. No pink little heart. Just the voice, whatever's left of it, rough as gravel and warm as honey.*

*Daniel, if you can hear me, this one's still mine.*

The sound came out of her unhurried, deep in her chest, low and a little cracked, and she let it ring under the bells without once looking at his face.

*Amara. Chidi. Daniel.*

*Hold the line.*

---

*Mini flying lizard.*

*He remembered. The merchant's sales pitch, with its ranch and its stock and its proven record, and the overgrown pickle has been carrying it around all night, turning it over like a pebble in his pocket.*

*"It's not that, is it?"*

*No. It isn't. And he knows it isn't, which means he's been watching my face for the answer and paying attention to what it says.*

*He's not trying to tame me. That's what he's telling me, in the clumsiest sentence in the galaxy. He doesn't want the lizard. He wants the woman who looks like she's about to bite.*

*"Curse me out. Just have some fun."*

*Fun.*

*A crack across my backside, another palm mark pressed over the others, and the heat is so layered there I can't tell where one ends. He's laughing. He's laughing at the sheer ridiculousness of it, with his hands holding mine and a scowl-eating grin on his face, and for one strange second it doesn't feel like a leash at all. It feels like being teased by someone who actually likes me.*

*Don't.*

*...But he did say I could.*

*I've been saving them, haven't I? Every one of them, honed since the market, loaded and waiting behind my teeth. I swore I'd spend them all the day the collar came off. But he's just handed me the whole armory and told me to open fire.*

*Fine.*

*Fine. You asked for it.*

"You overgrown," she managed, breathless, her voice cracking low and rough over the bells, "swamp-smelling, lemon-apron-handing, advice-column-reading *pickle.* You stink. You're the worst thing that's ever happened to me. You're rougher and uglier than any soldier I've ever met, and I hope the Tau Ceti brittle stars choke on you, *ah*—"

She bit down on the rest, furious, but it was out there, ringing, and he was grinning wider than ever.

*That felt good.*

*That felt good. Damn it, that felt* good. *Weeks of swallowing every barb, and it was like opening a window in a sealed room.*

*It doesn't mean anything. It's only the first real breath I've taken since the market.*

*So give him another.*

"And you hold your beer like a man holding a grudge!"

*Amara. Chidi. Daniel.*

*...Louder.*

*Hold the line.*
