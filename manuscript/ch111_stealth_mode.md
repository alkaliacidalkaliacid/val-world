## Chapter 111: Stealth Mode

The monster took Bog's order the way it took all orders: completely, and at once.

Earth folded around them. The breach in the cavern wall widened into a throat, and the monster flowed into it, digging as it went — tentacles churning stone aside like water — while within its own body, behind walls of warm, slick flesh, a space opened. It formed the way a fist unclenches: a compartment lined in smooth, pale membrane, lit by a soft bioluminescent glow, with the floor levelling itself beneath their feet and recesses rising out of the walls like furniture grown rather than made.

"All passengers will be accommodated," the monster said, from everywhere and nowhere. "Please remain inside the living space while transit is in progress."

Rin, halfway in, stopped dead. "Wait — my suit! It's still down there, you can't just— please, it's *mine*, I built it, I can't leave it—"

A tentacle uncoiled past her into the dark. It returned cradling thirty feet of dead armour as gently as a nurse with a baby, and laid the wreck in a recess of its own, where smaller tentacles were already tucking it in.

Rin made a sound no one had ever heard her make, and threw her arms around the nearest tentacle.

Bog tested one of the grown seats with his considerable weight. It held. It even, obscenely, adjusted.

"Comfy," he said, with approval, and sat down.

And so, in the belly of a tentacle monster, travelling beneath the earth, four people caught their breath — and realised, all at once, that they had never actually been introduced.

Priya, set down at last and smoothing her clothes with shaking hands, went first, because manners were a raft and she was clinging to it. "I— I don't think we've properly— I am Priya. Dr. Priya Raghavan. Of the Free Clinics. In Florence." A beat. Her smile wobbled. "You— you've been carrying me. And watching over me. Thank you."

"Nia Okonkwo," said Nia. "The Black Huntress." She said it levelly, chin up, bells and all, daring the room to comment.

"And I'm Rin! Yan Rin." Rin bounced in her grown seat, one arm still draped proprietorially toward her suit. "I do machines. That one's mine. Also I do kicks." She peered at Priya with enormous interest. "You're the one who was glowing. And floating. And—" She stopped, red to the ears, at the memory of everything else. "Um. Hi."

"Hi," said Priya faintly, red to hers.

Bog beamed at all of them like a man at a dinner party he had catered himself.

Nia smiled too. She crossed her legs. She folded her hands. She breathed, slowly, through her mouth, because breathing through her nose meant breathing *him* — he was right there, an arm's length away, in an enclosed space, after all this time, and her body had stopped consulting her about what it wanted somewhere back in the cavern. Her mouth watered. She swallowed. She swallowed again. The conversation went on around her — clinics, Florence, the suit, small civilised words — and Nia Okonkwo, the Black Huntress, sat in her seat with her hands knotted white in her lap and fought the most undignified battle of her life to simply, politely, not climb into his lap in front of witnesses.
