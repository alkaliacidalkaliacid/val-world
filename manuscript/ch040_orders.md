## Chapter 40: Orders


Bog stood in the middle of the pod, rolling his bad shoulder to work out the numbness, and addressed the walls the way he addressed a quartermaster who owed him a favor.

"All right. Listen carefully, because I'm only going to say this once, and then I'm going to say it again, because I know how you are."

"Awaiting orders, Commander."

"One. The prisoner sleeps well. Every night, soft bedding, no draft. She eats plenty, three square meals a day, plus the special one we just discussed. Two. Keep her safe from injury and illness. I mean *any* injury, any illness, any bug that crawls in through a crack in the floor." He paused, frowned, and glanced at the gray band at her throat on the monitor. "Though with that collar on her, I'm not sure a supervillain needs much protecting from anything. Do it anyway."

"Understood."

"Three. Everything else, every spare minute, goes into taming her." His grin crept back. "Drill it into her that the sole purpose of an Earth woman is to serve a Val male and bear his offspring. And above all else, make her understand that I'm her ideal master. Not the best master, not a good master. The *ideal* one. Make her practice every form of service you can think of. I don't care how many hours it takes."

"Every conceivable form of service," the voice repeated, with the flat diligence of a machine that had never been told to be embarrassed. "Duration: indefinite."

"Good. And four." Bog wagged a finger at the console. "She'll answer *everything* in Russian profanity. I'm betting she has quite the vocabulary. I want a record of every word. Log it, rank it, we'll compare notes when I'm back."

"Recording. Unique vocabulary count: three hundred and twelve and rising."

"Three hundred and twelve already?" Bog whistled, deeply impressed. "That's a *professional*."

Somewhere in the creature's belly, faintly, a woman's hoarse voice was cursing in a language he did not speak. He listened for a moment with something close to admiration.

"Now. Transport. Do we have an air-car in there?"

"Negative."

"Holy Val. How about a jetpack?"

"Define 'jetpack.'"

Bog opened his mouth, closed it, and tried again. "A thing you wear on your back. It, uh, makes you go up."

"Closest match located: personal propulsion unit, Class Two, in good condition."

"What does 'Class Two' mean?"

"Maximum airspeed: one hundred and fifty miles per hour. Maximum payload: one adult Val. Safety features: none."

"Fine, whatever. That'll do." Bog waved a hand. A hatch slid open in the wall and a squat, battered contraption slid out on a tray of cables, all bolts and dented plating and a tangle of fuel lines. He strapped it on with his good arm, tugged on the buckles, and flexed his shoulders. It gave a rattling cough.

"Sounds like me after a bad night," he said happily.

He climbed to the mouth of the burrow, where the monster had opened a long tunnel through the mud to a patch of gray daylight. He stopped there with one boot on the lip and turned back toward the dark.

"I'm heading to Appalachia to meet up with Nia. The clinic, the doctor, the toolkit. While I'm gone, you keep looking after Sonya. Every order stands."

"Understood, Commander."

"And be gentle with her," he added after a moment, as if the thought had just occurred to him. "Within reason."

The voice paused. "Please define 'within reason.'"

"Uh, never mind."

He laughed, and kept laughing, a wide, rumbling, thoroughly satisfied sound that echoed down the tunnel behind him. Then he fired up the jetpack. It choked, spat, caught, and carried him up through the ragged opening in a ball of smoke, zigzagging north through the pale morning sky toward the mountains.

The negotiations with the Free Clinic would not be easy. But for the first time in a very long week, he had sent a toolkit on its way to the right hands, and a monster at home to do the housework.

Behind him, deep in the earth, the cables began to move.
