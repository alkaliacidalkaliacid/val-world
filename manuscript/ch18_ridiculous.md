## Chapter 18: Ridiculous


"Ridiculous," Nia repeated, to no one in particular. She planted a stiletto in the cracked asphalt, wobbled, and caught herself on his forearm with a muttered curse. The heel was seven inches of malice. Every step came with a small, bright *ting* from her bells, and the pom-pom tail swayed in perfect rhythm, advertising each one.

"You keep saying that," Bog said cheerfully. His free hand was still resting on the swell of her backside, thumb tracing slow, idle circles as if he were checking the ripeness of fruit at a market stall. "I think you're doing great. Very natural."

"Take your hand *off*♥..."

"Mm, no."

The♥ had slipped out on its own, between the words, light as a feather. She heard it. He heard it. She set her teeth and walked faster, which only made the beads shift inside her, and the next word came out as a strangled hiss. Her cheeks burned under the half-mask, and she was fiercely grateful for it.

*Tomatoes. Scotch bonnet. Thyme.*

*Not a word. Not one word. Keep your eyes on the horizon.*

The wasteland stretched out ahead of them in rust and ash, a dead freeway curving through the haze like a shed snakeskin. Twisted rebar jutted from the rubble. A burned-out bus sat on its rims with its windows blown out and a small, stubborn weed growing through the driver's seat. Somewhere far above, too high to see, a Val patrol drone hummed along its route, and Bog didn't even glance up.

"Y'know," he said, "I really do think we could have done this over the net. Nobody at the Dominion is watching the old channels. Half the sentries can't work a keypad."

"Then why are we *here*♥?"

"Because the person we're meeting doesn't trust anything that can be traced." He shrugged his enormous shoulders. "Fair enough. I wouldn't either, in their position."

"Then why am I dressed like..." She gestured sharply at herself and nearly toppled over. Bog steadied her with an easy hand at her waist. "...like *this*?"

"Disguise."

"There are *plain* disguises!"

"There are," Bog agreed. "This one's better."

She opened her mouth for what would have been a truly spectacular insult and found, much to her horror, that the only thing that came to mind was a drawn-out *haaa* that began in her chest and refused to be anything but a sigh. The beads had picked that exact moment to turn a slow, deliberate quarter-rotation. She clamped her mouth shut and glared at the dirt.

Bog, who had felt the full-body shiver through her arm, merely hummed a tuneless little melody and gave her backside another unhurried squeeze.

Ahead, past a wall of tumbled concrete slabs, the ruin of a gas station slouched against the sky. Its canopy had collapsed years ago and its pumps were rusted into stumps. A single figure waited in the shade of the fallen roof, leaning on a post with an air of long, practiced patience. A wide-brimmed hat hid the face, and a long coat hung from narrow shoulders. Whether the silhouette belonged to a man or a woman was impossible to say at this distance. It stood very still, one gloved hand lifted in a slow, lazy wave.

Bog's grin widened. "Ah. That'll be them."

Nia straightened, her spine going rigid, and for one moment the soldier surfaced through the layers of silk and fishnet and bunny fluff. Her eyes narrowed behind the mask, sweeping the rubble, the rooftops, the dark gaps between slabs. Counting exits. Counting shadows.

"Remember," Bog murmured, pitching his voice low. "You're a pet. Don't speak unless I say so. Don't break anything unless I say so, either."

"I know the *plan*."

"Good." He patted her backside one last time, entirely without shame, and started forward. "Then let's go meet our friend."

The bells chimed softly as she followed, one careful, humiliating step at a time.
