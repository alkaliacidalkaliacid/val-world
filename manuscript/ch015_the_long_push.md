## Chapter 15: The Long Push


"You *♥ ngh* ... you absolute...*♥*... pit-dwelling, ... tusk-less ... *haa*♥ ..."

"Mm. Keep going. That one was new."

His bare hands moved over her belly in long, unhurried strokes, from the ribs down toward the hip, the way a physical therapist would coax a stubborn muscle. Nia would have recognized the technique in any other context. She had used it herself, on elderly patients in a clinic that smelled of liniment and burnt coffee. Now it was her belly under the green palms, and the thing that had been a knot an hour ago was giving way inch by inch under that slow, relentless pressure.

The jelly kept coming. It was clear and faintly green, pooling in a glossy coil against the rug, and it gave off a scent like cut cucumber and warm rain. It was the cleanest, least offensive thing in the room, and somehow that made it worse.

"Can't you just bear down and snap it?" Bog asked, in the same tone he might have used to ask about the weather. "No? Right. Then push it all the way out. Don't clench, you'll only make it stubborn."

"I know that!♥ I was *treating* people before your mother's cradle..." She broke off, because Val had no mothers, and the joke died in her throat. Her whole body shook. The bells chimed in a bright, unbroken run.

*Amara. Chidi. Daniel.*

*Tomatoes. Scotch bonnet. Thyme.*

*Hold the line. Hold the line. It is only a body. It is only a body.*

"You're doing great," Bog said. "Really. Look at that, it's practically art."

"Shut♥ up! Shut *up*♥!" Her voice cracked on the second one, and her fingers dug into the rug until the weave tore. Heat flooded her face and neck, and it wasn't only the strain. A long tremor ran down her thighs and she hated it, hated that her body, which had been broken and rebuilt and fed on his seed for weeks, had learned to answer to those palms.

He felt it. Of course he did. His hands paused for a half second, and then, tactfully, he pretended not to have noticed and went on with the strokes.

"Nearly there," he murmured. "One more good push, Nia. You've got it. I've got you."

She did not say *thank you*. She would have bitten off her tongue first.

She pushed.

The last stretch slid free all at once, and the pressure that had been squeezing her for hours let go in a rush so sudden that her breath left her in a thin, shuddering moan. Her forehead dropped onto the rug. Her shoulders trembled. For a long moment all she could hear was her own ragged breathing and the faint, silly chiming of the bells as they settled.

Bog's hand rested lightly on her lower back. He was grinning, she could tell without looking, that stupid crooked grin of his.

"Feel better?"

She turned her face away, into the crook of her arm, and mumbled something into her own skin that might have been *I hate you*, and might, for the first time, have lacked its usual conviction.

"Didn't catch that," Bog said.

"...I said bring me *water*, you overgrown cactus."

The household robot, loyal to a fault, was already rolling toward the kitchen.
