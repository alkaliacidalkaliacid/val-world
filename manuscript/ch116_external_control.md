## Chapter 116: External Control

"The whole loyalty assessment is a load of bullshit," Nia declared, to the room, to the ceiling, to whatever part of the universe accepted formal complaints. "All of it. The numbers, the holograms, the—" She jabbed a finger at the air above her own collar, where the 93% had lately been shining. "It measures nothing. It is a toy a machine made to torment—"

"Ooh," said Rin.

Her eyes were sparkling. She had been turning her head slowly, looking at the three collars in the room one after another, the way she looked at engines other people had given up on.

"Before anybody takes one of these off," she said, "we should try the good stuff. Amplification. Or a burst." She bounced a little on her heels. "You don't throw away a machine before you find out what it does. That's just waste. That's bad manners to the machine."

"Rin," Nia said, in the tone of a woman watching someone reach for a stove, "you are not a test bench."

"Which one?" Bog asked.

Rin lit up like a festival. "Amplification!"

Bog turned the wristband on his forearm, found what he was looking for in the way he had been shown, and gave the command.

Rin's hair rose.

It went up strand by strand, lifting off her shoulders and standing out around her head in a crackling halo, spitting tiny blue sparks into the air. Her eyes went wide — then they gleamed, bright and fixed, and her hands came up and turned over in front of her face as if she were reading them.

"Oh," she breathed. "Oh, oh, oh. I can feel how it sits. The suppression isn't a wall, it's— the whole thing is woven, it's—" Her fingers twitched, plucking at empty air, tracing connections nobody else could see. "I think I've got a handle on how this collar is built!"

Then her head snapped up. She stared at the wall of the living space — at the monster all around them — and her mouth fell open in pure, scandalised delight.

"Wait. Wait wait wait. I've figured out this monster, too!" She spun in a circle, arms out, sparks flying off her hair. "It's the same thinking! All of it! I could build— give me time and parts and I could make an incredible suit out of this!"

"Rin," Nia said again, with less authority than before.

"Now the burst!" Rin cried.

Bog, who had been watching her the way a man watches fireworks — head back, thoroughly pleased — shrugged and gave the command.

The static went over Rin all at once.

It sheeted across her skin in a crawling blue-white web, snapped and sang around the collar at her throat — and answered. Across the room, the collar on Nia's throat flared with light. Priya's lit a breath later — though no command had touched either of them — and Priya gasped and clutched at the air. The floor itself took up a deep, rising rumble, through the soles of their feet, through their teeth.

"Warning," the monster announced, from everywhere. "External control has been established."

"Holy Val," said Bog.

The discharge broke off like a cut wire.

The light went out of the other collars. Rin's hair drifted down around her shoulders, ordinary hair again, and she swayed once, smiling vaguely at nobody — and folded, straight down, in a heap.

"Rin!" Nia was across the room before the girl hit the floor, and only just managed to make it a landing instead of a fall. She got her head up, fingers at her throat, at the collar. "What did it do to her? What happened to her?"

"It appears," the monster said, "she has exhausted her stored power."
