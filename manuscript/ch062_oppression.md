## Chapter 62: Oppression


The bear came in through the hole it had torn in the wall where the door had been. For a moment it stood in the wreckage, nostrils flaring. It caught the scent of the sprawled, green, thoroughly unappetizing alien on the floor, and it snorted in open disgust.

Then it turned its head and made a beeline for Priya.

Arjun stepped into its path with the fire poker held in both shaking hands. He was a thin man with grease in the creases of his knuckles, and he planted his feet as though he meant to stay there. Behind him, the girls screamed. Dadi, in the doorway of the back room, said something in Hindi that was almost certainly a prayer.

"Hold on there, furball!" a voice called from the rubble.

Bog's head rose out of the splintered remains of the dining table. He pointed at the bear with an accusing finger.

"Aren't *you* the one being invaded here? Where's your pride?" He heaved himself upright, shedding plaster. "Don't worry. I'll show you how this is done. Time for some oppression!"

He lunged.

He did not so much run at the bear as fall toward it with an enormous green body, arms wide, and he caught the creature around its thick neck before it could swing. The impact shook the floor. Plates leaped off the shelves. The bear roared into his face, and its breath smelled of blood and old iron. It reared, raking at him with claws the length of kitchen knives.

"Ow! Hey! That's my good shoulder! Well, the other good one!"

Bog was a Val. He was seven and a half feet of bone and muscle, and in all his years of service he had never lost a wrestling match to anything that did not have a hull. He dug in his heels and squeezed.

The bear did not budge.

It was the aura that Priya noticed. It shimmered along the animal's back, and as Bog's arms tightened, the purple light began to pulse. It was slow and steady, like a heartbeat that was not the bear's.

"Bog," she said faintly. "It's not alone in there."

The bear's eyes rolled toward her. For one terrible instant they were not the eyes of an animal at all.
