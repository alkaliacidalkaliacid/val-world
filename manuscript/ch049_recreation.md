## Chapter 49: Recreation


The queue had a system, which surprised no one who had served in the Val infantry. Rank went first, then seniority, then whoever had won the dice game behind the supply truck. Sergeant Gorrak, who had lost the dice game and was taking it badly, stood at the end of the line eating a roasted beetle and glaring at the backs of his comrades.

Inside the tent, on a cot built for someone nearly a foot taller, Nia Okonkwo lay on her side with her hands cuffed in front of her and stared at the canvas ceiling.

*Four hundred and twenty-three.*

She had counted the first one an hour ago, when the squad's lieutenant ripped the tail out of her costume, examined it, and announced with a delighted laugh that she was "already fitted." She had counted the second when the lieutenant's palm came down on her right buttock and her whole body answered like a struck bell. The bells did chime. They chimed sweetly, in the pleasant tone the Val adored, and that was almost the worst part.

"*Physiology*," she said to the ceiling. "It's a reflex. A stupid, mechanical, stupid reflex."

The big green soldier currently occupied with her was too busy to ask her to repeat herself. He did not seem to mind that she was cursing him. The Val never minded the cursing. They treated it the way a man treats a dog that barks while it wags its tail.

*Tomato. Scotch bonnet. Thyme.*

The litany still worked, though it had worn thin. *Amara. Chidi. Daniel.* She added a name tonight she had not used in weeks: *Pickle.* She had no idea why. She decided it was a tactical mnemonic for the toolkit.

The toolkit. She had managed one look at it, through the gap between two broad shoulders, before the lieutenant tossed it onto the supply stack with a handful of other junk. A dull gray case, scuffed at the corners, sitting beside a crate of ration bars. *Class 5.* She had carried it through an escape pod crash and four miles of Mississippi pine without letting go. They had pried it out of her hands only after the fourth palm print, and she had not told them what it was. She had not told them after the eleventh, either.

She would tell them nothing. She would not say *Hermes* or *Appalachia* or *collar*. Whatever else was done to her tonight, she would hold that one door shut.

"Hey, Bunny," the soldier said, in thick, amused Val, slapping her thigh. "Look at this. She's *clenching*. She does it when I say 'tail.' Does she know she's doing it?"

"Ask her," said a voice from outside the flap.

"I know exactly what I'm doing," Nia said. The♥ slipped into the middle of it before she could catch it. "Go♥ choke on your own♥ boots, you overgrown♥ lizard."

The tent roared with laughter. Outside, down the queue, someone cheered.

She shut her eyes. Her face burned, and she let it, because shame was a reasonable thing to feel and she could use it. *Hold the corridor. Nine days. Thirty thousand people.* She had held a bridge against a tank division once. She could hold a cot for one night.

But the thought that surfaced next was not about her children, and she hated that it wasn't. It was about a rattling aircar and a fat green idiot's hands kneading her thighs. It was about the way he had thrown her into the pod and sealed the hatch without looking back, because his species had never invented the farewell.

*He's dead,* she told herself flatly. *The monster ate him. Stop it.*

Outside, the Val contractors huddled by the fuel drums, watching the tent flap with their jaws set and their eyes unable to look away. One of them, a thin man with a cracked radio, muttered something about the Geneva Conventions. The man beside him told him to shut up before somebody heard.

At the far end of the perimeter, the night shift lamps flickered, though nothing had touched them. A pine needle shivered on the hood of the nearest truck, then another. Sergeant Gorrak stopped chewing and looked at the dark treeline, and then, with great reluctance, down at his own boots.

The ground was humming.

In the tent, one of the bells at Nia's chest chimed again, very softly, with no one touching it.
