## Chapter 28: Offense and Escape


Sonya stopped holding anything back.

Every disc she had been keeping up for stealth, the dinner-plate mirrors, the thin film of the sphere, the silent shells around the bunny's bells, flared outward at once and turned to the work of killing. The warehouse became a machine for her. Drones swooped in, found a portal opening ahead of them, and came out of another one at full speed, slamming into walls and ceiling girders and each other in clouds of sparks. A heavy security robot rolled into the aisle and its gun arm vanished mid-swing, reappearing in the middle of its own chest. Soldiers charged around a corner and were simply gone. No scream, no body, no echo, only boots on a floor that was suddenly empty. Beams and slugs fired in her direction went in at one point and came out at another, ricocheting off at wild angles through stacks of crates, banks of lockers, and the glass of the observation booth far overhead.

*Spending everything,* Nia thought, flattened against a pillar with the toolkit crushed against her stomach. *She's not even trying to be precise anymore.*

The monster was the only thing that mattered now, and Sonya knew it. A barrage of portals converged on the thing, dozens of them, winking open along its flanks and snapping shut again, and bite-sized chunks of cable and plating fell away in flurries. It didn't seem to care. It tore the floor up in great sheets and swallowed them. It plucked rifles out of dead soldiers' hands and drew them into its mass, and the weapons sank into the cables with a sound like a wet gulp. Every wound closed behind the blow that made it. Every tentacle she cut was replaced by two. The soldiers and drones were still shooting at her, and she let them. Every beam, every burst aimed at the pale woman went into a disc and came out along the monster's flanks, so that half the depot's fire was pouring into the creature — and she was the one aiming it. The wounds it made closed as fast as the wounds she made.

Sonya opened a single enormous disc, wider than any she'd made all night, and drew a line straight through the monster's middle. The creature split nearly in half. For one second its two halves hung there, shuddering, and Nia's heart leapt.

Then the halves reached for each other and knitted together, as if she'd cut through fog.

"No core," Sonya hissed, and wiped the sweat from her eyes with the back of one wrist. She wasn't smiling now. "Nothing to kill."

A tentacle the thickness of a pylon smashed into the floor beside her, and she slid into a disc and out again, breathing hard. Nia saw her left hand tremble before she hid it in her coat.

Something warm and heavy slammed into her shoulder.

"Gah!" She nearly dropped the case. It was Bog, crawling the last yard on his good elbow and one knee, his left arm dangling like a rope, his uniform torn at the hip. He reached her pillar, wheezed, and sat down against it with the satisfied air of a man arriving at a restaurant.

"Hi," he said.

"Where's your gun?"

"Out of charge." He gave it a moment's thought. "Or I dropped it. Anyway." He peered around the pillar at the thrashing mass, the screaming alarms, and the pale woman at the heart of it all, who was fighting the biggest battle of her life without looking back. Another tentacle sailed across the hall and exploded a stack of crates. A hail of splintered wood pattered down onto the plating.

"Uh," Bog said. "Tell you what. Let's look for an escape pod."
