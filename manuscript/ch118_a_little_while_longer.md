## Chapter 118: A Little While Longer

"Well," said Bog, watching Priya settle back to the floor, bright-eyed and windswept. "That's good. That's real good. That's—"

He said it twice more, quieter each time.

"—wait a minute."

His head tilted. A thought had arrived, and it was taking its boots off.

"Does this mean," he said slowly, "that these vintage collars we pulled from our own arsenal aren't the restraints the Elites think they are? In fact — it's the opposite. They're actually weapons?"

Nobody answered him. He pondered it anyway, and the pondering was not comfortable. Somewhere out there, for all he knew, Earth's superheroes were still alive — captured, collared, filed away. The Elites believed they had those heroes under control. What they had actually done, if Bog had this right, was hand each of them an energy rifle with a fully charged power pack — and the safety still on.

If those heroes ever figured out how to disengage that safety...

"Right, then!" Bog announced, with an exaggerated, beaming smile. "Let's all take these collars off."

He went to Rin first — she was nearest, and still propped on her elbows. The wristband on his forearm woke; he pressed it to her collar, and the collar opened with a small, definite click. Rin's fingers lingered on it as it came away, as if her hand had not been consulted.

Priya's turn. Her hands came up under the collar as it released and stayed cupped beneath it, catching it like something fragile — or something she was not entirely sure she wanted to be rid of.

Then Bog turned to Nia.

He hesitated. What would happen if he removed her collar right now? Her powers would come back — all of them, the strength, the spirits, everything the collar had held down since the market. And what would happen if he didn't?

"I'm about to take your collar off," he said, "but before I do, let me just say one thing: this is an enclosed space. If you decide to punch me into a pulp, everyone's going to end up splattered with what used to be me. It's just not very hygienic — so, uh..."

Nia glared at him.

Then the glare softened into a smile, and her fingers came up and traced, slowly, the collar around her neck.

"Actually," she said, "I think I'll keep wearing this for a little while longer."

"Huh!?" said Bog.

Nia purred — sweet, coaxing, entirely pleased with herself. "Do you have a problem with that?"

Behind him, Rin tucked her collar out of sight behind her back. "I won't wear mine around my neck," she said, "but I'll hold onto it. I'd like to study it."

Priya cradled hers in both arms, held against her stomach. "And if it has a communication function," she said, "I could reach out to you, Bog, if an emergency ever arises..."

Bog felt the cold sweat arrive, on schedule. Three women. Three collars. Every single one of them, in one way or another, keeping his.

He shrugged.

"Well," he said. "Do as you please."
