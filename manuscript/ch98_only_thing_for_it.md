## Chapter 98: Only Thing for It

Rin ran at her.

Not at the argument — the argument was over her head in every sense — but at Priya, at the woman inside the red light, the way you run at a friend who is standing somewhere stupidly dangerous. She jumped.

Priya hung a full body's length above the cavern floor, robed in red, unmoving, and Rin's fingers missed her feet by a foot.

"Come DOWN!" Rin shouted, and jumped again, and again, springing straight up off the stone with her whole body in it, arms windmilling at the top of each leap. "You get down here! You don't get to— you don't say all that and then just FLOAT—"

"R-Rin, I c-can't—" Priya's voice, out of the red mouth, wretched. "I c-can't get— she w-won't—"

"You will attend from where you are," the Mediatoress said, in the same mouth, without looking down. "The incarnation's distress is noted. It changes nothing."

"J-just let me DOWN—"

"Nothing is required of your feet."

Rin jumped. Rin jumped again. On the fourth jump she got a hand to the hem of the manifested robe and hung there a moment, kicking, before the fabric of it — which was not fabric — gave her nothing to hold, and she dropped back to the floor and landed in a crouch, furious, breathing hard, glaring up.

Then both of them turned on Bog.

"Bog! Do something!"

"P-please— B-Bog, do s-something—"

Bog scratched his cheek.

"Something, huh," he said.

He considered the situation the way he considered situations, which was to file it next to the ones it resembled. The tentacle monster had ended up wearing a collar, and taking orders, and singing. Sonya had ended up wearing a collar, and taking training, and learning an anthem against her will. Rin, with the dead thing riding inside her, had ended up wearing a collar, and taking her meals off his lap, and falling asleep against his chest with her face in a place that embarrassed her in the mornings.

Bog looked down at the last collar on his belt.

"Only thing for it," he said. "Priya. Do your best and get yourself down here. Rin — soon as she's down far enough, grab her and hold on."

Priya's whole face worked. "I— I d-don't know h-how—"

"You do it the way you stopped her before, sweetheart. Same muscle."

The red light stuttered.

It was not a descent, at first. It was an argument conducted in altitude. The body in the light sank an inch and stopped, sank another and hung, trembling, while the mouth on it conducted both sides of the war.

"This is without purpose," the Mediatoress said. "The incarnation will tire. The account will keep."

"D-down," Priya ground out, through her own teeth. "Down, down, d-down— let— let GO of me, she's— I'm g-going DOWN—"

"The body is mine to—"

"IT'S M-MINE—"

She dropped the last of it all at once, not far, not gracefully — her feet found the cavern floor the way a dropped thing finds it, and she staggered, red robe flaring, still upright only because the light held her spine like a hand.

Rin was already in the air.

She hit the red light at the top of her leap and stopped there, a body's length off the floor, held. For one moment she was too surprised to move. Then she was furious in an entirely new direction — kicking at nothing, swinging her arms, twisting in the grip of the light like a fish on a line.

"LET GO! LET GO OF ME! PUT ME DOWN, YOU—"

"Attend, child," the Mediatoress said, and turned her burning eyes on the dangling girl, and her full attention with them. "You will be still. You will be—"

Bog came in from the side she was not watching.

He was seven and a half feet of Val and he did it quietly anyway, the way a man steps around a sleeping animal, and he got the collar open and around Priya's throat — over the high collar of the manifested robe, under the fall of her hair — and shut it with a click that sounded very small and very final in the stone room.

Priya froze. Both of her froze.

Bog put his arms around her, gathered the Red Mediatoress, the vessel, the whole burning armful of her, in against his chest like something he had every right to hold — and kissed her.
