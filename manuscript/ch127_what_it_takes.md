## Chapter 127: What It Takes

She did not let go of his hands.

That was the thing she would remember, later, when she was making her reports to herself: through all of it, her fingers stayed laced in his. Ten white, ten green, locked like rigging. She told herself it was for balance. She told herself it was so she could hurt him if she needed to, quickly, the small bones — she told herself a number of things, in the part of her mind that still filed reports, and the rest of her held on and bounced.

He kissed her when she came down. He talked when she went up. It was, she understood dimly, his idea of an evening.

"You've really been through a lot," Bog said, on an upstroke, with the air of a man making conversation over a fence. "With the — all the — " He nodded toward the monster's dark flank. "The training. I should've come sooner. Sorry about that."

"*Sorry♥?*" She came down hard enough to make the bells complain. "You are — sorry♥ — зелёная сволочь, you leave me in a *machine* for — for — and you say *sorry*, like a man who is late for — ах♥ — for dinner♥ —"

"Well," Bog said, reasonably, "I forgot."

She made a sound that was not in any of her languages. It had a ♥ in it anyway.

"You're bouncing so well," he said, a little later, watching her with open admiration, the way a man watches weather he approves of. "Really. I mean it. You've got — rhythm. Form. The — " he gestured vaguely at the whole of her, the softened, bell-hung, treacherously fluent whole of her, " — you can tell you've practised."

"Shut up♥," Sonya said. "Shut up, shut *up*♥ — не смей♥ — do not *praise* me for this, pig, I will — я тебе♥ — " She lost the threat entirely on a downstroke and had to start again, furious, glowing, riding him like a woman trying to win an argument with a horse. "I did not *practise♥*. I was — it was done *to* me♥. There is a difference. There is — ах♥ — a *legal* difference —"

"Sure," Bog said. "Sure. Still. Credit where it's due."

From the dark, in the tone of a trainer at ringside who has money on the outcome, the monster spoke.

"She is a hard worker, Commander. The hardest worker in the programme. Her module completion is — exemplary."

"See?" Bog said, delighted. "Exemplary."

"Молчи♥," Sonya told the darkness. "Молчи, железка♥ — you be silent, you — " The monster, courteously, was silent for almost four seconds.

"By the way," Bog said, on the next rise, in exactly the tone he had used for the weather of her bouncing, "how many children do you want to have?"

She stopped.

She stopped dead, mid-motion, sitting on him to the root with her hands locked in his and her mouth open, while the question went through her like a change of climate. Children. *Children.* Sofiya Ivanova, who had loved women briefly and left them kindly or cruelly and never once, in thirty-odd years of a life built out of exits, imagined — the word itself was a door she had never even walked past. Дети. Her. *His.*

"You are insane♥," she said, faintly. "You are — clinically — Bog. Bog♥. I kill people. I am a — я не♥ — I am not a *mother*, I am a —"

"You'd be great," Bog said, with total, ruinous sincerity. "Look how you commit to things." He squeezed her hands. "Have you decided on a name for the first one?"

"A *name♥ — *" She was laughing now, or the thing that was happening in her chest had decided to be laughing, high and helpless and appalled, while her body — her body, which had apparently seceded from the republic of her — took the opportunity of her distraction to resume, slowly, treacherously, its bouncing. "Нет♥. No. No names. No — слушай♥ — listen to me. There will be no — "

"Plenty of time," Bog said. "No rush. Something Russian, maybe. Strong." He considered. "Do you think you'll get along with the others? The other consorts. Nia, and — well. Household harmony. It's important. You'll be living together."

*Living together.* The big woman. The Black Huntress, whose *struggles* she had been compared to, whose name stood above hers on a holographic board in a burrow in the earth, first, *first*, in letters of light. Something in Sonya's face changed, and it was not softness.

"Get along♥?" she said. "With *her♥*? Bog. Милый♥. She is a — a sanctimonious, husband-haunted *cow* of a woman, I have seen her file, I — "

"She's wonderful," Bog said happily.

"— I will get along with her the way winter gets along with a *house♥* — "

And from the dark, mild, clerical, devastating, her trainer spoke again.

"She's such a hard worker," the monster said. "I think she's got what it takes to become the primary consort."

The bouncing stopped again.

Sonya sat very still, with the moon on her pale shoulders and the bells settling against her skin, and looked into the dark where the voice had come from. Primary consort. *Primary.* First, in letters of light. The big woman demoted to a footnote; the leaderboard rewritten; her own name at the top of the board, where it should have —

She was aware, with the cold clarity that still visited her at the worst moments, of exactly what was happening to her. The machine had found the last door in her that ambition could walk through, and it was holding it open, and *she was looking through it*. The assassin in her, the professional, the woman of exits, noted it down: *this is how they do it. This is the hook. It is in you now. It is — *

Her fingers tightened in Bog's. Her hips moved — once, deliberately, a full, slow, devastating stroke, the best she had in her, the exemplary one.

"Primary♥?" she said, to the darkness, to the machine, to the whole green earth. Her smile came up like a knife coming out of water. "Да♥. Watch me♥."
