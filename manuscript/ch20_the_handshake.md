## Chapter 20: The Handshake


The bespectacled man's smile never moved, but his eyes lit with the bright, hungry interest of a collector spotting a rare coin. He stepped forward and offered a slim, manicured hand. "Sonya, you can lower your finger. It's rude."

Sonya did not lower her finger. She kept it leveled at the kitschy black bow tie — and possibly at whatever was underneath it — for another three seconds, her pale eyes moving from the bow tie to Nia's masked face and back again. Then she dropped her hand, slowly, and slid it into her coat pocket.

Bog took the Broker's hand. It vanished entirely inside his green fist, like a child's mitten in a catcher's glove. He pumped it twice, with great sincerity and a good deal more strength than the situation called for.

"Well. Let's try that again," he said warmly. "Bog."

"Likewise," the Broker said, a little faintly, retrieving his fingers and flexing them behind his back where he thought nobody could see. "Shall we speak inside? The old service bay has a roof, and, if I may say so, a rather better class of shadow."

"Lead the way."

The group parted as the two of them passed, raiders edging aside with their rifles pointed carefully at nothing. A few stared at Bog's bulk. More stared at the woman on his arm. One man, young, gap-toothed, with a scarf wrapped around his chin, swallowed so audibly that the sound carried across the lot. His gaze dropped to the bare, ringing bells and then jerked away as if burned.

*Keep walking.* Nia kept her eyes forward and her chin level, a rental with nothing to say. The stilettos clicked on the broken asphalt, and each click answered itself with the soft, silly chime at her chest. *One. Two. Count the steps. Count the exits.*

She felt the Pale Murderess fall in behind them like a shadow with a pulse.

Inside, the service bay smelled of rust and ancient motor oil. A long steel workbench ran along one wall, and a handful of crates served as stools. Someone had strung a single lantern from a hook in the ceiling. Its light swung gently and threw long, wavering shapes across the oil-stained concrete. The raiders stayed at the doors. The Broker took a seat at the bench, folded his hands on its scarred surface, and gestured at the crate opposite.

Bog dropped onto it with a heavy *thump*. The crate groaned. He pulled Nia down beside him by the waist, settled her half against his thigh, and rested one broad hand on her hip as though she were a coat he'd set aside.

Nia stiffened. The beads chose that moment to shift. She let out a thin, tight breath through her nose and stared at the wall.

Sonya took up a position against the doorframe, arms crossed again. She was not looking at Bog. She was not looking at the Broker.

"Now, then," the Broker said, folding his hands the other way. "You wrote that you had information about the Dominion's military depot in geostationary orbit, and a proposal that might interest my colleagues."

"I do," Bog said. "But first, I'd like to know what exactly your colleagues can do."

"Ah." The Broker's smile thinned. "A reasonable question. May I?" He looked to the doorframe. "Sonya?"

For the first time, the Pale Murderess spoke. Her voice was low, even, and almost gentle.

"The rabbit," she said, "has very good posture for a pet."

The lantern swung. Nia's blood went cold.

Bog laughed, easy and bright, and squeezed her hip. "Told you. Sturdy stock."

But Nia saw Sonya's eyes stay on her neck, where they had been all along — on the kitschy bow tie and whatever it was hiding — and never move at all.

*Hold the line,* she thought. *Hold the line. Hold the line.*
