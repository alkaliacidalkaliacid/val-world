## Chapter 52: Orange Clobberess


Rin did not carry her like a prisoner. She carried her like a kitten she had found in a ditch.

The suit's left arm cradled Nia against its chest plating, the fingers curled loosely around her legs, one thumb positioned with great care so that it didn't press against anything with a bell on it. The hatch above her was open. Rin sat with her goggles pushed up into her hair and her boots swinging over the edge, steering with a pair of levers and humming something that sounded like a cartoon theme song.

"I'm Yan-Rin," she said, leaning out to look down. "Rin. My codename's Orange Clobberess, but nobody uses it."

"Why not?" Nia said. It came out hoarse. She had not intended to talk. Twelve hours of talking to Val in the dark had left her with a policy of silence, but the girl was twenty and radiant and had just thrown a truck at a lizard-man, and the policy lasted about four seconds.

Rin dropped her voice an octave, scrunched up her face into a craggy scowl, and growled, "'*Clobberess* isn't even grammatically correct.'" Then she collapsed into giggles. "That's Boss. He says I should've picked something else, but I like it! I'd paint the suit orange too, if I had paint. He says that'd make me stand out like an idiot in the middle of the woods."

"He's right," said Nia.

"I *know* he's right. That's what's so annoying."

Behind them, the Old Guards were working through the wrecked camp with quick, practiced efficiency. Two men were stripping Val rifles into a pile. The grandmother with the machine gun was sitting on an ammo crate eating the lieutenant's rations. Eight bound Val soldiers lay in a row like felled logs, still sneezing, each with a pair of silver cable loops locked around the ankles. Gorrak, the one who'd held her leash, was being dragged by four men on a tarp. He had not woken up.

Nia watched him go with a clinical eye. "What are you planning to do with them?"

"Oh, the usual." Rin said it with the casual brightness of someone discussing the weather. "Some of them'll cut a deal. Others are good for prisoner exchanges. The Dominion's Appalachian garrison is *super* flexible about that kind of thing. They'll trade you three Val for one Old Guard if you're polite about it."

"Trade."

"Yeah! Sometimes they pay in medicine."

Nia said nothing.

She had held a corridor in Baltimore for nine days. She had watched a Val colonel receive a bribe once, in the early occupation, and thought *that's the end of the dream.* Here, six years in, she was being carried through a pine forest by the nicest girl in the resistance, who was explaining the going rate on captive aliens. The Dominion's rank and file were corrupt, and so, apparently, were the people fighting them. Corruption, it seemed, was the one resource that had survived the war with its supply lines intact.

Even so, even so. A tied Val could still be dangerous. She watched one of them struggle against the cable, shoulders flexing, until the metal groaned. The silver loops held, barely. *Troublesome breed,* she thought. *Don't let them rest.*

"They'll be fine for the ride," Rin said, catching her look. "Boss rigs the loops to shock them if they pull too hard. Sneeze powder's just to slow them down."

"Where are we going?"

"The hideout. Don't worry, it's nice! Well. It's a cave. But there's soup."

*Soup.* Nia's stomach, long accustomed to a different diet, made a small and completely traitorous sound.

Ahead of them, through the pines, Darkwing walked alone. He did not look back, and he did not speak, but every few steps he lifted a hand and a gray-gloved finger touched a spot on the side of his cowl. She suspected he was listening to the forest. At the edge of her vision, under his cape, something moved against his belt: a dull gray case, scuffed at the corners.

Her toolkit.

He had picked it up from the supply stack on his way out. She had watched him do it. He had stopped, turned it over once in his hands, and then, instead of tossing it to a scavenger or logging it with the rest, tucked it under his arm as though it were a thing worth keeping.

"Rin," Nia said, very carefully. "Does your boss usually take the junk?"

"Hm? No. Boss never takes anything. He says looting makes us no better than the Val." She squinted ahead. "Huh. That's weird."

Darkwing stopped walking.

He did not turn around. He simply said, in a low, even voice that carried through the pines without effort, "Rin. Put the Huntress down gently. And bring the suit's lights down to nothing."

"Boss?"

"Now."

The pines went black. In the silence that followed, something very faint and very distant began to buzz, a thin electric rattle like a fly in a jar, coming from the south, and growing.
