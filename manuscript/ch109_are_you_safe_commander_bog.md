## Chapter 109: Are You Safe, Commander Bog?

The way up was a chimney of broken rock, and they were climbing it in single file — Rin's chemical light bobbing ahead, green and unsteady, Nia behind her picking the holds, and Bog at the rear with Priya riding his shoulder like a sack of grain. Somewhere above them was the surface. Somewhere above them were answers, and a reckoning. Nia climbed, and rehearsed the reckoning, and found it good.

Then the mountain began to growl.

It came up through the soles of her feet first — a tremor in the stone, deep and rising. Dust sifted down out of the dark above. The rumble swelled, near and nearer, no longer a sound so much as a pressure in the teeth.

"Brace!" Nia shouted, and flattened herself to the rock.

The wall beside them tore open.

Stone split with a crack that swallowed her shout whole, and through the breach they came — tentacles, countless tentacles, pouring out of the broken earth in a writhe of slick, gleaming coils. And behind them, a light. Not Rin's sickly green: a searchlight, white and piercing, an eerie beam that flooded the cavern and struck the dark out of every corner at once. Nia threw an arm across her eyes, blinking tears, her vision swimming with afterimages.

She knew that light. She knew those tentacles.

*That monster was still alive.*

Her teeth ground together. Of course it was alive. Of course it had dug its way here, of all places, now, of all times — now, when she could do precisely nothing about it. She herself remained shackled by a collar, her spirits deaf to her behind a band of Val metal. The plump Indian woman — who had wielded power enough to unmake the world, who had sunk a star into the void — was also collared, and riding Bog's shoulder half-conscious and useless. And Rin, Rin who had flown and fought in thirty feet of salvaged armour, had lost her powered suit to the dark below, and was likewise forced into a collar. Three women. Three collars. A child with a dying light, a goddess in a necklace, and the Black Huntress with her bare hands.

Whose fault was it? Well. The answer to that was obvious, wasn't it?

That lecherous, unprincipled, green invader. That total piece of trash, that moron, that complete blockhead, that brainless fool who thought and acted with his dick instead of his head — every disaster in her life for longer than she cared to count had his big green fingerprints all over it, and this one, she was quite sure, would turn out to be no different.

Something inside her settled, cold and clean.

Fine.

Her hand closed around a fragment of broken stalagmite at the chimney's edge — a jagged spear of stone, heavy as a club, sharp as a promise. She hauled it free and stepped down past Rin, putting herself between the tentacles and the others, bells chiming small and bright with every stride, and she did not care. Let them chime. She was done being protected. It had never been her style. It never would be.

The tentacles writhed — sinister, enormous, coiling through the breach in a slow obscene ballet, and the searchlight swept the cavern wall, hunting. It passed over Rin, frozen with her light shaking in her fist. It passed over Nia, and did not stop. It found Bog, at the rear, squinting up into the glare with Priya on his shoulder — and held.

The monster spoke. Flat. Literal. Courteous as a clerk.

"Are you safe, Commander Bog?"
