## Chapter 92: Good Girl

Rin cried the way she did everything: fighting it.

"Please," she said, and her smile was still on, stretched over the tears like a bandage over a wound, bright and useless. "Please. That's - that's my limit. That's really my limit, I can't - "

Her voice broke on the rest.

Bog looked down at her, considering, the way he considered weather. Then he leaned in and licked the tears off her face.

It was not a kiss. It was grooming, his tongue broad and warm, one cheek and then the other, and the sheer animal matter-of-factness of it stopped her crying like a hand over a candle flame. She hiccupped. She stared.

"There," said Bog. "Better."

And then - finally, finally - he pulled free of her, and the empty ache of it folded her in half, and he let her slide down the front of him until she landed in a heap on the stone floor, legs everywhere, suit in ruins, breathing hard.

The stone was cool against her cheek. It was the best thing she had ever felt.

"You should eat," said Bog.

Rin made a sound that was not a word.

"It will help," he said, reasonable as a doctor. "Heals the wounds. Brings the strength back." He nodded at her, spread on the floor like a dropped marionette. "You are leaking, and you are empty. This is the correct order of things. First the one, then the other."

"I am NOT - " The blush went all the way to the tips of her ears, like a sunrise. She pushed herself up on arms that shook. "I am not putting that - you can't just - that's not - "

Bog reached down and gave her bottom a squeeze through the torn suit, playful as a man testing a peach.

"Nia does it all the time," he said. "And look how healthy she is."

Rin shuddered from the scalp down.

She thought of Nia. Nia, who could carry a collapsing building. Nia, whose skin shone, who moved through the world like a banner in a high wind, fed and kept and collared and *magnificent*. Three to five times a day, every day, for months.

"...Well," Rin said, to the floor, in the voice of a girl signing a form she has not read, "I suppose I have no choice...♥"

"That's the spirit," said Bog, pleased, and settled himself. "Now. There is a way to do it. Watch."

What followed was the strangest part of the whole day, which was saying something.

There were steps.

"Down," said Bog, and because she was already on the floor this meant arranging herself: forehead to the stone, back bared, two of his fingers pressing between her shoulder blades. "Like that. Good. Now you ask."

"Ask...?"

"In the old tongue. Repeat." He said a line of archaic Val, all gravel and glottal stops, and Rin - face down on the cave floor, in the wreck of her suit - parroted it back in a mangled attempt that bore the same relation to the original as a kazoo to an organ.

"Wait," she mumbled into the stone, halfway through. "What *is* this...?"

"Custom," said Bog. "Again."

There were kisses for his toes - each green toe, because the liturgy asked for it, and Rin did it with the obedience of a girl in a dream, and did not laugh, which surprised her. Then he offered her two fingers, and she understood - upward from the toes - and licked them clean: salt, and green skin, and the day's work, each finger so thick her tongue had to travel to get around it. Bog watched with the patience of a man whose customs had never once been questioned in his hearing.

Then there was instruction, patient and exact, in the manner of a man teaching a card trick: *Hold. Now move. Slowly. Hold still - open. Show me.*

And there was the other problem, which was scale.

The first honest attempt to take him deep nearly unhinged her jaw - a bright spike of panic at the hinge of it, her eyes watering, her hands fluttering up - and Bog, without any fuss, adjusted the arrangement, as if compromise were a knob on the side of the world.

"Just the tip," he said, kindly. "We will work up to it."

So she worked at the tip of him, obedient and scarlet, and it was *clumsy* - there was no other honest word for it. Her tongue went everywhere except where tongues were presumably supposed to go. Her teeth grazed him twice, and he did not even flinch, which was somehow worse. Her jaw ached in its sockets from holding itself open around a circumference it had never been consulted about. The taste of him filled her mouth and kept filling it - salt and musk, and something green and animal underneath - and the smell of him rose off his skin and into her nose with every breath she stole around the edges.

*Stinky♥,* she thought, dazed, working away like a girl at an ice-cream cone in a heatwave, drool escaping at the corners despite her best efforts. *Are all men like this? No, wait - surely that can't be right, right?♥* Somewhere underneath the panic and the ache there was a traitorous, bubbling wish to laugh: the Boss had protected her from a great many things, but the protection had never covered *this*, and nobody had ever warned her it would smell as if a locker room and a thunderstorm had had a baby.

"Hold," said Bog, and she held, cheeks hollowed, eyes streaming. "Now move. Slowly. Good. Hold still - open. Show me." And his hand came to rest on the back of her head and stayed there - not pressing, never pressing, just stroking, slow, the way you stroke a cat that has come in out of the rain.

"Good girl," said Bog, at last. "You can swallow."

She swallowed.

"Now the thanks." Another line of old Val, shorter, and she repeated it with her mouth still tingling and her face on fire, forehead nearly to the stone, getting the glottal catch wrong in a way that made him hum with approval, as if wrong were only a local variation. "Clever girl. So devoted."

Rin stayed down a moment longer than she had to, forehead against the stone, his hand still moving in her hair. The shaking had gone out of her arms. Somewhere in her, low and warm, the hollowness of the last hour had begun to fill.

It was, she thought dimly, probably the protein. That, or the rest of it. Her thoughts went where they wanted now, loose and warm, and where they wanted was up: to Nia on the ledge, magnificent and unsuspecting. Oh dear, she thought, dreamy and too pleased with the thought: if someone *bad* like him set his sights on Nia, she was bound to be snatched away from that husband of hers...♥

Beneath her on the stone there was a small, complete map of the hour: the sweat, and the blood - already stopped, only a thin trace left, drying at the edges - and, Rin discovered with a fresh, exhausted flush, a little urine as well, leaked somewhere in the middle of it all among her other fluids and let go without her ever noticing. Bog followed her eyes, glanced at it, and shrugged, enormous and unbothered, as if a floor were a floor, and could be rinsed.
