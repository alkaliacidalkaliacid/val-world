## Chapter 35: Tickled


The tentacles tore through her costume. Or, to be more precise, they absorbed it, greedily, thread by thread, like a swarm of ants stripping a carcass. The torn scraps of her coat and the racy outfit beneath it dissolved into the cables, and Sonya was left standing in the middle of the monster's hollow, exposed and defenseless, with nothing on her but sweat and a few strips of fabric.

Then the tickling began in earnest.

Tentacle tips as fine as paintbrushes swept the hollows of her underarms. Others went for the soles of her feet, ran along the tendons of her ankles, and dug into the soft spots between her ribs. One fluttered behind her ear. Another hovered beneath her nose and drew a slow, maddening circle. They were not random. They were *learning*. Each time her body twitched, they marked the spot, adjusted their pressure, and came back to the same place, a little lighter and a little faster, with the patient precision of a machine that had never once been asked to be gentle and had been given the whole of eternity to practice.

"Hnnh… ha… *nyet*… ha…"

"Wouldn't it be in your best interest to just surrender?" the collar asked, with infuriating politeness.

*Surrender.*

That green bastard was saying something about surrender.

*That Val scum thinks Sophia Ivanova will surrender?* Her mind, scrambling for solid ground, reached for her old training and found only scraps of it. *Бледная убийца. The Pale Murderess. Alpha Group. Four hundred meters. A general's head, no flinch. Tortured by tickling? By a stupid, oversized worm? Don't make me laugh. Absolutely not. Never. Not in a thousand years. Not…*

A tentacle slid into the hollow of her knee.

"*Ah*… ah, ahahaha!?"

*No.*

"Ahahahaha, ah! *Hahaha!* Not… I won't… *absolutely not*, damn it! Absolutely… ahahahahaha!"

The portals she had been clinging to flickered, shrank, and winked out one by one, like candles in a gale. Her shoulders shook. Tears sprang to her pale eyes and ran down into her silver hair. Her heels drummed helplessly against the cable floor. A high, wheezing, perfectly human peal of laughter echoed through the hollow belly of the monster, and she could hear it bouncing off the walls and hating every note.

Outside, through the cracked viewport, the Earth was growing larger. In Bog's pod, a tinny voice said, "Ground contact in two to the third barium half-lives, Commander." Bog nodded slowly, wearing the expression of a man who had converted the figure perfectly and found it satisfactory. He had not. "Ha. Take your time."
