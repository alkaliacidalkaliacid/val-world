## Chapter 71: Counting Trees

Rin was, Nia decided somewhere around the third hour, the loudest person she had ever met, and she had once worked a rehab gym full of retired drill sergeants.

The cockpit of the powered suit was built for one and a half people if both of them were generous about elbows. Nia sat wedged in the jump seat behind Rin's shoulder in the loose overalls the nurses had found her, knees up, collar cold against her throat, while the thirty-foot machine strode down a deer trail with a motion like a drunk giraffe in a bus. Every step jingled. The bells had been muted by nothing but luck and denim, and each footfall made one of them sing, a clear, lovely, humiliating note.

"That's the nice thing about you," Rin said cheerfully, not turning around. "I always know where you are!"

"I'm sitting directly behind you."

"Yeah, but now it's *musical*."

Rin talked the way other people breathed. She talked about the suit's left knee, which had been a bulldozer's. She talked about the school-bus door, which she had welded on because "a shoulder needs a door, Nia, don't ask me why, it just does." She talked about Darkwing, in a gruff impression so exact that Nia flinched the first time. *Rin. Stop narrating. Rin. We are in a forest. Rin. The trees have ears.* Then she giggled and said the real one had never once said the trees had ears, he'd said *Val have ears*, which was a different thing.

And she talked about machines, which was where Nia began listening properly.

"I'm a bit of an expert, you know," Rin said, tapping a dial with a knuckle. "Even the boss asks me. *Rin, will it blow.* *Rin, can you make it not blow.* I'm the one who worked out how to block the signals from those ring things. Val ones, yours, everybody's. Easy." She wriggled. "I bet I can take a look at your collar *and* the toolkit. I mean, probably *both*. And Dr. Hermes! I can't wait to meet Dr. Hermes! Is it true he's a mutant? Does he have, like, a *lot* of arms?"

Nia looked at the back of her head, at the lopsided ponytail and the goggles pushed up like a second pair of eyes, and could not decide how much of it to believe. A girl who fixed machines with her bare enthusiasm and a salvaged bulldozer knee. A girl who, when Darkwing said *very good, Rin*, had visibly glowed for twenty minutes.

But Nia had watched the suit slam a Val sergeant into the dirt until he stopped moving, and she had watched a girl laugh in the middle of it. In six years Nia had not met many people who laughed in the middle of it.

*Keep your hopes up,* she told herself. *Not high. Up. Like a flag. A small, sensible flag.*

*He's dead,* said another part of her, without being asked. *Stop picturing the couch.*

"You're quiet," Rin said.

"I'm counting."

"Counting what?"

"Trees."

Rin accepted this at once, which was one of her better qualities.
