## Chapter 82: Love You

The ledge opened under the fallen suit like a trapdoor.

It happened without a sound. One moment the nine-meter machine was kneeling in a pool of cooling glass, steaming faintly, its pilot muttering at a dead dashboard, and the next the rock beneath it split along a black seam, and a column of purple aura came roaring up out of the crack and folded over the suit like a hand closing on a bug.

"*Rin!!*"

Nia's voice tore out of her. She thrashed on Bog's shoulder, kicked, clawed at his coat. The suit's arm was still reaching up out of the violet as the light closed over it. A glove of steel, fingers spread, as if it had been about to wave. Then it sank, and the fissure drew itself shut with a long slow *grind*, like a mouth chewing, and the ledge was just a ledge again, with a smear of glass where a girl had been.

Silence.

"Huh," said Bog, from somewhere beneath her. "That's a persistent one, isn't it?"

He let out a low, thoughtful growl.

Then his hands moved.

Both of them. In the same breath, without the slightest warning, the left to the plump brown curve riding his left shoulder and the right to the firm dark curve slung over his right, and he *kneaded*. Slowly. Warmly. With the huge, rough, green palms of a man who was not the husband of either woman, a man who had no business within a mile of either of those wedding rings, and who did it all with an air of tender, bottomless, almost reverent contentment.

"*Bog!!*" said Nia.

"What? Come on." He sounded wounded. "It's been a while. Let me enjoy it a little."

"A *girl just got swallowed by a mountain!*"

"I know. I'm *focusing.*"

Priya, on his other shoulder, made a small, drowsy noise like a kitten. She did not wake.

Bog gave both of them one last, thorough, considering squeeze, and then with a gentleness that made Nia want to scream into a pillow, he bent at the knees and set them down. Priya went first, laid out on a flat of warm stone with her head pillowed on his folded coat, sleeping the sleep of the very tired. Nia he lowered second, onto her own two feet, with one steadying hand on her elbow, as if he had been escorting her down the steps of an opera house.

"There you go."

She stood there. She was shaking. She could not have said whether it was fury.

"Anyway," said Bog, straightening, and rolling his stiff left shoulder with a crackle, "when Priya wakes up, tell her that her attendant is waiting 'down below,' and that she should come and find me. She'll know what that means. Probably. She's very sharp."

"Her *attendant.*"

"Me. Subordinate. It's in the paperwork." He was already backing toward the place where the fissure had been, flexing his knees, loosening his arms in great swinging circles, like a man warming up for a race. "Don't let her get possessed, okay? She's got a thing about that. And Nia?"

"*What?*"

He grinned over his shoulder. It was huge, broad, bright, unguarded, entirely without guile, the grin of a man who had never once been told that you did not say a thing like this to a woman in a collar.

"Love you!"

Then he ran.

He did not hesitate. He did not so much as slow. He charged across the ledge in four great strides, boots striking sparks, and without a pause or a glance at the dark seam in the stone, he threw himself headlong *into* the closing rift. For some reason, at the very last instant, he pinched his nose shut with two green fingers, like a boy about to dive off the high board into a swimming pool, and the last Nia saw of him was a pair of boot soles, a flapping coat, and a smell of cockroach skewers.

The crack shut behind him with a soft, wet *pop*.

The wind blew. Far down the mountain a tree creaked. The purple glow overhead dimmed, then flared, then settled to a low, sullen throb.

Nia stood alone on the ledge.

She stood there with her fists clenched at her sides and her bells hanging silent from every one of the eleven places they had been put. She listened to a woman snore. She counted her breaths, as she always did. *One. Two. Three.* She did not say anything. She did not say it back. She would not have said it back if he had held a knife to her.

She went on not saying it for quite some time.

"...Idiot," said Nia Okonkwo at last, to the empty mountain, and the word came out with a very small♥ in the middle of it, all alone, where nobody could hear.

Beside her, Priya turned over in her sleep, murmured "Arjun," and went still.
