## Chapter 91: Soon Enough

The deal was sealed, and the cavern was quiet, and Rin was still sitting on Bog's lap.

That was the part her brain kept circling back to. The collar at her throat shone a soft, steady white. The violet had gone out of the stone. Somewhere far above, presumably, there were people having a serious conversation, and down here she was perched on seven and a half feet of Val like a child on a parade float, with her feet dangling nowhere near the floor.

"So," she said brightly, to the cavern in general. "This is the part where we, uh. Discuss logistics."

"This is the staying part," said Bog.

He said it the way he said everything, mild and final, and then he began to move.

At first it was gentle. That was almost the worst of it - how gentle it was, how slow, one deep unhurried roll at a time, his enormous hands spread across her waist to hold her steady, as if she were something he was carrying across a river and did not intend to drop. Rin let out a breath she had been holding since the fissure. Her shoulders came down. A small, stupid laugh escaped her.

The blood was already there. It had been there since the deal - the thin line of it dried dark at the edges on her thigh, still bright where their bodies met and moved, the evidence of whatever had given way inside that slow roll of his hips while he asked her about children. It had not stopped him then, and feeling it shift and smear with every gentle roll, Rin understood it would not stop anything now. It was part of the record, written on her skin.

She could not stop looking at it. She had been shot at. She had been thrown through the air in thirty feet of dying armor. She had been possessed, hollowed out and worn like a glove by a thing made of faces. None of it had prepared her for carrying a mark like this into everything that followed, because of the size of the man she had just made a deal with.

Bog slowed. "Hurt?"

"You - you have to ask that♥ *now*?"

He waited. That was the thing about him, she was learning: he asked, and then he waited, solid and patient, and the waiting was its own kind of answer. Rin shook her head, furiously, because stopping would make it a thing she had chosen to stop, and she was Yan Rin, and she did not stop things.

"Keep going," she said. "Boss♥. I mean - Bog. Bog!"

"Efficient," said Bog, and the gentleness ended.

There was no other word for what followed but the one her rattling brain supplied: a jackhammer. The slow rolls became a pounding, relentless rhythm that shook her whole small body on his lap, her goggles bouncing against her forehead, her ponytail flying, every breath knocked out of her and replaced with a cry she did not remember deciding to make. The pleasure hit her in waves too big for her frame, one after another, until the cavern went white and then went away entirely -

- and came back with a jolt, Bog's hand twisting at her buttock, a smack that rang off the stone, his voice mild in her ear: "Stay with me."

She stayed. She did not have a choice about staying; that was the revelation, arriving somewhere around the third time the world went white. The Boss had spent years putting himself between her and everything. The boys at the bunker treated her like a mascot, a little sister, brought her juice and looked away from her changing. Nobody - nobody in her whole life - had simply *used* her like this, kept her, worked her like a thing that belonged on his lap, without anger, without apology, without even the courtesy of thinking it was remarkable.

It shocked her to the bone. And underneath the shock, settling into the place the possession had hollowed out, something in her filed the fact away with terrible simplicity: *master*.

When she could breathe again - hanging off his shoulder, shaking, wrung out like a rag - she made herself look around. The purple in the cracks of the cavern had backed off a little further. Emergency over. Practical time.

"Okay," she gasped, and forced the cheer back into her voice by main strength. "Okay! Purple's going down, show's over, I should - I should climb down and go look at my suit, it's probably still fixable, I just need to - "

"No," said Bog.

"Just for a bit! Ten minutes! You can watch me not run away, I'll - "

"Priya will be coming down soon enough," Bog said, with the air of a man mentioning weather, and settled her more firmly where she was, one arm across her like a seatbelt. "Stay."

He had not thought about it at all. That was clear, and somehow that was the most overwhelming part: no scheme, no threat, just the calm assumption that she would be sitting here when company arrived, because here was where she sat now.

"But she'll *see*♥ - "

"Mm," said Bog, and kissed her again, long and deep and unhurried, like a man with nowhere else in the world to be.
