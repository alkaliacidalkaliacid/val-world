# THE ASHEN DOMINION
## Bog & Nia: Raid on an Orbital Military Depot

*(Manuscript export of this session's story. Final, refined versions of each scene, in story order.)*

---

## 1. Transport

Inside a transport craft, powered by some inscrutable mechanism devised by a mad-scientist-style human supervillain, Sonya, Bog, and "Tummy-Achy Bunny" sat in an awkward silence. Well, perhaps not entirely awkward; Bog, for one, seemed quite content, kneading the thighs of the bunny.

The transport was closing in on the Val military depot, which sat in geostationary orbit.

"How are we going to slip past Val's hyperspace radar?" Nia asked. Bog shot her a look, as if to say, *Hey, that wasn't the deal!* But Nia had already concluded that their cover was blown.

Sonya didn't answer. Instead she countered with a question: "An American hero. Never fought in Eurasia."

"Bunny," Nia replied. The two glared at each other, both refusing to share any information.

Bog sighed. It seemed that, just like in comic books, supervillains and superheroes were destined to be at odds no matter the situation. Still, he decided to look on the bright side: at least the Pale Murderess's costume was quite racy.

The transport's AI announced their approach to the depot. Sonya stood and raised her hand, and a glowing disc opened inside the craft. Without a word she leaped into it, and the other two scrambled after her. For a fleeting moment they were falling through a surreal, incomprehensible landscape — then out the far side of it, into vacuum.

There was no air. Then, all at once, there was: air bled out of the other place through countless tiny discs, streaming around them, thin and freezing and barely there, but it pressed against their skin and pushed them gently onward. The little discs winked open and shut on every side, a tunnel of light — a docking tube made of portals. Nia's ears sang. She kept her mouth shut and held her breath, the way you do underwater. Its last disc opened on the far side of the depot's wall, and before they knew it, they were inside the warehouse.

---

## 2. Sector Delta

There were, in fact, several barriers of energy and matter in their path. Yet the discs Sonya opened acted as a master key, and they passed through every one of them with ease. To her, the distinction between a wall and a corridor seemed not to exist; she simply kept walking, unerringly, toward Sector Delta. Bog had no grasp of what her ability truly was. Nia, who had seen her share of clashes between superpowered individuals, was beginning to piece it together. It was an extraordinary power. If only this woman had fought alongside the heroes back in the day… Damn it. Then again, Nia reflected, she wasn't exactly the type.

They reached Sector Delta without a hitch. It was a treasure trove, or possibly a junk heap. Military supplies, or rather military relics, of the Val civilization lay scattered about, coated in dust and draped in cobwebs. Strictly speaking, the "cobwebs" were the filaments of a predatory, silicon-based slime mold, descended from a species native to some star system Bog couldn't quite place. The place looked less like a military depot than the storage room of an underfunded museum.

Nia frowned. It was in dire need of a cleanup.

Showing no sign of fatigue, Sonya turned to Bog. "Explain. What is all this?"

Bog began pointing out whatever he recognized. Unpacked firearms and artillery. Assorted vehicles. Hand grenades and defensive gear, identified by their packaging. Strings of anal beads, bells, collars, branding irons, paddles. "Unknown," he said gravely, nodding at a crate. "Unknown," he said of the next one, with equal dignity. There was even a run-of-the-mill planetary-core-destruction bomb. ("Say that again?" said Nia.) Expired beverages. "Unknown." Machine oil that was somehow still usable despite being centuries old. Obsolete uniforms. And a Collar-calibration toolkit, Class 5. ("Class 5? Is that higher or lower than Class 3? Oh well, this should do.") Among other things.

Sonya took an interest in a few items before finally pointing to a cocoon-like object in the corner, fashioned from a tangled mass of steel cables.

"Ugh…" Bog recoiled.

When Sonya asked for an explanation, he muttered, "If it's what it looks like, that's the space tentacle monster. A legend among soldiers. An autonomous weapon, developed ages ago by military scientists and modeled after an actual space-faring beast. Probably the most vicious thing in this whole warehouse."

---

## 3. Betrayal

Using her immense strength, Sonya hauled the supplies she wanted into a single pile, and last of all she set the steel-cable cocoon on top of it.

While she worked, Bog casually picked up a toolkit. Nia gripped his shoulder and whispered, "Are you really going to let her take those weapons? If that broker, or whoever he answers to, gets hold of them, they'll be used for something terrible."

Bog shrugged and whispered back, "Doesn't concern us, does it? If they help the struggle against the Dominion, shouldn't you be all for it?"

"The people those villains will use them on are human beings. Defenseless civilians."

"Civilians? There's barely a 'city' left anymore…"

Nia stared at him. "Do something."

"Wha…? Hey, come on. You should be focusing on your own freedom…" Under her unwavering gaze, Bog faltered. "Well. I suppose the tentacle monster might be a bit much," he muttered, and trudged over to Sonya.

"Hey, you might want to leave that tentacle monster behind. I hear it's completely uncontrollable. The Dominion even tried to get rid of most of them."

Sonya laughed softly. "It seems the hero does not bow to you. You bow to her."

"Look out!" Nia shouted. Bog leaped aside on instinct. A portal flashed open and snapped shut on the very spot where he had been standing a heartbeat earlier.

"Wait… let's talk about this…" But then it dawned on him. The Pale Murderess had meant to dispose of these two nuisances, neatly and quietly, right here in the warehouse. She had meant to from the very beginning.

---

## 4. Portals

"What in Val's name?" Bog bellowed, scrambling backward on all fours.

"She opens portals! Whatever goes into one comes out of another!" Nia shouted.

Sonya's pale eyes flickered. Her gaze seemed to say, *I knew it. Even with that collar on, you're a seasoned hero.* The discs that had swallowed the bells' chimes winked out, and Nia's body rang again, bright and clear, through the vast warehouse.

Another disc flared into being beside Bog's ear and vanished. Another opened beneath his boots, and he jumped before he'd finished thinking about it. A fourth appeared at the level of his belly and snapped shut as he twisted away, so close that the air it displaced tugged at his uniform. He kept stumbling backward, hugging the toolkit to his chest, cursing in three dialects.

"Portals that move things from one place to another," he panted. "How scary can that be? You'd think the worst you could do is, I don't know, rearrange somebody's furniture…"

A crate toppled from the stack beside him, struck by his flailing elbow. It slid across the floor toward a disc that had just opened there. The front half of the crate sank into the glowing circle, and the disc snapped shut.

The crate fell in two pieces. The cut was so clean it looked polished, and a puff of fine powder trickled from the sliced contents.

Bog stared at it. "Okay," he said weakly. "I take it back."

*Not teleportation,* Nia thought, watching Sonya's hands. *Doorways. And she can close them whenever she likes, no matter what is halfway through.* She had fought storms and walking furnaces, but never anyone whose weapon was an edge of nothing that could be drawn through anything.

"Bog! Move left, then drop!" she shouted.

He obeyed without an argument, which surprised both of them. A pair of discs shot open where his head and chest had been, and a second later two matching exits flashed out of the air behind him. Sonya had sent her own blow through the first pair and was redirecting it. Whatever she had hurled at him gouged a trench in the floor where he'd been lying. He rolled aside, wheezing.

"She's aiming at you," Nia called. "I'm collared. She knows I can't do anything. You're the one with the muscle!"

"I have muscle! I have *tremendous* muscle! I don't have any *plan*!"

"Then charge her! Get close! The portals need space around her to open. She can't fill a room with them without straining!"

*Two or three at a time,* Nia added to herself. She'd been counting since the first strike, and the number never climbed beyond four. Sonya's temple was glistening, and she kept her left foot planted when she shifted her weight, as if she were bracing against a headache. *Size, count, and stamina. The more she opens, the more she pays.* She hopes that's the case.

Bog charged. For a Val with his weight and size it was an alarming sight, and a lesser human might have fled the warehouse in terror. Sonya stepped aside without hurry. A disc yawned in the floor ahead of him, and he hurdled it at the last instant, trailing curses. A second blossomed at the height of his throat. He ducked, and the toolkit slipped from his grip.

It skidded across the floor and stopped at Nia's feet.

Both of them looked at it.

"Oh, wonderful," Bog groaned.

Sonya's eyes dropped to the toolkit, then lifted to Nia's collar. For the first time she looked amused. "Collar-calibration," she said softly. "You are the reason he came."

"He came for his own reasons," Nia said.

She snatched up the case and hugged it to her stomach. The bells shivered, and she felt the cold weight of the collar settle against her throat. She wished, for one insane second, for the strength to break the woman in half. A disc opened just beyond her left foot, a warning, not a strike, and she stood very still.

"Put it on the ground," Sonya said. "Both of you. Then we will talk."

"I'd love to," Bog said from somewhere behind a tower of crates. His voice was brittle with false cheer. "But somehow I doubt you meant 'talk.'"

## 5. The Stun Pistol

Behind the tower of crates, Bog's hand closed around something heavy and cold. He pulled it out of a split carton: a long-barreled Dominion sidearm, still wrapped in a film of centuries-old grease. The grip was warm. A pale blue indicator ticked on along the receiver, and a low whine climbed in the stock.

"Charged," he whispered, delighted. "It's *charged*. Thing's been sitting here since before my grandfather was cradled, and it's still ready to fire."

He thumbed the selector without looking, because even a low-ranking soldier knew the basics. Lethal output would punch a hole through a bulkhead. The lowest setting made the target twitch and sleep. He shrugged and chose the second.

He rose from behind the crates and fired.

A ribbon of blue light cracked across the warehouse. Sonya did not even raise her hand. A disc blossomed in front of her, and the stun wave poured into it like water into a drain.

"Heh," Bog said. "Got her."

Another disc opened at his left, at the level of his shoulder.

The blue ribbon came out of it.

He threw himself sideways, but not quite far enough. The edge of the beam brushed his left shoulder, and the whole arm went numb, dead weight dangling from his body. The gun clattered to the floor. He hit the deck shoulder-first and slid, grunting, into a stack of armor lockers.

"Holy Val," he wheezed. "Could it be…?"

Could it be that she had been doing this since the beginning?

Sonya stepped into a disc on the floor and was gone.

She emerged from another, twenty feet to his right, her long coat swinging out behind her like a battle flag. Her face was calm. Her hand was already lifted, and as it came down, six discs opened in a ring around him, hovering at head, chest, hips, and knees.

"Damn, Six!" Nia shouted.

"Whoa!?"

He didn't stop to think about it. He pushed off the lockers with his one good arm and bolted, shoes skidding on the grimy floor. A disc snapped shut where his neck had been. Another bit off the corner of a locker; the metal fell in neat slabs. A third opened beneath his boot and closed on nothing. Bog yelped and ran faster, with the ungainly speed of something very large trying to be very small.

On the far side of the hall, Nia watched, the toolkit tight against her stomach and the bells silent for once, because she had forgotten to breathe.

*Six,* she thought. *She's holding six.*

She had counted four before. *Four was her ceiling when she wanted precision, or when she needed to keep the sphere up around us. Now she's dropped the sphere.* Nia's eyes went to the woman's throat, where a pulse beat hard. To her temple, slick with sweat. To the way Sonya's shoulders had tightened. *She isn't breaking the limit. She's spending everything she saved, on the one man who made the mistake of pulling a gun.*

*And the sooner he's down, the sooner she turns around.*

Nia's heart hammered against her collar.

"Bog!" she shouted. "Don't run in a line! Zigzag! She can only fire where she can see you!"

"I'm *zigzagging*!" he howled, in a voice cracking at the top. "It's just *very* hard to do while screaming!"

Sonya's lips twitched. It wasn't quite a smile.

Behind her, hidden behind the tangled bulk of the steel-cable cocoon, something shifted.

## 6. The Cocoon Awakens

Several small discs hovered around Sonya's face, each one opening onto a different corner of the warehouse. Like the mirrors on a car, they erased her blind spots, and every angle of the hall lay open to her at once. Bog's lunge toward the lockers, the toolkit in Nia's arms, the drones still far down the aisles: she saw it all without turning her head.

A small field gun on a nearby pallet winked out of existence. It reappeared directly above Bog's head and dropped.

"Gah!" He threw himself sideways. The gun struck the floor where he'd been standing and gouged a crater in the plating, and the shock of it rattled his teeth. He lay there for a second, staring at the ceiling, his good arm flung out. "Okay. That's the second time she's tried to kill me with *furniture*."

Nia gritted her teeth. *Damn it.* If it weren't for this collar, she could have torn the woman off her feet by now. The old strength sat somewhere beneath her skin, heavy and sullen, behind a wall that would not give. She could feel it answer her like a limb that had fallen asleep: there, but deaf.

Then the cocoon stirred.

It started as a tremor in the tangled cables, a wet, grinding creak that ran through the stack of supplies piled beneath it. A thread of steel uncoiled. Then another. Then, with a shriek of tearing metal, the whole cocoon unfolded like a flower, and a mass of cable-thick tentacles lashed out across the hall.

Sonya was already moving. She slid into a disc, and the tentacles passed through the place where she'd stood. She emerged forty feet away, coat flaring, and the tentacles wheeled after her without hesitation.

"It's targeting her," Nia breathed. "The strongest non-Val in the room."

*That's what it's built for.*

Sonya understood it too. She opened a wall of discs in front of her, and the first wave of tentacles poured in. They came out the far side as stumps, severed clean, writhing in a heap on the floor. She had done it with ease, and for an instant she looked almost bored.

Then the stumps twitched, and new cable grew from the wounds, thick and glistening, and the creature surged forward on its regrown limbs as though nothing had happened.

"Regeneration," Sonya muttered. Her eyes narrowed.

The monster seemed to calculate. It raked the floor with a tentacle and tore up a long strip of plating, and the metal flowed into its body, rippling along the cables like a swallowed meal. It grew. It grew again, bolting more floor into itself, and the thing that had been a bundle the size of a van became a heaving, shrieking mass the size of a bus. It charged her with a ferocity that rattled the crates on their stacks.

That was when the alarms went off.

A howl tore through the warehouse, a flat metallic wail that pounded at the walls. Red light flooded the aisles. Somewhere in the dark, doors rumbled open. Drones swarmed out from behind the stacks in loose, humming flocks. Heavy security robots rolled into the intersections, their weapons already glowing. And behind them, boots: the heavy, ordered tread of Val soldiers, shouting in their harsh native tongue.

Sonya spat out a string of Russian that made Bog's ears burn, even without a translator.

"Ah," Bog said from the floor. He propped himself on his good elbow and surveyed the carnage: the alarm, the monster, the drones, the line of armored soldiers at the end of the aisle. He grinned. "This. This *chaos*. It's all going according to plan."

*Is that so?* Nia thought, watching him. She hugged the toolkit tighter against her stomach, and the bells gave one small, helpless chime.

---

## 7. Offense and Escape

Sonya stopped holding anything back.

Every disc she had been keeping up for stealth, the dinner-plate mirrors, the thin film of the sphere, the silent shells around the bunny's bells, flared outward at once and turned to the work of killing. The warehouse became a machine for her. Drones swooped in, found a portal opening ahead of them, and came out of another one at full speed, slamming into walls and ceiling girders and each other in clouds of sparks. A heavy security robot rolled into the aisle and its gun arm vanished mid-swing, reappearing in the middle of its own chest. Soldiers charged around a corner and were simply gone. No scream, no body, no echo, only boots on a floor that was suddenly empty. Beams and slugs fired in her direction went in at one point and came out at another, ricocheting off at wild angles through stacks of crates, banks of lockers, and the glass of the observation booth far overhead.

*Spending everything,* Nia thought, flattened against a pillar with the toolkit crushed against her stomach. *She's not even trying to be precise anymore.*

The monster was the only thing that mattered now, and Sonya knew it. A barrage of portals converged on the thing, dozens of them, winking open along its flanks and snapping shut again, and bite-sized chunks of cable and plating fell away in flurries. It didn't seem to care. It tore the floor up in great sheets and swallowed them. It plucked rifles out of dead soldiers' hands and drew them into its mass, and the weapons sank into the cables with a sound like a wet gulp. Every wound closed behind the blow that made it. Every tentacle she cut was replaced by two. The soldiers and drones were still shooting at her, and she let them. Every beam, every burst aimed at the pale woman went into a disc and came out along the monster's flanks, so that half the depot's fire was pouring into the creature — and she was the one aiming it. The wounds it made closed as fast as the wounds she made.

Sonya opened a single enormous disc, wider than any she'd made all night, and drew a line straight through the monster's middle. The creature split nearly in half. For one second its two halves hung there, shuddering, and Nia's heart leapt.

Then the halves reached for each other and knitted together, as if she'd cut through fog.

"No core," Sonya hissed, and wiped the sweat from her eyes with the back of one wrist. She wasn't smiling now. "Nothing to kill."

A tentacle the thickness of a pylon smashed into the floor beside her, and she slid into a disc and out again, breathing hard. Nia saw her left hand tremble before she hid it in her coat.

Something warm and heavy slammed into her shoulder.

"Gah!" She nearly dropped the case. It was Bog, crawling the last yard on his good elbow and one knee, his left arm dangling like a rope, his uniform torn at the hip. He reached her pillar, wheezed, and sat down against it with the satisfied air of a man arriving at a restaurant.

"Hi," he said.

"Where's your gun?"

"Out of charge." He gave it a moment's thought. "Or I dropped it. Anyway." He peered around the pillar at the thrashing mass, the screaming alarms, and the pale woman at the heart of it all, who was fighting the biggest battle of her life without looking back. Another tentacle sailed across the hall and exploded a stack of crates. A hail of splintered wood pattered down onto the plating.

"Uh," Bog said. "Tell you what. Let's look for an escape pod."

## 8. The Pods

"Um…" Nia began.

"Look, now that things have gotten this far, the Dominion will deal with that thing. Probably." Bog was already struggling to his feet, wincing as his dead arm swung against his hip. "And we can't do any 'hero work' with you in that collar anyway. Come on, move! Before the pod gets eaten by the monster, or wiped out by that psycho!"

"Bog, I can walk…"

"You can't run in bells!"

Before she could argue, he scooped her up with his one good arm, hooked the numb one under her knees like a hammock, and bolted. Nia clutched the toolkit to her chest and clenched her jaw as the world bounced past. Every stride set her bells jingling, a bright, ridiculous music, and the alarms swallowed it whole.

"Put me down!"

"Later!"

They ducked through a gap in a wall of crates, skidded around a corner, and vaulted a smoking drone. By some miracle, the glowing arch of an emergency bay appeared at the end of an aisle, and inside it a row of squat, bullet-shaped escape pods sat in their cradles, hatches hanging open.

Most of them were already gone — not launched, eaten. Tentacles were coiled thick around the cradles, and two pods were half sunk into the creature's glistening mass, drawn in like swallowed meals. But one pod still sat untouched in its cradle, hatch open and waiting.

"Ha! Told you!" Bog crowed.

Then a soldier stepped out from behind it.

He was a young one, pale green, with his helmet askew and a rifle slung across his chest. He had clearly had the same idea. He turned, saw Bog charging toward him with a half-naked human in his arms, and his eyes went wide with the particular outrage of a man who had done everything right and was about to be robbed.

"Hey! That's…"

A tentacle lashed across the bay ahead of the creature's advance — a blind, sweeping thing, part of the thrash of its coming — and caught him in the ribs as he stepped into its arc.

He rose into the air like a cork out of a bottle. A long, thin, mournful wail trailed behind him as he sailed across the warehouse, over a stack of crates, and out of sight. Somewhere in the distance, something big fell over.

Bog skidded to a halt. Nia twisted in his arms.

"Is it attacking a Val?"

"Legend says..." Bog said. "It's just a bit clumsy."

The creature was already closing in. It tore across the bay on a carpet of ripped-up flooring, thick as a freight train now, its cables woven into a huge shuddering mass, and a dozen tentacles stretched toward the untouched pod like fingers about to crush a nut.

"It'll swallow it!" Nia shouted.

"I know!" Bog dropped her unceremoniously onto her feet and leaped for the hatch. "Hold on, hold on, I've done this drill in training, it's just a couple of switches…" He flung himself at the open hatch, his good hand reaching for the lever inside.

A disc opened.

It was enormous, and it swallowed the pod whole, hatch and all, an instant before he reached it. His lunge carried him through the space where it had been, and he landed hard on empty plating — a clean circle of bare metal that smoked faintly at the edges.

Bog was sitting in the middle of it.

He blinked up at the ceiling. Then he turned his head slowly toward the aisle.

Sonya stood at the mouth of it, gasping for air. Her sweat-soaked hair clung to her forehead, her long coat hung in tatters, and the racy costume beneath it had been torn to a degree that went well beyond unnecessary. A trickle of blood ran from one nostril. In the red flashing light she looked like something dragged out of a furnace and not at all pleased about it.

She let out a stream of Russian, low and fast and vicious. Bog didn't understand a word of it. He understood the tone perfectly.

She glared daggers at him.

Bog let out a long, heavy sigh.

## 9. Separation

"Тебя убью!" Sonya screamed.

"Got that part," Bog said.

A disc flared open above his head, wide enough to drop something heavy through. He caught the glint of it, a sliver of an overhead gantry lined up with the portal's mouth, and had just enough time to think, *Oh, come on…*

A tentacle hit her from the side.

It struck with a crack like a whip. The portal collapsed, and the girder that had been poised above Bog's skull dropped three feet and thudded harmlessly to the floor. Sonya went tumbling, rolled, and brought up another disc in a single motion. The tentacle was severed at the root and fell in twitching coils. She stepped into a disc and was gone.

She reappeared twenty feet away, and a second tentacle had been waiting. It seized her round the waist and slammed her onto the deck with a sound that shook dust from the ceiling.

Bog watched with his head tilted and his arm hanging like a wet rag.

"Aren't the two of you tired of this yet?" he muttered.

He pushed himself to his feet and promptly tripped over something metal. He caught himself on a crate, swore, and looked down. A collar lay in the rubble, a spare, unlocked, its dull gray band still gleaming with factory oil. He had no use for it just now. He picked it up anyway, because it was lying there, and hooked it onto his belt with the vague thought that it might come in handy.

Then he looked up and spotted the pod.

It sat at the far end of the bay, half-hidden behind a toppled rack of armored lockers: squat, bullet-shaped, its status light blinking green. The pod from the cradle row. The one that had vanished in front of his eyes. Its hatch was shut now, and sealed — she had tucked it away here, out of the monster's reach, for herself. Bog stared at it, then at the woman fighting for her life in the middle of the hall.

"I *knew* it," he said, almost admiring. "You were saving that for yourself, weren't you?"

He ran. Nia was still on her feet by the pillar, and he scooped her up with his one working arm and carried her the whole way. She let out a startled sound and the bells rang. He hauled the hatch open with his shoulder and tossed her inside, toolkit and all.

"Bog!"

"This'll do. Look, it's powering up."

The console flickered. A soft whine climbed in the walls, and the seat harnesses snapped across her chest on their own. Nia twisted around to look at him through the closing gap, the red alarm light striping his face.

"What about you?"

"Take the toolkit to Dr. Hermes," Bog said. "At the Free Clinic's Appalachian branch. You know the place."

"*Bog…*"

He slammed the hatch.

The locks engaged with a heavy click. Through the little round viewport she saw him thump the outside of the pod twice with his good fist, like a man patting a horse, and turn away before she could say anything else. There was no look back, no parting speech. The Val had no word for self-sacrifice, and Bog would have blinked at the human one. He had sent the important cargo on its way; his own pod was still out there, and watching hers launch would not bring it any closer.

The pod shuddered and began to slide.

Bog was already sprinting toward the next one.

It sat in a neighboring bay, hatch ajar, a smaller, battered model with scorch marks along its side. He dove in headfirst, scrabbling for the lever, and had just gotten the hatch half-closed when the world went dark around him.

A tentacle the width of a tree trunk wrapped around the pod and *squeezed*.

"Seriously…?"

The pod spun. The walls groaned. Through the viewport he saw cables sliding past like the walls of a throat, glistening and coiling, and then he was inside the creature, swallowed whole in a dark, wet world of grinding metal.

For a moment there was only the roaring in his ears and the smell of burnt oil.

Then the whole world lurched.

It wasn't the creature. It was the warehouse. A tremor ran through the floor, up the bulkheads, into the monster, and into the little pod caught in its belly, a deep, boneshaking rumble like a mountain deciding to move. Bog braced his boots against the console and grabbed a strap with his good hand. Alarms screamed overhead.

"I don't know what's happening," he said to nobody, "but I've got a really, *really* bad feeling about this."

He was right.

In the ruined depot, Sonya stood over the wreck of her plan with blood on her chin and every nerve in her body burning, and she did the last thing she had left in her. A hundred discs bloomed around the monster at once, every one she could hold, and snapped shut in a single blinding instant.

The cut was perfect. It ran through the deck, the ribs of the hull, and the gantries holding the creature's weight, and it severed the thing from the half-destroyed warehouse like a splinter pulled from a thumb. For a moment the whole mass hung in the open dark of space, a bloated knot of steel cables the size of a barge with a tiny pod in its stomach, flailing its limbs at nothing.

Then the run-of-the-mill bomb went off.

It did not do what it said on the casing — centuries in a dusty storehouse had seen to that — but what it did was more than enough. The blast burned away every part of the creature still tangled in the depot's bones, and kicked what was left, the great severed knot of it, out of the dark and Earthward.

It fell, tumbling end over end, trailing fire and wreckage and a faint wail of tearing metal, as the blue curve of the Earth rose up to meet it.

## 10. Freefall

Bog sat in the dark, wedged between a dead console and a groaning bulkhead, and decided the thing to do was to be calm.

*All right. Let's think this through.*

*Situation: an Earth-side supervillain got into a scuffle with one of the Dominion's space monsters, and now I'm falling toward the planet inside the monster's stomach. Simple enough. So, how do I get out of this alive?*

Nothing came to mind.

*Maybe the Russian witch will have a sudden change of heart and come to save me.* He snorted. *Not that she could. And she hates me, anyway.*

The pod shuddered. Through the cracked viewport, the darkness outside had begun to take on a faint, sickly orange glow. Bog gritted his teeth.

*Right now we're still high up, and falling slow. The heat's barely started. Which means…* He frowned. *Wait. Hold on. There's something here. Think, Bog. Think.*

*If the supervillain won't play along, what about the monster? It's technically ours. It's Dominion property. A weapon, built for soldiers.*

He cleared his throat and raised his good hand, as though he were about to address a platoon.

"Hello? Hello! This is Bog, soldier of the Val Dominion! Private Monster, listen to my orders!"

The wall answered with a groan.

The pod was beginning to buckle. The tentacles outside tightened their grip, and the hull creaked and popped like an old ship in a storm.

"Whoa, hey now!"

A seam split along the ceiling. Cold air hissed in, bitter and stinking of ozone. And a single steel tentacle thrust through the crumpled plating, feeling blindly around the cabin like a fisherman's hook.

"Don't attack me!" Bog scrambled backward on his heels. "I'm your master! Holy Val, you're just as disobedient as Nia!"

The tentacle swung through the air, found the arm of the seat, and tore it out by the roots. Then it swept down and brushed against his leg.

"Gah! Get back! Seriously, stay away! Attacking Val is the superhero's job! You're on *our* side! When did you switch sides, you idiot?"

In a state of panic, he cycled through his usual, makeshift ideas: "Same as Nia," "superhero," "switch sides."

At that time, his hand drifted down to his belt and bumped something cold.

The collar.

He looked at it. It hung there, gray and oily, the spare he'd picked up from the rubble for no good reason.

*A collar suppresses a superpowered creature. Cuts off whatever drives it. Every collar in the Dominion is equipped with a control system that nobody can explain, and that obeys the Val.* His mind raced. *A monster that's been dormant for centuries, in a depot full of collars and calibration kits and prototype suppressors…*

It was a crazy, insane idea.

He was out of options.

"Oh, to hell with it."

Bog lunged. The tentacle was whipping past his shoulder, and he caught it behind the thick cable joint with his good hand and slapped the collar around it with a snap. The lock closed. A pale blue light pulsed along the band.

"Hahaha!" He was laughing, and he didn't know why. His face was running with sweat. "Suits you perfectly, you moron! Hahahahaha!"

*What on earth am I doing?*

The tentacle froze.

All of it froze, the whole writhing mass outside, each tentacle going rigid at the same instant. The pod stopped creaking. In the sudden silence he could hear only the thin shriek of air against metal and the pounding of his own pulse.

Then a flat, mechanical voice rang through the pod, from the collar, or from the walls, or from somewhere deep inside the thing that had swallowed him.

"General control system engaged. Awaiting orders, Commander."

Bog's laughter died in his throat.

"…Wait," he said slowly. "Who just spoke?"

---

## 11. Commander

"A 'general control system'? This collar?"

"Yes, Commander."

"Never heard of it. This thing's supposed to weaken superheroes."

"Please define 'superhero.'"

"Uh… never mind."

Bog took a long, slow breath. It occurred to him that the weapons the Dominion was waving around these days might have been built for entirely different purposes in the first place. He chewed on that for a fleeting moment, then let it go. The Val, and the lower ranks especially, never read the manuals. They never wondered what a piece of equipment had originally been designed for or what philosophy lay behind it. In the field, established custom was all that mattered, and the custom was: *it works, so it works.*

"For now, just follow my orders."

"Yes, Commander."

"Land us safely on Earth. On this planet."

"Yes, Commander."

Outside the cracked viewport, the orange glow had deepened to a furnace red. The pod rattled so hard that Bog's teeth clicked together, but the tentacles that had been crushing the hull had gone slack and now wrapped around it like a cradle. The creature, he realized, was holding them. Somewhere deep in its belly something began to hum, and the tumble slowed, then steadied into a long, stately fall.

"Good. Good. Wonderful." He wiped his face with his sleeve. "Now what about the supervillain? The human woman you were fighting?"

"Engagement is ongoing."

"*Seriously?* We're in freefall!"

"Report, Commander. The hostile activated an emergency charge emplaced to destroy this unit. This unit was not destroyed. She has teleported into an escape pod absorbed by this unit, and maintains a habitable environment within it. She continues her acts of destruction."

Somewhere beneath his feet, something exploded. The whole mass shuddered. A thin howl of tearing cable rose through the walls, followed by the long, wet rip of a limb being cut at the root.

Bog went pale green to the tips of his ears.

"Oh no. Oh no, oh no, oh no. *What do I do?*"

"In approximately one Carbon-11 half-life," the voice said calmly, "we will gain the upper hand and be able to neutralize her."

He looked at the glowing red viewport, at the planet swelling behind it, and at the faint trembling in the floor as another cut tore through the creature's guts. He pressed his good hand to his forehead. "Seriously…"

---

## 12. A Different Way

He frowned. He had the oddest sense that he had just been outvoted by himself.

Another tremor rolled through the creature, and a thin scream of cable rose from somewhere below. He cleared his throat and looked up at the collar, which was still glowing a faint pulsing blue.

"Hey. When you say 'neutralize,' what do you mean, exactly?"

"To cease the functions of the life form," the monster said.

"So, kill her," Bog muttered.

"Affirmative."

"Right. Right." He rubbed his chin with his good hand, and the silence stretched out around them, filled with the groan of metal and the roar of air against the hull. The orange light from the viewport flickered across his face. "Hmm."

Then he began to grin.

It started small, at one corner of his wide, blunt mouth, and spread until his sharp teeth showed in the red glow. It was the grin of a man who had just remembered that he was, in fact, a soldier of the Val Dominion, with a heavy weapon at his command and all the time in the world.

"Listen. I'll show you a different way," he said. "A much more entertaining one."

"Understood, Commander."

Far below them, in the gutted heart of the monster, Sonya's portals were still flickering. And the thing that had been trying to kill her grew very quiet.

---

## 13. Sonya's Limit

Sonya was reaching her limit.

Her strength, at least, was still intact. A single punch or kick could send a weapon, Earth-made or Val-made, flying ten meters, and the ordinary handgun rounds the soldiers had fired earlier hadn't so much as scratched her. But her real weapon had been the portals, and against this thing the portals were useless. She could sever the tentacles as they came, but they grew back almost instantly. She could slip through a disc to escape, but she'd only come out in the hollow of the creature's own body, among more cables. There was no heart to cut out, no brain to destroy, no core at all. Her temples throbbed. Her vision had begun to swim at the edges. Blood from her nose had dried to a crust on her lip.

She spat a curse in Russian.

"Hello, hello! Can you hear me, Pale Murderess? It's me, your business partner, Bog."

Sonya froze.

The voice had come from a tentacle. It hung in the air before her, swaying gently, and the sound issued from a gray band clamped around its middle, an awful, cheerful, gravelly voice she had last heard whining about a missing toolkit.

Her face tightened with rage.

"If you agree to a truce, I'll call off the attack."

She rejected the offer outright, with a word that would have made a Val drill sergeant blush.

"Good grief. I figured as much." The tentacle gave an almost human shrug. "Well, I'll have to resort to force, then. Don't worry, I won't kill you. I've decided to have this creature fill a certain role instead. The kind you used to see promised on the covers of old Earth magazines."

*What?*

Sonya blinked. She had never read an old American science-fiction magazine in her life. She did not understand a word of this.

Something cold coiled around her ankle.

She cut it off with a disc. Another tentacle wound itself around her instead. She cut that one too, and a third took its place, and a fourth, and the discs she opened were smaller each time, and slower. Her breath came in hard, shallow bursts. *Don't think. Hold the sphere. Cut. Cut. Cut.* But the limb she'd just severed was already sprouting again, and she couldn't keep this up. Her body was burning through what little she had left.

A tentacle looped around her foot a fifth time. This time she didn't cut it.

She didn't have the strength.

It tightened, almost gently, and the tip slid along the top of her foot, tracing the tendons one by one, light as a feather. Then it did it again.

Sonya's eye twitched.

"What are you…"

The tip drew a slow, careful line down the arch of her foot, and then another across the sole, and then all of them at once.

A sound escaped her. A strangled, high, entirely undignified squeak.

"*Ahh…!*"

Her hand flew to her mouth. She clamped her lips shut. She was a former operative of the FSB's Alpha Group, an assassin who had put a bullet through a general's head at four hundred meters, a woman who had never once laughed in the presence of an enemy. The tentacle wriggled harder. A second one joined it, and a third slid under her coat and found her ribs.

Her whole body convulsed.

"Hahh… no… *nyet*… ha… *stop!*"

"Entertaining?" Bog's voice asked, from the collar.

---

## 14. Tickled

The tentacles tore through her costume. Or, to be more precise, they absorbed it, greedily, thread by thread, like a swarm of ants stripping a carcass. The torn scraps of her coat and the racy outfit beneath it dissolved into the cables, and Sonya was left standing in the middle of the monster's hollow, exposed and defenseless, with nothing on her but sweat and a few strips of fabric.

Then the tickling began in earnest.

Tentacle tips as fine as paintbrushes swept the hollows of her underarms. Others went for the soles of her feet, ran along the tendons of her ankles, and dug into the soft spots between her ribs. One fluttered behind her ear. Another hovered beneath her nose and drew a slow, maddening circle. They were not random. They were *learning*. Each time her body twitched, they marked the spot, adjusted their pressure, and came back to the same place, a little lighter and a little faster, with the patient precision of a machine that had never once been asked to be gentle and had been given the whole of eternity to practice.

"Hnnh… ha… *nyet*… ha…"

"Wouldn't it be in your best interest to just surrender?" the collar asked, with infuriating politeness.

*Surrender.*

That green bastard was saying something about surrender.

*That Val scum thinks Sophia Ivanova will surrender?* Her mind, scrambling for solid ground, reached for her old training and found only scraps of it. *Бледная убийца. The Pale Murderess. Alpha Group. Four hundred meters. A general's head, no flinch. Tortured by tickling? By a stupid, oversized worm? Don't make me laugh. Absolutely not. Never. Not in a thousand years. Not…*

A tentacle slid into the hollow of her knee.

"*Ah*… ah, ahahaha!?"

*No.*

"Ahahahaha, ah! *Hahaha!* Not… I won't… *absolutely not*, damn it! Absolutely… ahahahahaha!"

The portals she had been clinging to flickered, shrank, and winked out one by one, like candles in a gale. Her shoulders shook. Tears sprang to her pale eyes and ran down into her silver hair. Her heels drummed helplessly against the cable floor. A high, wheezing, perfectly human peal of laughter echoed through the hollow belly of the monster, and she could hear it bouncing off the walls and hating every note.

Outside, through the cracked viewport, the Earth was growing larger. In Bog's pod, a tinny voice said, "Ground contact in two to the third barium half-lives, Commander." Bog nodded slowly, wearing the expression of a man who had converted the figure perfectly and found it satisfactory. He had not. "Ha. Take your time."

---

## 15. Rocked

At Bog's command, the tentacles changed their tempo.

They tickled, stroked, kneaded, and rocked her, slow and rolling, the way one gentles a skittish horse. Rows of small suckers brushed her skin and let go with soft, wet smacks, leaving a trail of damp kisses down her ribs, across her shoulders, behind her knees. Whenever one found a spot that made her flinch, the others converged on it at once, circling and teasing with tireless patience, until the flinch became a shudder.

Between the bursts of laughter, a few sounds slipped out that she had never intended to make: breathy, broken, stifled the instant she heard them. Her back arched off the cable floor without her permission.

*Val.* Even through the fog, a cold thread of thought held on. *They really do know how to break a human woman. Every one of them, every rank.* But she was the Pale Murderess. She was not some collared American heroine who had let a green brute lead her around on a string. When her strength came back, when the portals came back, she would take this thing apart one cable at a time, and then she would take *him*…

A tentacle slid along her flank, and her body bucked.

"Hah… ah… *ahahaha!* No…"

Her thoughts scattered like startled birds. She curled, uncurled, thrashed, and the tentacles followed every motion and lost nothing. She laughed until her breath ran out and then laughed without breath, mouth open, a thin wheeze of sound. Tears streaked her face and mixed with sweat and spit and the rest of the mess of it, and somewhere in the chaos her bladder gave out too. She registered the shame of it distantly, like a pain in another person's body. The tickling did not stop. It never stopped. It had no reason to.

And her powers did not return. She could feel them, a taut ember under her sternum, but every time she tried to gather them the next wave hit and the thread snapped. There was no way to hold a portal open while laughing herself inside out.

Outside, through the cracked viewport of the little pod wedged in the creature's gut, the glow of re-entry had turned the horizon orange. Bog lay back in his seat with his numb arm across his chest, listening to the distant shrieks of laughter with a look of deep and unbothered satisfaction.

"See?" he said to the collar. "Much better than killing her."

"Affirmative, Commander," said the voice. "Ground contact in two to the second barium half-lives."

Bog folded his good hand behind his head and sighed. A long way down, a pod carrying a toolkit and a very angry woman in bunny ears was falling through the same sky. He pictured her face when she found out how it had all ended, and found that he could not stop smiling.

---

## 16. The Breaking Point

Sonya fought.

She kept fighting long after the laughter had hollowed her out. She was a warrior born of the Родина, and her body was a weapon that had been broken, rebuilt, and tempered by men who did not believe in surrender. She clawed at the cables. She bit down on the inside of her cheek until she tasted blood. She dug her heels into the floor and tried, again and again, to reach the ember beneath her sternum.

It did not matter. The tentacles had learned her too well.

They found their way past every defense she had left. They came as fine as threads, so fine she did not realize what was happening until the first one had already slipped inside her, and by then the second had followed. Inside, the threads twined together and fused, and they thickened as they went, slow and patient and thorough. Nothing about it was cruel. That was the worst part. It was *efficient*, a procedure carried out by a machine that had been told the goal was *entertaining*.

They drew out and carried away whatever waste her body held, and in its place sent a warm, thrumming rush of nutrients that made her limbs go heavy and her skull go light. Other tentacles worked the grime from her skin in long, slow strokes, over her shoulders, down her spine, behind her ears, and they never stopped tickling her. The tickling underlay everything, a bright, dizzying current that no thought could cut through.

A stifled laugh broke in her throat. It tumbled into something else halfway out, a high, ragged note that she did not recognize as her own voice.

Her back bowed. The world went white.

For an instant there was nothing at all, no cold, no cable, no collar-voiced Val bastard in her ears. Then the blackness came, soft and absolute, and she slid into it gratefully.

It did not last.

She woke to the same hollow, the same slow rocking, the same impossible patience, and a tentacle already tracing the arch of her foot as if it had only paused for her to catch up. A sound escaped her before she could stop it. Her hands closed on empty air.

*No,* she thought, very faintly. *Not again. I won't…*

The second time, she did not even have the strength to be ashamed.

---

In the pod wedged in the creature's belly, Bog had begun to hum. The laughter echoing through the walls had grown quieter and stranger, and he wasn't sure whether it was still laughter. He tilted his head, frowned, and decided it was none of his business.

"How long?" he asked.

"Ground contact in two to the first barium half-lives, Commander," the voice said.

"Is she still alive?"

"Affirmative. Vital signs elevated but stable."

"Good." Bog stretched his good arm behind his head and watched the glowing sky rush up to meet them.

---

## 17. The Collar Clicks

*Подожди-ка,* she thought, from somewhere far down in the dark. *Wait. As soon as my strength returns, my power…*

She was still holding on to that thought, the way a drowning woman holds a splinter, when she felt something cold slide around her throat.

It had come from nowhere. One of the tentacles had been busy somewhere behind her, and when it withdrew, it left a dull gray band wrapped around her neck, still slick with the monster's insides. The creature had swallowed every crate in that warehouse, she remembered distantly. Rifles, grenades, lockers, machine oil. Collars, by the dozen.

*Click.*

It was a small, neat, final sound. A lock closing.

The ember beneath her sternum, the one she had been guarding through every wave, went out. There was no pain and no warning. It simply stopped, like a lamp unplugged from the wall, and the place where it had been became a quiet, smooth emptiness that she could not reach and could not mourn.

Her mind, rinsed clean of everything but this one fact, understood it at once.

*So that is how it ends.*

Through the walls of the monster came a tinny, cheerful voice. "Oh, it fits you perfectly," said Bog. "I told you it would suit."

She wanted to tell him what she would do to him. In Russian, in English, in every language she had ever sworn in. She opened her mouth, and a tentacle traced the underside of her chin, and what came out was another breathless, ragged laugh.

The collar hummed against her throat. Everything the tentacles had been doing to her, patient and thorough, picked up again, and her body answered before she could think to stop it.

Once more, she reached the peak. Her back bowed, her toes curled against the cables, and a long, shaking breath left her that was almost a sob.

*Не сдамся,* she told herself. *I won't surrender.*

It was the last thing she still believed, and she did not know any more whether it was true.

---

## 18. Landfall

The monster hit the Gulf like a meteor, and the sea closed over it with a roar that Bog felt through the soles of his boots. Then the roar faded, and there was only the long, muffled hush of deep water.

It did not stop. The creature walked the ocean floor on a thousand cable-legs, dragging a plume of silt behind it, past drowned highways and the black ribs of sunken ships, until the slope began to rise toward shore. Somewhere near the ruins that had once been the city of Mobile, it found soft ground, broke the surface of the earth, and burrowed in. The pod in its belly tilted, steadied, and settled. Water drained out of the walls.

"Landing complete, Commander," the voice said.

"Huh." Bog sat in the silence for a while with his good hand on his chest. He was alive. He had fallen out of orbit inside a living weapon and he was alive. "Huh," he said again, with deep respect for himself.

His left arm had begun to tingle. He flexed it and winced.

"All right, squirmy, wriggly monster, what've you got in there? Make me an inventory."

A screen lit on the dead console, and a list began to scroll: ordnance, vehicles, fuel cells, bolts of uniform cloth, crates of expired ration bars, some of it intact and stacked neatly in the monster's gut, some of it already digested into the cables of its body. Bog skimmed with his head tilted, muttering "Useless… useless… ooh, machine oil…" Then he stopped.

Piercing-rings. Bells. A dozen strings of beads, in assorted models.

"Well, well." He scratched his jaw. "Fit them on the prisoner. All of them. Nicely."

He did not watch. He leaned back and closed his eyes and let the machine go about its work, and through the walls of the pod, after a while, came a small, bright sound, a clear, lovely chiming that rose and fell and rose again. It was the same sound that Nia's body made when she walked. The Val had designed it to be pleasant. It was.

On a cradle of woven cable deep in the creature's belly, the Pale Murderess lay with her silver hair plastered to her forehead and her eyes half-open, empty of focus. A gray collar sat at her throat. Rings and tiny bells had been fitted at her breasts and elsewhere with a surgeon's neatness, and her body gave one faint tremor each time the tentacles shifted her, and each tremor chimed.

"Done, Commander."

"Good. Good." Bog opened one eye. "Now, about her meals. I want what she eats to be exactly the same composition as my own seed. Same formula. Same everything."

"I will require a sample."

Bog's other eye opened.

"Of course you will," he said slowly. He rubbed his face with his good hand, the way a man does when he has a bad idea and knows it and has decided, without much struggle, to go ahead. "Fine. Fine. But I'm not doing this with *you*, you understand. Make me something nicer to look at."

"Specify the form."

Bog hesitated. He thought of a woman with close-cropped hair, a scar from temple to cheek, and a mouth that never stopped cursing him. Then he said it before the thought could take root.

"Nia. The stunning beauty who was with me at the depot! With a big butt and big breasts, and smooth, dark skin..."

The cables in front of him knotted, rippled, and drew up into a column. Muscle formed along the length of it, then shoulders, a long back, a heavy, powerful frame. A face came last, with a scar running down the left cheek. Well, It put in a good effort. The creature had had a long look at Nia on the way in. The eyes opened, brown and wary. Tiny bells chimed on the copy's chest, because the creature had copied those too.

It walked across the pod with her stride and stopped in front of him.

Bog swallowed.

"Don't talk," he said hoarsely. "Not a word. I don't want to hear it say anything."

The copy tilted its head. Then it climbed into his lap, and the pod went quiet except for the bells.

---

"Sample acquired," the voice said some time later. "Analysis complete. Composition replicated at one hundred percent."

Bog sat in the dim light with his head thrown back and his eyes on the ceiling, breathing slowly. The copy had already melted back into the cables, and the pod looked as if it had never been there.

"Right," he said at last, hoarse and distracted. "Good. Start the meals."

Then, quietly, to himself: "…Don't tell her about this part."

---

## 19. Orders

Bog stood in the middle of the pod, rolling his bad shoulder to work out the numbness, and addressed the walls the way he addressed a quartermaster who owed him a favor.

"All right. Listen carefully, because I'm only going to say this once, and then I'm going to say it again, because I know how you are."

"Awaiting orders, Commander."

"One. The prisoner sleeps well. Every night, soft bedding, no draft. She eats plenty, three square meals a day, plus the special one we just discussed. Two. Keep her safe from injury and illness. I mean *any* injury, any illness, any bug that crawls in through a crack in the floor." He paused, frowned, and glanced at the gray band at her throat on the monitor. "Though with that collar on her, I'm not sure a supervillain needs much protecting from anything. Do it anyway."

"Understood."

"Three. Everything else, every spare minute, goes into taming her." His grin crept back. "Drill it into her that the sole purpose of an Earth woman is to serve a Val male and bear his offspring. And above all else, make her understand that I'm her ideal master. Not the best master, not a good master. The *ideal* one. Make her practice every form of service you can think of. I don't care how many hours it takes."

"Every conceivable form of service," the voice repeated, with the flat diligence of a machine that had never been told to be embarrassed. "Duration: indefinite."

"Good. And four." Bog wagged a finger at the console. "She'll answer *everything* in Russian profanity. I'm betting she has quite the vocabulary. I want a record of every word. Log it, rank it, we'll compare notes when I'm back."

"Recording. Unique vocabulary count: three hundred and twelve and rising."

"Three hundred and twelve already?" Bog whistled, deeply impressed. "That's a *professional*."

Somewhere in the creature's belly, faintly, a woman's hoarse voice was cursing in a language he did not speak. He listened for a moment with something close to admiration.

"Now. Transport. Do we have an air-car in there?"

"Negative."

"Holy Val. How about a jetpack?"

"Define 'jetpack.'"

Bog opened his mouth, closed it, and tried again. "A thing you wear on your back. It, uh, makes you go up."

"Closest match located: personal propulsion unit, Class Two, in good condition."

"What does 'Class Two' mean?"

"Maximum airspeed: two hundred kilometers per hour. Maximum payload: one adult Val. Safety features: none."

"Fine, whatever. That'll do." Bog waved a hand. A hatch slid open in the wall and a squat, battered contraption slid out on a tray of cables, all bolts and dented plating and a tangle of fuel lines. He strapped it on with his good arm, tugged on the buckles, and flexed his shoulders. It gave a rattling cough.

"Sounds like me after a bad night," he said happily.

He climbed to the mouth of the burrow, where the monster had opened a long tunnel through the mud to a patch of gray daylight. He stopped there with one boot on the lip and turned back toward the dark.

"I'm heading to Appalachia to meet up with Nia. The clinic, the doctor, the toolkit. While I'm gone, you keep looking after Sonya. Every order stands."

"Understood, Commander."

"And be gentle with her," he added after a moment, as if the thought had just occurred to him. "Within reason."

The voice paused. "Please define 'within reason.'"

"Uh, never mind."

He laughed, and kept laughing, a wide, rumbling, thoroughly satisfied sound that echoed down the tunnel behind him. Then he fired up the jetpack. It choked, spat, caught, and carried him up through the ragged opening in a ball of smoke, zigzagging north through the pale morning sky toward the mountains.

The negotiations with the Free Clinic would not be easy. But for the first time in a very long week, he had sent a toolkit on its way to the right hands, and a monster at home to do the housework.

Behind him, deep in the earth, the cables began to move.
