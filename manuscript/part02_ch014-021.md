# Bog & Nia
## The Ashen Dominion — Side Story

---

### 1. Warm Towel

In her "daily life", naturally, there were times when her stomach or digestive system would act up. For an Earth woman kept by Val, that was a difficult ordeal.

Bog's household robot rolled in on its squeaky wheel, a steaming towel folded across its tray like a butler's napkin. He took it with a nod of thanks and sat cross-legged on the rug beside her, the whole room smelling faintly of lavender soap and hot cloth.

"Hold still, squeeze candy. Experience pays off, remember?"

"Don't♥ you dare♥ call me that, you overgrown♥ *ngh*, ugly, green♥..." She curled tighter around her stomach, bells chiming with every shudder. The sound was far too lovely for the language it accompanied.

"Mm-hm. Lovely diction." He pressed the towel low against her belly and began circling it clockwise, slow and wide, the way he'd seen on a stray page of a prenatal pamphlet. His palm was the size of a dinner plate, and the heat sank in all the way through the dense muscle and the softness laid over it. "You know, the old Earth magazines say warm compresses help. They also say drink water and eat fiber. Which, ah. Isn't really in the cards for you now."

"Shut♥ up. This is your♥ fault. Your *diet*."

"Sure is." He didn't sound sorry in the slightest. He sounded delighted. "Best thing I ever did."

She wanted to snap back. Instead a long, rumbling gurgle rolled through her intestines, and her jaw clenched as she breathed out through her nose. Her eyes watered. The tears ran down into the rug, clean and faintly sweet, and Bog watched one slide off the bridge of her nose with great interest.

*Amara. Chidi. Daniel. Hold the line.*

It was a ridiculous thing to hold the line against. She had held a Baltimore corridor for nine days. She had not once, in nine days, been defeated by her own bowels.

"Breathe out slow," Bog said. "Don't fight it. I'll be right here."

"I'm *not♥ fighting*..." She hissed, because the towel had moved lower and the warmth had found exactly the knot that had been tormenting her since the morning. Her toes curled. The bells shivered. "...Left. A little left."

"Here?"

"*Yes.* Ugh♥. Don't♥ stop."

"Wouldn't dream of it."

Far off across the wasteland, a transponder blinked on the wall console. The supervillain, or villainess, nobody had been sure which, had been waiting at the rendezvous for twenty minutes already. Bog glanced at the blinking light, then at the woman on the rug with her forehead creased and her mouth set in a thin line of pure hatred, and then back at the light.

"Robot," he said. "Message to the meet. Tell them the plan is postponed. Family emergency."

The robot beeped in confusion. "Which family?"

Bog thought about it for a moment. The towel kept up its slow, patient circles.

"The one that's lying on my rug," he said. "Just tell them that."

Nia opened her mouth to say something terrible. What came out instead, as another wave rolled through her, was a long, shaky, undignified sigh. Her hand slid out from her stomach, searched blindly across the rug, and closed around his wrist, not to stop him, only to hold on.

"...Don't tell anyone," she muttered, "that you saw this."

"My lips are sealed," Bog said solemnly, and went on rubbing.

### 2. The Long Push

"You *♥ ngh* ... you absolute...*♥*... pit-dwelling, ... tusk-less ... *haa*♥ ..."

"Mm. Keep going. That one was new."

His bare hands moved over her belly in long, unhurried strokes, from the ribs down toward the hip, the way a physical therapist would coax a stubborn muscle. Nia would have recognized the technique in any other context. She had used it herself, on elderly patients in a clinic that smelled of liniment and burnt coffee. Now it was her belly under the green palms, and the thing that had been a knot an hour ago was giving way inch by inch under that slow, relentless pressure.

The jelly kept coming. It was clear and faintly green, pooling in a glossy coil against the rug, and it gave off a scent like cut cucumber and warm rain. It was the cleanest, least offensive thing in the room, and somehow that made it worse.

"Can't you just bear down and snap it?" Bog asked, in the same tone he might have used to ask about the weather. "No? Right. Then push it all the way out. Don't clench, you'll only make it stubborn."

"I know that!♥ I was *treating* people before your mother's cradle..." She broke off, because Val had no mothers, and the joke died in her throat. Her whole body shook. The bells chimed in a bright, unbroken run.

*Amara. Chidi. Daniel.*

*Tomatoes. Scotch bonnet. Thyme.*

*Hold the line. Hold the line. It is only a body. It is only a body.*

"You're doing great," Bog said. "Really. Look at that, it's practically art."

"Shut♥ up! Shut *up*♥!" Her voice cracked on the second one, and her fingers dug into the rug until the weave tore. Heat flooded her face and neck, and it wasn't only the strain. A long tremor ran down her thighs and she hated it, hated that her body, which had been broken and rebuilt and fed on his seed for weeks, had learned to answer to those palms.

He felt it. Of course he did. His hands paused for a half second, and then, tactfully, he pretended not to have noticed and went on with the strokes.

"Nearly there," he murmured. "One more good push, Nia. You've got it. I've got you."

She did not say *thank you*. She would have bitten off her tongue first.

She pushed.

The last stretch slid free all at once, and the pressure that had been squeezing her for hours let go in a rush so sudden that her breath left her in a thin, shuddering moan. Her forehead dropped onto the rug. Her shoulders trembled. For a long moment all she could hear was her own ragged breathing and the faint, silly chiming of the bells as they settled.

Bog's hand rested lightly on her lower back. He was grinning, she could tell without looking, that stupid crooked grin of his.

"Feel better?"

She turned her face away, into the crook of her arm, and mumbled something into her own skin that might have been *I hate you*, and might, for the first time, have lacked its usual conviction.

"Didn't catch that," Bog said.

"...I said bring me *water*, you overgrown cactus."

The household robot, loyal to a fault, was already rolling toward the kitchen.

### 3. Doubt-Capture

Bog returned with the camera cradled in both hands, a polished brass-and-walnut box on a folding tripod, a relic from some Earth museum he had talked a scavenger out of. He set it up on the rug with the care of a man defusing a bomb, fussing with the bellows and muttering over the silvered plate.

"Hold still, it's finicky. Takes a good while to catch the light."

"You're out of your *mind*♥." Nia had pushed herself up onto one elbow. Her face was still flushed, her hair damp at the temples, and the rug beneath her was a ruin of torn weave and cooling jelly. "You want a *picture*? Of *that*?"

"Of you. It's a keepsake." He didn't look up from the lens. "A memento of the occasion. Bring it in close, would you? Left a bit. The light's good over there."

"I'm not going to..."

"The deal, squeeze candy." He said it gently. He was still fiddling with the focus, still not meeting her eyes, and that was what made it land. "When you go back to your family, I'm going to have a lot of quiet evenings to fill. I'd like something to look at. Something that's *you*. Not the Black Huntress with the war paint on, not the one in the market cage. Just you, in my house, with your hair a mess, hating me. That's the one I'm going to miss."

The words hung there in the lavender-and-cucumber air. Nia opened her mouth, and for once nothing came out.

*Amara. Chidi. Daniel.*

*He is going to let you go. Isn't that what you wanted?*

The silence stretched out. At last she let out a long, furious, defeated breath through her nose, and shifted closer to the pooled coil, the bells chiming their absurd little song as she settled. She reached back with one hand and, with her jaw set so hard the old scar stood out white against her cheek, did what he'd asked.

"There," she bit out. "Happy? Take your stupid picture."

"Perfect." His voice had gone soft. "Now smile."

"I *am* smiling."

"That's a snarl. A nice snarl, don't get me wrong. I'll hang it up. But give me one real one."

"I will *never*♥..."

"Come on, Nia. For your cactus."

She almost laughed. That was the humiliating part. A hot, startled breath of it escaped before she could swallow it back, and it cracked her face into something that was neither snarl nor smile but sat somewhere between them, rueful and tired and, damn him, real.

"*There* it is," Bog breathed, and pulled the cap off the lens.

Neither of them moved. The seconds crawled by, one, then five, then ten, as the old plate drank in the light. A fly buzzed somewhere. The household robot waited in the doorway with a glass of water and the tact of a machine that had been told, in no uncertain terms, to stay out of the shot.

*Hold the line,* Nia thought, fixing her eyes on the little black eye of the lens. *Hold the line. He is getting a picture of a hostage. That is all this is.*

But her lips were still curved, just barely. And when the cap went back on, and Bog let out a long happy sigh and said, "Ah, that's a keeper," she realized she hadn't once thought to curse at him.

### 4. Tummy-Achy Bunny

Some time later, Bog finally set the promised plan in motion, and the timing was far from ideal for Nia. However, with a wink, he said, "Don't worry. Val has a tool that can solve all your problems at once," and he held up a string of fist-sized beads with a lecherous glint in his eye. Nia gritted her teeth and glared at him. After about fifteen minutes of her sweet moans and curses, Bog stood back to admire his work with his head tilted and his arms crossed, like a tailor at the end of a long fitting.

"Huh. Everything fit. I figured I'd be improvising with a sewing kit."

Nia said nothing at all. She stood in the middle of the ruined rug on stilettos she had no business balancing in, the fishnet pulled taut over her thighs and the bustier cinched around a ribcage built to take tank fire. The bunny half-mask covered her eyes and the bridge of her nose and stopped just short of the scar. At her throat, a white pointed collar and a black bow tie sat prim and neat - and hid the null-collar completely. Behind her, the pom-pom tail hung from the end of the Val bead string, bobbing faintly whenever she breathed. The beads sat inside her, heavy and humming, working quietly at their lore-sanctioned task. Her bells were bare to the air and shivering, each one ringing a clear, bright, shameless note.

The silence went on long enough that the household robot rolled quietly out of the room.

"Tummy-Achy Bunny," Bog said, savoring it. "It has a ring to it. I think it's catchy. Practical, too. No one's going to mistake you for the Black Huntress in that. Not a chance. Those villains still hold a grudge against every hero who ever beat them, even with the world upside down. Walk into that meeting as yourself and you'd get a knife in your back before the handshake."

"*You♥*..." Her voice shook, and her fists clenched at her sides. The tail wobbled. "You are... the single... most...*♥*" The word she wanted had apparently not been invented in any language she knew, and the bells chimed through the gap.

"Good, that's the spirit. Stay in character." Bog crouched in front of her and tweaked the mask's long ears so they stood up straight, his thick green fingers surprisingly deft. "Backstory time. You're my pet, I picked you up cheap, you're a little dim and very bitter. You don't talk unless I tell you. If anyone asks, you're just a rental I'm showing off."

"I'm *not* a..."

"If anyone asks, squeeze candy."

She glared at him through the eyeholes with enough hatred to scorch the paint off a Val carrier. Under the mask, her cheeks were burning. She was aware of the weight of the beads, of the sway of the tail, of the wet shimmer of the dozens of small bells. She was aware that *Amara, Chidi, Daniel* had come to her mind slower than usual, and that the roll call had stuttered on the third name.

*It's a disguise. Reconnaissance. You will be walking into a room full of the people who can get you to the fortress.*

*This is leverage. This is a mission.*

"...Fine," she bit out. "But if anybody laughs, I'm breaking something."

"Nothing a good Val can't afford." Bog stood and offered his arm, grinning from ear to ear. "Ready, Tummy-Achy?"

She took it. Not out of choice, she told herself. The stilettos were simply that unforgiving.

Together they stepped out into the dust and orange light of the Wasteland, the soft chime of her bells carrying behind them on the wind.

### 5. Ridiculous

"Ridiculous," Nia repeated, to no one in particular. She planted a stiletto in the cracked asphalt, wobbled, and caught herself on his forearm with a muttered curse. The heel was seven inches of malice. Every step came with a small, bright *ting* from her bells, and the pom-pom tail swayed in perfect rhythm, advertising each one.

"You keep saying that," Bog said cheerfully. His free hand was still resting on the swell of her backside, thumb tracing slow, idle circles as if he were checking the ripeness of fruit at a market stall. "I think you're doing great. Very natural."

"Take your hand *off*♥..."

"Mm, no."

The♥ had slipped out on its own, between the words, light as a feather. She heard it. He heard it. She set her teeth and walked faster, which only made the beads shift inside her, and the next word came out as a strangled hiss. Her cheeks burned under the half-mask, and she was fiercely grateful for it.

*Tomatoes. Scotch bonnet. Thyme.*

*Not a word. Not one word. Keep your eyes on the horizon.*

The wasteland stretched out ahead of them in rust and ash, a dead freeway curving through the haze like a shed snakeskin. Twisted rebar jutted from the rubble. A burned-out bus sat on its rims with its windows blown out and a small, stubborn weed growing through the driver's seat. Somewhere far above, too high to see, a Val patrol drone hummed along its route, and Bog didn't even glance up.

"Y'know," he said, "I really do think we could have done this over the net. Nobody at the Dominion is watching the old channels. Half the sentries can't work a keypad."

"Then why are we *here*♥?"

"Because the person we're meeting doesn't trust anything that can be traced." He shrugged his enormous shoulders. "Fair enough. I wouldn't either, in their position."

"Then why am I dressed like..." She gestured sharply at herself and nearly toppled over. Bog steadied her with an easy hand at her waist. "...like *this*?"

"Disguise."

"There are *plain* disguises!"

"There are," Bog agreed. "This one's better."

She opened her mouth for what would have been a truly spectacular insult and found, much to her horror, that the only thing that came to mind was a drawn-out *haaa* that began in her chest and refused to be anything but a sigh. The beads had picked that exact moment to turn a slow, deliberate quarter-rotation. She clamped her mouth shut and glared at the dirt.

Bog, who had felt the full-body shiver through her arm, merely hummed a tuneless little melody and gave her backside another unhurried squeeze.

Ahead, past a wall of tumbled concrete slabs, the ruin of a gas station slouched against the sky. Its canopy had collapsed years ago and its pumps were rusted into stumps. A single figure waited in the shade of the fallen roof, leaning on a post with an air of long, practiced patience. A wide-brimmed hat hid the face, and a long coat hung from narrow shoulders. Whether the silhouette belonged to a man or a woman was impossible to say at this distance. It stood very still, one gloved hand lifted in a slow, lazy wave.

Bog's grin widened. "Ah. That'll be them."

Nia straightened, her spine going rigid, and for one moment the soldier surfaced through the layers of silk and fishnet and bunny fluff. Her eyes narrowed behind the mask, sweeping the rubble, the rooftops, the dark gaps between slabs. Counting exits. Counting shadows.

"Remember," Bog murmured, pitching his voice low. "You're a pet. Don't speak unless I say so. Don't break anything unless I say so, either."

"I know the *plan*."

"Good." He patted her backside one last time, entirely without shame, and started forward. "Then let's go meet our friend."

The bells chimed softly as she followed, one careful, humiliating step at a time.

### 6. The Pale Murderess

The man identified himself only as "the broker." A bespectacled man in a long coat, smiling the thin, polite smile of a boardroom, with eleven armed men and women in patched leathers and dust-gray scarves spread out behind him among the pumps. Raiders, hired muscle. Nia catalogued them the way she had been trained to, rifles, exits, shadows - and then her eyes reached the rusted pump at the Broker's left, and stopped.

A moment ago, no one had been leaning against it.

Now a woman was. Silver hair, undercut, a long coat, arms crossed - settled there as comfortably as if she had been waiting an hour, and Nia would have sworn on her children's names that the pump had stood empty one breath before. No footsteps. No movement at the edge of her vision.

*Pale Murderess.*

The file unrolled in her head the way files do when you are trying not to run. *Seven confirmed kills before the invasion. All of them heroes. All of them in single combat. Nobody ever found out how she does it - not a mark on the bodies, they were just done. The Accord's file was this thick and most of it was guesses. And no collar. Nobody ever put one on her. She walked through the whole war untouched and she still has everything she had.*

Nia's hand closed around Bog's and squeezed - hard, the bells at her chest trembling - and she tilted her masked head the smallest fraction. *Look. There. That one.*

Bog followed her gaze. He saw a pale woman of unremarkable height leaning on a pump, and his face arranged itself into the expression of a man to whom all of this was perfectly familiar.

"Ah, there you are," the Broker said, in a smooth, cultured voice. "The famous Val. You were three minutes late. I do hope your lateness wasn't caused by anything... *unfortunate*."

"Bathroom emergency," Bog said pleasantly. "Not mine."

A long pause. One of the raiders coughed. Another very carefully studied the sky.

"Then let me make the introductions complete," the Broker said. "Our associate - though I am sure she needs no introduction. You will know her, of course." He inclined his head toward the pump. "The Pale Murderess. A bad woman to cross."

Bog nodded, slowly and wisely, like a man confirming the weather.

"Bad is a strong word," he murmured. "She doesn't look that tall."

*He has never heard of her in his life,* Nia thought. *He is going to get us both killed with that nod.*

The woman at the pump had not smiled at any of it. Her gaze was already fixed on Nia. Pale gray eyes, almost colorless. A thin white scar split one eyebrow.

She looked at the mask. At the ears. At the tail. At the bells.

Then, slowly, she tilted her head.

*Don't react,* Nia told herself, and every muscle in her body locked. *She hasn't recognized you. You're a rental. You're a stupid, bitter rental. Hold the line.*

*Amara. Chidi. Daniel.*

"I see," the Broker said. "And your... companion?"

"Tummy-Achy Bunny." Bog gave her backside a proprietary pat that rang out across the dead lot. "Don't mind her. She doesn't talk."

Nia felt, more than saw, the Pale Murderess's attention sharpen. The silver-haired woman pushed herself off the pump with one slow, easy motion and took a single step forward. The air around her felt oddly thin, as though the oxygen had gone shy.

Bog patted Nia's rear again, as though he hadn't noticed the murderess's attention at all. "Sturdy stock."

Beneath the mask, behind the burning cheeks and the tight, furious smile she did not remember putting on, Nia counted. Eleven raiders. Two exits. One woman who could kill a man in an instant.

And one green-skinned fool beside her who had just introduced her as livestock to the deadliest villain in the Eastern Seaboard.

*Hold the line,* she thought, grimly, as the Pale Murderess took another step. *Hold the line, and if this goes wrong, I am going to take your spine out through your ears, Bog.*

### 7. The Handshake

The bespectacled man's smile never moved, but his eyes lit with the bright, hungry interest of a collector spotting a rare coin. He stepped forward and offered a slim, manicured hand. "Sonya, you can lower your finger. It's rude."

Sonya did not lower her finger. She kept it leveled at the kitschy black bow tie — and possibly at whatever was underneath it — for another three seconds, her pale eyes moving from the bow tie to Nia's masked face and back again. Then she dropped her hand, slowly, and slid it into her coat pocket.

Bog took the Broker's hand. It vanished entirely inside his green fist, like a child's mitten in a catcher's glove. He pumped it twice, with great sincerity and a good deal more strength than the situation called for.

"Well. Let's try that again," he said warmly. "Bog."

"Likewise," the Broker said, a little faintly, retrieving his fingers and flexing them behind his back where he thought nobody could see. "Shall we speak inside? The old service bay has a roof, and, if I may say so, a rather better class of shadow."

"Lead the way."

The group parted as the two of them passed, raiders edging aside with their rifles pointed carefully at nothing. A few stared at Bog's bulk. More stared at the woman on his arm. One man, young, gap-toothed, with a scarf wrapped around his chin, swallowed so audibly that the sound carried across the lot. His gaze dropped to the bare, ringing bells and then jerked away as if burned.

*Keep walking.* Nia kept her eyes forward and her chin level, a rental with nothing to say. The stilettos clicked on the broken asphalt, and each click answered itself with the soft, silly chime at her chest. *One. Two. Count the steps. Count the exits.*

She felt the Pale Murderess fall in behind them like a shadow with a pulse.

Inside, the service bay smelled of rust and ancient motor oil. A long steel workbench ran along one wall, and a handful of crates served as stools. Someone had strung a single lantern from a hook in the ceiling. Its light swung gently and threw long, wavering shapes across the oil-stained concrete. The raiders stayed at the doors. The Broker took a seat at the bench, folded his hands on its scarred surface, and gestured at the crate opposite.

Bog dropped onto it with a heavy *thump*. The crate groaned. He pulled Nia down beside him by the waist, settled her half against his thigh, and rested one broad hand on her hip as though she were a coat he'd set aside.

Nia stiffened. The beads chose that moment to shift. She let out a thin, tight breath through her nose and stared at the wall.

Sonya took up a position against the doorframe, arms crossed again. She was not looking at Bog. She was not looking at the Broker.

"Now, then," the Broker said, folding his hands the other way. "You wrote that you had information about the Dominion's military depot in geostationary orbit, and a proposal that might interest my colleagues."

"I do," Bog said. "But first, I'd like to know what exactly your colleagues can do."

"Ah." The Broker's smile thinned. "A reasonable question. May I?" He looked to the doorframe. "Sonya?"

For the first time, the Pale Murderess spoke. Her voice was low, even, and almost gentle.

"The rabbit," she said, "has very good posture for a pet."

The lantern swung. Nia's blood went cold.

Bog laughed, easy and bright, and squeezed her hip. "Told you. Sturdy stock."

But Nia saw Sonya's eyes stay on her neck, where they had been all along — on the kitschy bow tie and whatever it was hiding — and never move at all.

*Hold the line,* she thought. *Hold the line. Hold the line.*

### 8. Quality Control

"Give us the exact coordinates, and Sonya will quickly retrieve the supplies we need from there. Don't worry. Your comrades won't even notice when she comes to them and leaves."

Bog paused for a moment. *Wait. That was too clean. His whole idea had been a proper mess — a supervillain outfit and the Dominion at each other's throats, alarms, confusion, every eye on the fight. But a woman who could slip in and out before anyone even noticed? No mess at all. No chaos. And no moment, in all that chaos that wasn't going to happen, to slip a collar-removal tool into his pocket.*

"Well... that's—" Bog said. "Huh."

The Broker pressed the point, feigned philanthropy: "The risk of your own kind being drawn into a spectacular....conflict is surely vanishingly small."

*Yes. That was exactly the problem.* So his mouth, which had never once asked his permission, opened and said:

"I see... but Val's security is highly advanced, you know." (In reality, it's not.) "So, it would probably be more useful if I came along." (In reality, he doesn't actually know the details.)

*Why did I say that,* Bog thought, with a small, sinking feeling. Is it really a good idea to tag along with a supervillain assassin just because his "clever" original plan fell apart?

The Broker's eyebrows rose a fraction of an inch above the rim of his glasses. "Come along," he repeated, as if tasting an unfamiliar dish. "Into orbit. With Sonya."

"With Sonya," Bog agreed, nodding gravely, a man in complete command of the facts. "The security up there is layered. Authentication handshakes, patrol schedules, rotating key-codes. Dominion engineers treat it all like scripture. Walk in cold and you set off every alarm in the sky before you've touched the door."

*They do not,* Nia thought. She kept her eyes on the wall and her face blank, and she did not so much as twitch the long rabbit ears. She knew instantly that Bog was spouting nonsense.

"Layered," the Broker said. "How fascinating. Sonya?"

"I don't need doors," the Pale Murderess said from the doorframe.

"No. Of course you don't." The Broker steepled his fingers. "Still, Val, you must admit that your offer to come along sounds very much like an offer to stand beside the goods while they're being collected. I'd be a poor businessman if I didn't ask what you're after. We were told you wanted a share. We were *not* told you wanted to be present."

A pause. The lantern swung. Bog's hand stayed warm and heavy on Nia's hip, and she felt his thumb stop moving, just for an instant.

"Quality control," Bog said. "Some of that gear is delicate. Val-made, fifty-ton ritual junk with a manual that's older than your grandparents. If Sonya grabs the wrong crate, I'd hate to see it go off in her hands. Wouldn't be any fun for anyone."

The Broker laughed softly, then turned his head. "What do you think?"

For a long moment Sonya said nothing. She had not moved from the doorframe. But the air in the service bay had changed, grown thin and dry, and the lantern flame leaned toward her and guttered.

"He can come," she said at last. "If he stays out of my way."

"I'll stay *so* far out of your way," Bog said, with feeling.

"And the rabbit stays here."

Nia's spine went rigid.

"Can't," Bog said at once. "She's with me. Part of the package."

"A pet doesn't go to orbit."

"Mine does." He gave Nia's hip a pat. "She'd chew the furniture if I left her alone. Besides..." His grin spread, wide and easy, and his voice took on the lazy swagger of a man who had absolutely nothing to hide. "...she's lucky. Never had a mission go wrong with her around. Right, Bunny?"

Nia looked at him.

Under the mask, her eyes were as flat and cold as wet slate. It was a look that had once made Val sergeants rethink their career choices. Then, very slowly, she let the corner of her mouth curl into something that might, with generous interpretation, be called a smile.

She said nothing, because she was not supposed to speak. She only leaned her head, ever so slightly, against Bog's arm, and let the bells chime once, bright and sweet, in the hush.

Sonya's pale gaze rested on her a moment longer than was comfortable.

"Fine," the Pale Murderess said. "But if she bites anyone, I'll take her tail."

"Deal," said Bog, who did not seem to understand that this was a threat.

*Hold the line,* Nia thought, as the Broker began unrolling a map across the workbench and the lantern light slid over the silver-haired killer's profile. *Hold the line. An orbital fortress. A woman who can erase a man with a look. And me with no powers, a null-collar, and seven-inch heels.*
