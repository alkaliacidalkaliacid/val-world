## Chapter 51: The Knight of Vesper


Nia did not hit him.

She could have. Point-blank, with the cuffs for a club, she might have rung his skull hard enough to buy three seconds. But three seconds was all it would buy, and she had spent twelve hours learning exactly how little the collar left her. A Val sergeant who got angry would not stay down. He would stay *angry*, and there was a leash in his fist.

So she did the other thing.

She arched her back, put her knees in the mud, and wiggled. The bells sang. The tail, which she had never forgiven, swayed in a lazy arc, and two hundred and forty pounds of soft, flushed, thoroughly abused backside moved from side to side in the floodlight like a pendulum in a hypnotist's hand.

Gorrak's eyes dropped. The rest of his head followed. It was the single most predictable thing she had ever seen a man do.

*Four hundred and twenty-four,* she thought bitterly. *You can count this one as reconnaissance.*

"Oh, you *are* a good rabbit," he breathed.

The shadow behind him closed like a lid.

A steel fist the size of a refrigerator came down around his torso and lifted him straight off the ground. Gorrak roared, more in outrage than in fear, and braced both arms against the fingers. The metal screamed. The Val were built for exactly this, shoulders like bridge cables and bones like pig iron, and for a moment the fingers actually began to part.

"Hold him!" a voice yelled from somewhere high above.

"I AM holding him!"

"Hold him *harder!*"

"That's not a thing!"

The suit stopped arguing and slammed him into the ground. It did it again. The third time the earth bounced, and by the fifth Gorrak had gone slack, mouth open, a green hand still curled around the end of Nia's leash. The girl in the cockpit gave a delighted whoop and dropped him on the other side of a wrecked truck as though discarding a candy wrapper.

Nia exhaled and began to crawl again.

She got four yards. That was when she saw the line breaking.

The Old Guards had been winning for ten minutes, and they had been winning against *contractors*. Humans in stolen vests who had joined the Dominion for food had folded the moment the shooting got serious. The Val had not folded. The Val were a species that had conquered a planet, and cornered, they did not retreat. They *swelled*. A pair of infantrymen had thrown aside their rifles and were charging the armband line with bare hands, taking rifle rounds in the chest like thrown pebbles. A grandmother with a machine gun was backing up. A boy with a hunting rifle had stopped firing and was simply staring.

*One suit,* Nia thought. *One suit isn't going to be enough.*

A black shape went over the top of it.

It cleared the suit's shoulder in a single clean vault, cape spread behind it like a pair of wings, and in midair the figure's arms snapped out. Three thin silver shapes spun away from each hand, trailing cable, and struck the charging Val across the shins, the wrists, the throat. The cables wrapped and cinched. Two soldiers hit the dirt in a tangle, rifles ripped from their grips and clattering away across the mud.

The figure landed in a crouch. Black cowl, white bat-edged ears of armor at the temples, a gray streak running through the hair that spilled out of the back of the hood.

Nia's breath stopped.

"*Darkwing,*" someone near her whispered. A wounded armband, flat on his back, was staring at the sky with the face of a child at Christmas. "Oh my God. It's *him.*"

It was. She had seen him once, across a conference table at the Accord's first planning summit, a lean silent figure in the corner who had said nothing for two hours and then solved their logistics problem in a single sentence. The Knight of Vesper. A billionaire in a mask with no powers at all, who had spent twenty years beating monsters and gods with nothing but money, brains, and a great deal of stubborn courage. Both governments had put his name on the same list. Everyone, *everyone*, had assumed he'd died in the first week.

He had to be sixty now. He moved like thirty.

A gloved hand dipped to his belt and came up full of small round canisters. He threw them in a flat arc, almost lazily, and each one burst against a Val face with a soft *pff*. Pale yellow powder bloomed. The nearest soldier sneezed, then sneezed again, then folded in half, hacking, tears streaming from both eyes and the rifle tumbling out of his hand. A second went down beside him. Then a third.

"Clobber 'em," Darkwing called, humoring her.

"AYE-AYE, BOSS!"

The suit did not so much advance as arrive. It scooped up a wrecked truck bed in one hand and used it as a broom, and the remaining Val soldiers went over in a green wave.

It was over in under a minute. The camp lay shattered and smoking, the Val soldiers tied in bundles of silver cable, the contractors on their knees with their hands on their heads, and the thin man with the cracked radio weeping with open relief. The girl in the cockpit had popped her hatch and was standing on the shoulder of the suit with both fists in the air, hollering at the stars.

Nia did not get up. She lay in the churned mud with her cuffs under her chin, the tail drooping behind her, and listened to her own bells settle one by one into silence.

A boot came down in the mud beside her face.

She looked up. Past the black boots, past the cape, to the cowl and the gray eyes beneath it. He was not looking at the collar. He was not looking at the costume, or the piercings, or the leash. He was looking at her face, with the specific, unreadable attention of a man who had once recognized a woman across a conference table, and was now deciding what to do about it.

"Black Huntress," said Darkwing quietly.

Nia opened her mouth to say something dry and brave, and what came out, to her eternal horror, was a small, helpless, chiming "♥."
