## Chapter 97: Removed

The Red Mediatoress turned from the cupped stone, and her gaze — passing over Bog the way weather passes over a field — came to rest on Rin.

Rin met it bare and unembarrassed, because Rin was naked. She had been naked since the cavern took her suit, and somewhere in the hours of liturgy and sleep it had stopped being a fact she carried and become simply the shape of her: small, quick, browned, the null collar a dark band at her throat, her whole recovered body drawn up now like a spring. She did not cover herself. She stared back at a goddess with her chin out.

"Attend," the Mediatoress said, to no one, to everyone. "The burying is finished. The threshold is shut. It will not stay shut."

The red eyes did not leave Rin.

"Humanity works its siddhis and looses its astras," she said. "Power spent against the fabric thins the fabric, and thinned here, above what is buried, the stone will be bared again. And the widening that follows may not stop. The fabric will go. All of it. Every star in its weave."

She said it the way she said everything, like a measurement being read aloud.

"So humanity will be removed. All of it. That is the resolution."

For a moment the only sound in the cavern was Rin's breathing.

Then Priya Raghavan tore out of her own mouth.

"N-no—" It was her voice, her real one, small and stammering, coming out of the same red lips that had just pronounced the end of her species, and it was the strangest thing Bog had ever heard, and he had heard the monster sing. "No, you— you c-can't— s-stop, stop it, I w-won't—"

"You will attend," the Mediatoress said, in the same mouth, unhurried, and the mouth obeyed her for exactly one sentence before Priya broke through again.

"The g-girl— you c-can't lay it on the girl, she was f-fighting the dead thing, she was—"

"The account is hers, and it is rendered." The red gaze rested on Rin the way a weight rests. "What this child loosed tore the threshold wider. The singularity woke under her hand. It rose toward the unmaking and was turned back at the edge — at the edge, incarnation. One more widening, and nothing I could bury would have mattered. That is what humanity does. That is what it will do again, with cleaner tools and better reasons, every generation, until one generation does it last."

Bog cleared his throat.

"Not to interrupt a god at work," he said, "but I was standing right there." He kept his voice easy. Easy was what he had. "When she blew that Bhuta apart, the universe wasn't coming apart. Nothing was breaking. Sky was where the sky goes. And it's thanks to her you got your burying done quiet, isn't it? She'd already knocked the fight out of that thing before you ever came down. No Bhuta chewing on your—" He searched for a tactful word and failed. "—on you. The whole time."

The red eyes did not turn to him.

"Arjun," Priya said, out of the red mouth, desperate, changing the subject the way a drowning woman changes handholds. "M-Meera. Kavya. D-Dadi. My f-family. You c-can't— they're not astras, they're not— they're p-people, they make t-tea, they—"

"You will leave them," the Mediatoress said.

Priya stopped. Even her breathing stopped.

"There is the green one," the Mediatoress said, and now — almost — the red eyes flicked toward Bog, the way they'd flicked in passing when she arrived, a glance with nothing in it. "Live with him in their place. You are drawn to him. I have worn your heart from inside; do not trouble to deny what it does when he enters a room."

Priya made a sound that was not a word.

"His kind is no obstacle," the Mediatoress went on, serene, reasonable, discussing livestock. "The green ones have none of humanity's potential. They do not reach. They do not pry the world open to see how it is made. Left alone, they will thin, and end, by themselves, and nothing of consequence ends with them. They are no threat to the fabric. They may stay."

"Well," Bog said. "That's real nice to hear."

Priya was crying now — tears running down red cheeks out of burning red eyes, the costume splendid and terrible on her, the crimson sash streaming in its wind that wasn't there, a goddess weeping with a nurse's grief. Bog's eye, traitor that it was, caught on the gold band at her waist and the whole revealed glory of the manifested shape even now, even with the end of the world on the table, and he hated it and it happened anyway.

"W-was I—" Priya's voice shook apart and came back together. "Was I b-born for this? Is that— is that what I— all my l-life, the seeing, the— for THIS?"

"You were born as—"

"YOU DON'T UNDERSTAND ANYTHING!"

Rin was on her feet. Rin, naked and collared and blazing, one finger leveled at a cosmic being like a schoolyard accusation, her voice cracking off the cavern walls.

"You don't understand ANYTHING! Not her! Not anybody! You just— you look at people and you see— you don't— she's RIGHT THERE, she's crying, she's in your own FACE—"

"Rin," Bog said, quietly, and got no answer, and Priya was sobbing *no, no, I won't, I w-won't* in the same breath and the same throat that answered her, calm as snowfall, *you will attend, incarnation, the account is rendered*, and Rin shouted over both of them, and the red light burned on all of it, and the declaration stood in the middle of the noise exactly where she had set it down, untouched, unwithdrawn, waiting.
