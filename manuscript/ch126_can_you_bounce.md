## Chapter 126: Can You Bounce?

It should have been enough. That was the worst of it, afterward, in the part of her mind that kept accounts: it should have been *enough*.

The monster had stretched her. For longer than she had any honest count of, in the warm dark of its keeping, it had opened her by degrees — patiently, thoroughly, with instruments and tentacles and a custodian's total absence of haste — until there was no part of her that had not been measured, and worked, and prepared. She had been made ready the way a runway is made ready. She had been *rehearsed*.

And then Bog — Bog, who was not a regimen, who was not patient or thorough or graduated, who was simply *Bog*, ridged and flaring and built to a scale that mocked the word preparation — Bog caught in her, partway down, on something. On what, neither of them could have said. Her body, for all its schooling, simply declined to proceed.

From the dark along its flank, the monster spoke, very quietly.

"Commander. My apologies. The preparation appears to have been — incomplete."

"Ah, forget it," Bog said.

He took hold of her pale backside in both broad hands — hands whose prints she already wore, layered, like a wall that has been posted over — and pulled her down.

All the way down. To the base. In one swift motion.

The world went white at the edges, and then it went out entirely, and the infamous Pale Murderess — the portal assassin, the terror of two continents' underworlds, the woman who had put her commanding officer's head in another dimension — fainted, in a rather ungraceful heap, across the chest of a Val reservist, with her bells giving one small, apologetic tinkle as she folded.

*Smack.*

The spank that brought her back was firm, and it was affectionate, and it landed exactly where a hundred of its brothers had landed before it. She came up out of the dark gasping, with her eyes wide and her hair in her face and her dignity in a condition that could charitably be called historical.

"Hey," Bog said, looking up at her with enormous, uncomplicated concern. "Hey. You're fine. Even the Black Huntress had her struggles, you know. First while. It's nothing to be — " He searched for the word, kindly. " — embarrassed about."

*The Black Huntress had her struggles.*

She stared down at him. Somewhere in the wreckage of her, the assassin stirred and took notes: he was defending her. To *her*. He was comparing her — his second, his captive, his enemy — to the great Nia, and finding the comparison *reassuring*, and offering it to her as a gift, this green fool, this swamp-smelling catastrophe of a man, who had just folded her in half on the most humiliating hinge of her life and was now patting the pieces together with praise.

"There," he said. "See? You're back." His hands settled at her hips, holding her steady, holding her *on* him, the whole impossible length of him seated in her to the root. He moved her, very slightly, experimentally, the way a man tests whether a joint has reset. "Can you bounce?"

Can you bounce.

As if she were a novice. As if she were some trembling girl at her first — as if she had not been *practising*, God and the monster and the whole green earth bear witness, practising past counting, in the dark, for *him* — the modules, the positions, the endless courteous rehearsals of exactly this, her body taught like a instrument taught to a student who never sleeps. *Can you bounce.* The indignation came up in her like a struck flame: she could have wept with it. She could have killed him with it.

And underneath the indignation, blooming up through it like heat through ice, the other thing — the thing that was worse than the fainting, worse than the spank, worse than every day of the regimen put together: she was *proud*. Some traitor clerk in the back of her mind was standing over the wreck of her honour with a clipboard, noting it down with satisfaction: *she took him. All of him. To the base. Who else — * The proficiency was real. The training had *taken*. She was good at this, this unspeakable thing, good the way she had once been good with a blade, and the pride of it moved in her chest like a second heartbeat, and it was the most humiliating sensation of her entire life.

Anger. Shame. And the arousal, which had never once left, which sat under both of them like the ground under a battlefield, patient and total and hers.

Sonya Ivanova, the Pale Murderess, put her white hands flat on the green chest beneath her, and rose, and sank, and rose again.

She bounced.

Tentatively at first, and then not tentatively. The bells at her breasts took up their small bright rhythm; the beads inside her shifted with her; the softened, upholstered body that the regimen had made of her moved with a fluency that no amount of fury could disguise — the hips knew, the thighs knew, the whole treacherous instrument knew its lesson and played it, and her face, above him, was a war of expressions losing to one. "Будь ты проклят♥," she whispered, riding him, "будь ты — проклят♥ — " and the curses came apart in her mouth like sugar in rain.

Bog's hands came up. Not to her hips this time. To her face — one great green hand, gentle as anything in the world, cupping her jaw and drawing her down, down through the last of her resistance, which was by then mostly a matter of posture.

The kiss was long, and it was lingering, and neither of them hurried it. The owl had stopped asking its question. The monster's glow breathed around them, slow and even, and somewhere in the middle of the kiss Sonya made a sound that was not a curse in any language, and did not pull away.
