## Chapter 6: Under the Table


**Shlup. Shlup-shlup. "Stinker..." Slrp! Chime. Slop. Slop-slop. "...rougher..." Glurp.**

**Slurrrp... "...and uglier..." shlup. Shlup. Chime-chime. "...than any soldier..." Gulp-slurp.**

**Schlurp-schlurp-schlurp. "...I've ever..." Slop. Sloosh. "...encountered..." Slrp, slrp, slrp.**

**Sluuurp! ...Shlup. "...and the absolute..." Glurp. Chime. "...worst." Gulp-slurp.**

---

*Don't gag. Don't you dare gag. That's what he wants. Breathe through the nose, you've done harder things than this.*

*Breathe. Count.*

*Four hundred and thirteen.*

*Sweet spirits, it just keeps coming. How much does one of these creatures hold? It's like being hosed down by a fire engine. Don't think about the taste. Think about Baltimore. Think about the corridor, nine days, thirty thousand people walking out under a sky full of fire. You held that line. You can hold this.*

*That hand in my hair. He's patting me. Like a dog. Like a good girl who fetched the paper.*

*Let him. Let him pat. Every touch is a note in the ledger, and I never forget a debt.*

*Daniel would laugh at that. He'd say, "Nia, only you would audit your own captors." He'd say it in that soft, tired voice after a double shift, with his shoes already off and his thumb rubbing the scar on my cheek. And Amara would roll her eyes, and Chidi would ask if the bad man had a name.*

*Bog. His name is Bog. Remember it. Like a swamp. Like what he's going to be buried in.*

*It's over. It's finally over, and I didn't choke, and I didn't give him my voice. He got my mouth. He can have it. The spirits know where I actually live.*

*Swallow. Swallow, and keep your face still. Don't let him see the tears. They're not for him. They're just water, and they'll dry.*

*Somewhere out there my family is alive, and they're waiting. I'm coming back to them.*

*And this one will not be coming with me.*

---

*Look at it. A thread of spit, hanging off my lip like a spider's silk, and I can't even wipe it away because my hands are locked behind my head.*

*Don't look away. Don't give him the satisfaction of shame. This isn't mine to be ashamed of. It's his. Every drop of it belongs to him, and one day I'll hand it all back with interest.*

*It catches the light. It's almost pretty. That's the worst of it, how everything in this house is made to look pretty. The bells, the collar, the lemon apron, the music of my own body being used against me.*

*He's staring. He's thinking it's beautiful. He's a child who found a toy and thinks it loves him.*

*Let him think it.*

*Daniel, if you could see me, you wouldn't see this. You'd see the woman who walked into a burning building because a kid was in the window. You'd see the one who held a corridor for nine days. This is a thread. Threads break.*

*So will the collar. So will he.*

*Breathe. Wait. Remember Baltimore. Remember your children's names.*

*Amara. Chidi. Amara. Chidi.*

*I'm still here.*

---

*Good girl.*

*Four hundred and twelve men, and not one of them ever said that. They called me plenty of things. Not that. That's a word for a dog that has finally stopped biting.*

*His hand is warm. That's the part I can't forgive. I want it to be cold, rough, cruel, something I can hate cleanly. It's almost gentle, as if he thinks he's being kind. As if he believes this is how a decent owner treats his property.*

*That's how it works, isn't it? That's how they get you to the end of the road. Not with the pain. Pain I can survive. It's the small, soft moments, the pat on the head and the "good girl," so you almost forget what you are supposed to be.*

*I won't forget.*

*I am Nia Okonkwo. I am a mother. I am the woman who held Baltimore. I am not a good girl, and I am not yours.*

*Pat my head all you like, Bog. Pat it while you can. Every kindness you hand me is a debt, and I'm the most patient creditor you'll ever meet.*

*Look at him, half-asleep, one hand on my hair, his beer going warm on the table. He thinks it's over. He thinks I've learned.*

*Learn this instead.*

*The door hasn't closed. The spirits are still listening. And the next time this hand reaches for my head, I will be counting its fingers.*

---

The kiss was dry and brief, the kind a man gives a child who has finished her homework. It landed on her forehead and lingered a second longer than it needed to.

*You did well.*

Nia's breath caught. She felt it happen, a small stumble in her chest, and she hated it more than she had hated anything in the last six years.

*No. Not this. Anything but this.*

*In the barracks, nobody said a word. Not "good," not "well done," not even an insult worth remembering. They took what they wanted and left, and I stared at the ceiling and counted the cracks. Four hundred and twelve men and not one of them looked at my face. I was furniture. I was weather. I was a thing that happened to them.*

*And this idiot, this soft, stupid, lemon-apron-handing pickle, kisses my forehead and tells me I did well.*

*It's a trick. It has to be. It's the oldest one there is. Starve a person of kindness long enough, then hand her a crumb, and watch her follow you home.*

*But he doesn't know it's a trick. That's the horror of it. He means it. He thinks he's being kind.*

Her eyes stung. She fixed them on the table leg and let her vision blur, because tears were water and water dried.

*Daniel used to kiss my forehead like that. After the long shifts. When I came home with blood under my nails and couldn't talk about it.*

*No. You don't get to borrow that. You don't get to put your mouth where his went.*

*I am Nia Okonkwo. I am not grateful. I am not confused. I am a woman who is going to take this collar off and make a very long list of the things I'm owed.*

The bells chimed once as she swallowed.

*Amara. Chidi. Amara. Chidi.*

*Hold the line.*

---

"Consort," Bog said, later, in the same tone he used for the weather, and then, while he was towelling her face dry with enormous, clumsy care: "Kind-hearted. Cherished, even." He appeared to think these were ordinary words.

*Consort.*

*He said it like he was reading a name off a menu he'd always wanted to try.*

*Kind-hearted. Cherished. Those are Daniel's words, the ones he whispered over Chidi's crib at three in the morning, when I was too tired to be anything but soft. And now this thing is wearing them like stolen clothes.*

*Don't flinch. He's watching for it. Every kiss is a test, every stroke of my hair a probe, and he is checking where the wall gives. He doesn't even know that's what he's doing. That's what makes him so dangerous.*

*He thinks the others were the villains. The ones in the barracks, the "foolish Vals." As if he's a different species from them. As if there's a version of this where the collar is a ring and the leash is a vow.*

*He bought me. He hung bells on me. He told me to crawl beneath his table. And in the same breath he's calling me kind.*

*Listen to him, Nia. Listen to what's underneath. He isn't lying. He believes it. He actually believes that if he's gentle enough, I'll forget which of us is holding the leash.*

*That's the whole trick. They don't need to break you. They just need you to start flinching less.*

*Another kiss. Another word. "Consort."*

*I'm not your consort. I'm not your good girl. I'm not your Bound. I'm a mother of two, and one day my husband will see my face again.*

*Say it again, Bog. Say it a hundred more times. Say it until it's the only thing you remember.*

*Because when the collar comes off, you will hear it in your sleep.*

*Hold the line.*

---

The soap was Earth-made, sharp with lavender, and Bog used far too much of it. He worked in careful, clumsy circles, as if he were afraid she might dissolve. He brushed her teeth with a toothbrush that vanished in his fist, humming something tuneless, and rinsed her with water he'd warmed himself.

Then he didn't lock her in a cube. He carried her to the bed instead, a real one, with a mattress and a sagging blanket, and lay down with her tucked against his chest.

*Where is the field? Where's the cage?*

*He's holding me. His arm is across my ribs, and it weighs more than my whole body used to feel like. I could count his heartbeats. Slow. Heavy. Like a drum buried under a hill.*

*He's smaller than I thought. No. I'm smaller. I hadn't noticed. My whole body fits inside his arms, all the muscle, all the years, every bruise. Six years of training, and I'm a child curled against a wall.*

"Good girl," Bog murmured into her hair, again and again, a low rumble she could feel through her back. "Good girl. Good girl."

*Stop saying it. Stop saying it. Stop.*

*Soap and lavender. Clean sheets. A mattress. I haven't slept on one since the corridor. The last thing I smelled that wasn't ash and sweat was Chidi's hair, the night before the evacuation, and he squirmed when I kissed him and said, "Mom, you're squishing me."*

*Don't. Don't go there. You'll cry, and he'll hear it.*

*He's not even trying to take anything. That's what I can't understand. He bathed me like I was a sick animal he'd rescued from a ditch. Nothing in him wants to hurt me right now.*

*But he bought me. He hung the bells. He is still the one who decides when I eat, when I sleep, when I'm washed.*

*Kind and cruel. Gentle and monstrous. Both of them wearing the same face.*

*This is the most dangerous thing in this house. Not the collar, and not the bells. This.*

*The warm bed. The heartbeat. The lullaby that isn't even a song.*

*I'm so tired.*

*Amara. Chidi. Amara. Chidi.*

*Daniel's thumb on my scar.*

*Hold the line.*

*Hold—*

Her breathing slowed. Her hands, tucked against her chest where he'd left them, loosened.

Beside her, the bells gave one small, silver chime, and then nothing.
