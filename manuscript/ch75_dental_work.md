## Chapter 75: Dental Work

The nine-meter suit hit the mountainside the way a dropped anvil hits a porch.

Rin had meant it to be a landing. It was, technically, a landing, in the sense that the machine's feet were afterwards on rock. The shock traveled up the legs, through the frame, into the jump seat, and from the jump seat into Nia, who discovered that a body fitted with eleven small rings, a bead string and a dozen sensible reasons not to be vibrated could be vibrated all the same. Every bell went off in a single bright clash. Her toes curled in her borrowed boots. Her spine arched. She put the back of her wrist against her mouth, and she said absolutely nothing, and she would be saying nothing about it for the rest of her natural life.

"Whoa, big bounce," said Rin, shaking her head clear. "You good?"

"Fine." A pause. "Seat."

"Right. Squeaky."

Then the purple came up through the stone.

It erupted from the cliff face in a blinding sheet, a flood of violet that turned the cockpit glass to a pane of bruised light, and out of the flood a shape stood up. A giant, human in outline, shoulders like a bridge, built from rock: boulders for fists, a slab of granite for a chest, a head cobbled out of scree. And every stone in it, each individual stone, carried a face. They pressed through the surface like drowning men pressing against ice. Mouths opened and closed. Eyes rolled. A thousand tiny agonies, packed together and set in motion.

It did not seem happy to have visitors. It swung.

Rin caught the fist, barely, with a shriek of metal, and the whole suit skidded a meter back along the ledge. "Spot on!" she crowed, grinning through the strain. "Just like you said, Nia! Boss told me you're a seasoned hero, and he was *right*, you called it straight away!"

"Ah... yeah... I suppose so...♥ " The giant's second fist came in. The ledge shuddered. The bells, with nobody asking them, sang. "L-Let's try to keep the fighting quiet, shall we?♥"

"Quiet! Right! I'll be quiet!" said Rin, who was not.

She was strong. Nia had to give her that. The suit threw the stone giant against the cliff with a crash that shook pebbles loose for a hundred meters, drove a shoulder into its chest and cracked it down the middle. But the crack bled purple light and *closed*. The thing sat up. It was patient. It did not tire.

And then there was a second one.

It climbed out of the slope at the suit's left, a brother of the first, broader, lower, with a face on every flat surface of it screaming something that no longer had a throat. Then a third, shouldering up out of the scree on the right. Rin turned the suit, braced, and took a blow to the back that snapped one of the welded shoulder plates and sent the school-bus door spinning off the mountain into the dark.

"Hey!" she yelled after it. "That was *structural*!"

The giants closed in. The suit, an inch at a time, gave ground. Rin's grin had thinned. A line of sweat ran down her temple into her goggles. "Damn it. Okay. I guess I really don't have a choice. I'll have to use my secret weapon."

A fourth one stood up.

Nia bit her lip hard enough to taste copper.

She had been looking at the wrong thing. For three hours she had watched a girl fling trees and had thought, *strong, fast, fearless*. But she had not been watching how Rin fought, which was well and loud and *forward*, as if each fight were the last page of a comic. There was no plan in it. There was no *fallback*. It had been enough, so far, because nothing so far had been enough to hurt her. Nia knew the look of a fighter about to find out the difference. She had worn it once, on a bridge outside Baltimore.

"Rin," she said. "We need to retreat."

"Retreat? To *where*?"

"Anywhere. Up. Back. The jets, Rin, now, before—"

The ground split.

It began as a dry crack at the suit's feet and ran, with a snapping sound like a whip, across the ledge, up the slope, and out into the dark, and then it *opened*, wide and slow and wet, a long jagged mouth with rows of stone teeth along both lips. Behind it the whole face of the mountain moved. A brow of rock ground down. Two hollows of cold violet light, each the size of a house, rolled open and found the cockpit.

The mountain had a face. It looked at them with the vast, weary hatred of something that had been disturbed for the last time.

"You've *got* to be kidding me," Rin whispered.

She stood there. For one second, one terrible, ordinary second, a twenty-year-old girl in an overbuilt toy stood frozen at the edge of a grave.

The jaws came down.

Stone teeth closed over the suit's shoulders with a roar of crushing steel. The glass went white with cracks. Nia threw herself across Rin, a hand clamped over the child's goggles, thinking, absurdly, *Amara, Chidi*, and then, *pickle*, and then nothing at all.

There was a flash, white and thin and exact, like a needle through cloth.

The teeth came apart. They did not break so much as *scatter*, in a bright spray of cooked gravel, and the jaw above them lurched up and away with a groan. Cold air rushed in through the shattered glass. Rin was shouting. Somewhere, very high, a stone giant was howling in a voice made of voices.

And from the dark below the ledge, over the ringing in her ears, Nia heard a voice she knew, easy, bored, faintly greasy, the voice of someone strolling across a battlefield as if it were a shopping arcade.

"Pardon me," it said. "If you came to Florence, you could get much better dental work done."

Nia did not move.

In the quiet that followed, one of her bells rang. It was a tiny, bright, clear note, and it did not stop for a very long time.
