## Chapter 105: Plan B

After a while, the duet wound down, and the darkness went back to being only darkness.

Then came the sounds of Rin exploring it. She had gone off on all fours, quite naked and entirely unbothered by the fact, crawling slow circuits of the cavern floor — palms flat, knees careful, stopping now and then to feel at a crack or a fallen stone and file it away. She was making a map the only way the dark allowed.

Behind her, Bog shifted, preparatory to getting up, and Priya stirred on the floor — and Rin stopped dead.

"Don't move! You'll get hurt!"

A pause. Bog, who had survived the day so far, obediently did not move. Somewhere in the black, a seven-and-a-half-foot Val sat back down with the air of a man being managed, and liking it no less for that.

Rin resumed her circuit. She was passing close by him when a large green hand, travelling with a precision that total darkness should not have permitted, found her bottom and took a comfortable hold.

She swatted it away without breaking stride — brisk, accurate, the way you would wave off a fly — and crawled on.

Presently the crawling changed its character. She had begun to sniff: short, deliberate intakes, her head turning as she went, following something the way a compass follows north. Oil. Sealant. Metal that had been hot and gone cold, and the faint chemical sweetness of hydraulics. The machinery had a smell, and she had lived with that smell for years, and she remembered every note of it.

What she did not know — what nobody had told her, and what she would have hotly denied — was that her nose was better than it had been. All of her was. Hearing, touch, taste: the whole instrument sharpened a notch past human, quietly, in the dark — an early outward sign of what a Val's consort becomes. She only knew that the suit was *that* way. She was right.

"Always have a Plan B," she announced, in the deepest man's voice she could do, and hauled herself up into the cockpit.

There was a rummage. There was a small, pleased noise. There was a crack and a hiss, and green light bloomed in the cavern — chemical, steady, held up high in her fist like a tiny torch.

For the first time since the red light died, they could see each other: Bog against the wall, Priya blinking on the floor, and Rin naked in the green glow, grinning the grin of the girl who had saved everybody — which, the record should show, she had.

"I hope there's an exit..." she said, looking up into the dark beyond the light.

"I'm sure there is," said Bog, with the complete unconcern of a man for whom exits, like most things, had always turned up eventually.

He got to his feet, bent, and hoisted Priya up onto his shoulder in one motion, the way you would carry a sack of something soft and valuable. On the way up, his palm delivered a sharp smack to her bottom, the flesh rippled, and Priya made a small sound that she did not finish.

Rin stood a moment in the green light, looking back at the wreck of her suit — the dead cockpit, the school-bus door, the whole faithful ruin of it.

"Thanks for everything," she whispered. "Partner."

A large green hand came down and stroked her head, once, gently — the way you would thank somebody back.

Then they set off: Rin and her light in front, Bog behind with Priya over his shoulder, heading up toward the surface.
