## Chapter 21: Quality Control


"Give us the exact coordinates, and Sonya will quickly retrieve the supplies we need from there. Don't worry. Your comrades won't even notice when she comes to them and leaves."

Bog paused for a moment. *Wait. That was too clean. His whole idea had been a proper mess — a supervillain outfit and the Dominion at each other's throats, alarms, confusion, every eye on the fight. But a woman who could slip in and out before anyone even noticed? No mess at all. No chaos. And no moment, in all that chaos that wasn't going to happen, to slip a collar-removal tool into his pocket.*

"Well... that's—" Bog said. "Huh."

The Broker pressed the point, feigned philanthropy: "The risk of your own kind being drawn into a spectacular....conflict is surely vanishingly small."

*Yes. That was exactly the problem.* So his mouth, which had never once asked his permission, opened and said:

"I see... but Val's security is highly advanced, you know." (In reality, it's not.) "So, it would probably be more useful if I came along." (In reality, he doesn't actually know the details.)

*Why did I say that,* Bog thought, with a small, sinking feeling. Is it really a good idea to tag along with a supervillain assassin just because his "clever" original plan fell apart?

The Broker's eyebrows rose a fraction of an inch above the rim of his glasses. "Come along," he repeated, as if tasting an unfamiliar dish. "Into orbit. With Sonya."

"With Sonya," Bog agreed, nodding gravely, a man in complete command of the facts. "The security up there is layered. Authentication handshakes, patrol schedules, rotating key-codes. Dominion engineers treat it all like scripture. Walk in cold and you set off every alarm in the sky before you've touched the door."

*They do not,* Nia thought. She kept her eyes on the wall and her face blank, and she did not so much as twitch the long rabbit ears. She knew instantly that Bog was spouting nonsense.

"Layered," the Broker said. "How fascinating. Sonya?"

"I don't need doors," the Pale Murderess said from the doorframe.

"No. Of course you don't." The Broker steepled his fingers. "Still, Val, you must admit that your offer to come along sounds very much like an offer to stand beside the goods while they're being collected. I'd be a poor businessman if I didn't ask what you're after. We were told you wanted a share. We were *not* told you wanted to be present."

A pause. The lantern swung. Bog's hand stayed warm and heavy on Nia's hip, and she felt his thumb stop moving, just for an instant.

"Quality control," Bog said. "Some of that gear is delicate. Val-made, fifty-ton ritual junk with a manual that's older than your grandparents. If Sonya grabs the wrong crate, I'd hate to see it go off in her hands. Wouldn't be any fun for anyone."

The Broker laughed softly, then turned his head. "What do you think?"

For a long moment Sonya said nothing. She had not moved from the doorframe. But the air in the service bay had changed, grown thin and dry, and the lantern flame leaned toward her and guttered.

"He can come," she said at last. "If he stays out of my way."

"I'll stay *so* far out of your way," Bog said, with feeling.

"And the rabbit stays here."

Nia's spine went rigid.

"Can't," Bog said at once. "She's with me. Part of the package."

"A pet doesn't go to orbit."

"Mine does." He gave Nia's hip a pat. "She'd chew the furniture if I left her alone. Besides..." His grin spread, wide and easy, and his voice took on the lazy swagger of a man who had absolutely nothing to hide. "...she's lucky. Never had a mission go wrong with her around. Right, Bunny?"

Nia looked at him.

Under the mask, her eyes were as flat and cold as wet slate. It was a look that had once made Val sergeants rethink their career choices. Then, very slowly, she let the corner of her mouth curl into something that might, with generous interpretation, be called a smile.

She said nothing, because she was not supposed to speak. She only leaned her head, ever so slightly, against Bog's arm, and let the bells chime once, bright and sweet, in the hush.

Sonya's pale gaze rested on her a moment longer than was comfortable.

"Fine," the Pale Murderess said. "But if she bites anyone, I'll take her tail."

"Deal," said Bog, who did not seem to understand that this was a threat.

*Hold the line,* Nia thought, as the Broker began unrolling a map across the workbench and the lantern light slid over the silver-haired killer's profile. *Hold the line. An orbital fortress. A woman who can erase a man with a look. And me with no powers, a null-collar, and seven-inch heels.*
