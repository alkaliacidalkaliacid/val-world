## Chapter 110: Just Do It

For a moment nobody moved. The searchlight held on Bog. The tentacles held on everything else. And Nia's stalagmite, raised and ready, found itself with nothing whatsoever to hit.

The silence stretched — and finally, Nia could not hold back.

"Did you actually fuck a tentacle monster?"

Bog looked away.

Nia's grip tightened on the stone. Her eyes narrowed to slits.

"Bog," she said, very quietly. "Why did you look away?"

"I-I didn't—"

"An amazing machine!"

Rin's voice rang out, bright as a bell, and the moment shattered. She had crept halfway down the chimney toward the breach, her chemical light forgotten in her fist, her whole face alight. She was staring at the tentacles the way other girls stared at jewels.

"It's a machine, isn't it? Not an animal — a machine! Is it yours, Bog? Did you build it? Can I see inside it?"

From Bog's shoulder came a small, involuntary sound.

Priya had privately resolved — somewhere around the moment the god in her head had been declared a married alien's fourth consort — that nothing in this world would ever surprise her again. She had been wrong so many times since that the resolution lay in pieces, and now, at the edges of this latest enormity, all she could do was mumble to herself, face burning, in a voice meant for no one.

"N-nothing... I s-said nothing surprises me, I s-said it, and then— n-no. No more. I am not l-looking..."

Bog, dodging Nia's suspicious, narrowed-eyed glare — a glare that was still drilling into the side of his head and showed every sign of resuming its interrogation at the worst possible moment — turned hastily to the monster.

"Uh. Right. How did you end up here, anyway?"

"I have come to report the completion of my assigned mission," the monster said. "However, upon detecting a significant energy signature nearby, I surmised that Commander Bog might be engaged with an enemy, and I proceeded to the scene with all speed. Our reconnaissance units should also be arriving shortly. Would you like me to establish contact with them?"

"Our units?" Bog repeated. Then it landed. "Oh. *Our* units."

The Val Dominion. Reconnaissance. Here. Shortly.

"That's not good," he muttered. "That's really not — um. Look. I'm currently on a high-level mission. Very high level. Can you block all radio communications and switch to stealth mode?"

He had no idea whether the monster could do any such thing. It had seemed worth asking.

"Yes," said the monster. "This unit will cease all transmissions and proceed below the surface. We will not be detected."

Bog blinked. It could do that? Apparently it could do that.

"Just do it," said Bog.
