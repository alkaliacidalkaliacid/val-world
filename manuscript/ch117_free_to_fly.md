## Chapter 117: Free to Fly

Rin lay where Nia had lowered her, breathing hard, hair still settling around her shoulders. Her eyes drifted — down the length of Bog, to his crotch — and stayed there. She swallowed, audibly.

Then she shook her head. Hard, side to side, like a dog throwing off water. "No. No no no."

Priya stared at her. Understanding arrived a second later and flooded her face with colour.

Nia understood too. Her eyes went very wide. Then they narrowed, and fixed on Bog with the concentrated force of a woman choosing where the first blow would land.

"Whoops—" Bog turned, bright and brisk, to Priya. "Right! Priya. Why don't you try Amplification and Burst too?"

"M-me?" Priya's hands came up between them like a shield. "No. No, I — I really must decline." The cavern was still behind her eyes: the red vastness, the borrowed mouth, her own hands doing another's will. "After what happened, I — I couldn't possibly—"

"Nonsense. Rin did it." Bog gestured at Rin, who was still flat on the floor. "Look how much she learned."

It has to be said that Bog, in that moment, appeared a great deal more eager to get out from under a superhero's glare than to keep a cosmic being — one he had already defeated — safely asleep.

"Please," Priya said, attempting firmness. "I am asking you not to—"

"Amplification," said Bog.

The command went through her collar like a struck bell. Warmth flooded up her spine and Priya gasped — and rose. Her feet left the floor and she went up in a scramble of limbs, both hands clutching at her own backside, as if the lift had taken her by it.

"Oh! Oh — oh, I —" Her eyes squeezed shut. She waited for it: the immensity, the red, the voice that used her mouth.

Nothing came. Only Priya Raghavan, heart hammering, floating.

"She's not — " Her eyes opened, wondering. "I'm still me."

She rose another foot and knocked her head against the ceiling of the living quarters. "Ow!" She hung there, rubbing her crown, lopsided with indignation. "Bog!"

"You're flying, though," Bog said, encouraging. "Now — out that way. Just push some of it aside, there's a good girl."

Priya drifted to the wall — the monster's own substance, warm and cable-woven — set both palms to it, and pushed with the power humming through her. The wall parted under her hands, strands drawing back like a curtain, opening a ragged mouth into darkness.

"Report," said the monster, from everywhere, mildly aggrieved. "This unit registers an inexplicable external interference with its own body."

"S-sorry!" Priya called, and slipped through.

Beyond was the hollow the monster's passage had left in the earth, vast and utterly dark — and she lit it red, glowing like a coal, and flew. She shot the length of the monster's flank in a streak of crimson, wheeled at its front, hung laughing in the black before its sensor strands, and looped once, twice, for the pure joy of the looping. No voice. No vastness. Hers.

Then, dutiful even in delight, she flew back, fitted herself to the parted wall, and pressed the strands home until the living quarters were whole again, and sank down toward the floor, flushed and bright-eyed.

"See?" Bog chuckled, spreading his hands. "Perfectly fine."

Nia's glare had not moved. It had, if anything, sharpened — a piercing, level stare that promised the surface, and time, and a long conversation.

From the floor, Rin propped herself on her elbows, exhaustion forgotten, and burst out at Priya in a shining rush: "You flew! On your own! Was it the amplification or was it you, did it feel like the other times or different, could you steer, how high could you—"
