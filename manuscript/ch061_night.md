## Chapter 61: Night


In the middle of the night, a bear came into Florence and began to run wild.

The town had an elected sheriff who could be reached by radio, but it seemed unlikely he would arrive in time.

In the spare room, Bog was fast asleep and snoring like a sawmill. Val were known across the galaxy as a race of warriors, an epithet that suited him about as well as a tutu. Priya, on the other hand, woke instantly. She lay rigid in the dark for a heartbeat, feeling the wrongness in the air like a cold hand on the back of her neck. Then she shook Arjun awake.

Her husband grabbed the fire poker from beside the stove and helped her get the girls into their clothes and shoes, so the family could flee at a moment's notice. Dadi was already dressed and sitting upright on her bed, with her spectacles on and a face like stone.

Outside came the barking and then the screaming.

"That's the house next door," Arjun said, his voice cracking. "We have to help them!"

Priya ran to the spare room and shook Bog's shoulder with both hands.

"Ugh," he mumbled into the pillow. "Fighting ghosts isn't a soldier's job."

He said it as though he had ever done a proper soldier's job in his life. Then he rolled out of bed, glanced around the room, and picked up the nearest weapon he could find, which was a rolling pin.

"Well. I suppose I'll go take a look."

He stepped outside and locked eyes with the bear.

The animal stood in the middle of the street, in the light of a single burning porch, with a human being clamped in its jaws, head first. Bog was not an expert on the species. He did not know the average size of a black bear. But he was fairly sure it was not supposed to weigh two thousand pounds, and it was not supposed to stand taller than the neighbor's fence. And surely there was no reason for a shimmering purple aura to be pouring off its back like heat off summer asphalt.

"Right," Bog whispered.

He began to close the door. Slowly, politely, the way one withdraws from a conversation with a drunk.

The bear dropped the corpse. It threw back its head and roared, a sound that shook the dust out of the rafters, and then it charged. The door, the doorframe, and a good part of the wall disappeared in a burst of splinters. Bog, who was in front of them, went sailing backward into the house and hit the dining table, flattening it into the floor.

The rolling pin spun away across the boards.
