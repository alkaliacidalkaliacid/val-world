## Chapter 13: A Day in the Life of the Black Huntress


She had no way of knowing whether Bog would ever keep his promises. And so Nia's new "daily life" grew more intense — and, ironically, that intensity brought her a sense of... comfort. It went something like this:

**9:00 AM.** She wakes before Bog, as she has every morning since the second week. The arm across her ribs weighs more than her old body ever did. She counts the stains on the ceiling (Maryland, still), slides out from under it an inch at a time, and the bells give her away anyway. "Mmh. Morning, consort," he mumbles. "Not yet," she says, and goes to start the kettle.

**9:30 AM.** Breakfast. The kitchen is still a crime scene, but she has spent a week rearranging it in secret, one drawer a day, and Bog has noticed nothing. Egg, rice, a tin of Earth beans that expired before the war. The lemon apron goes on. After it, the ritual: forehead to the tiles, the petition in flawless Val, a short, fitting insult to go with it. He praises her pronunciation. She tells him to choke on it. The food itself is all his. Her meals she takes under the table while he eats — on her knees, his cock in her mouth, drinking down what he gives her, with permission, as the ritual demands.

**10:30 AM.** Laundry. The water runs through a Val processor that could fill a lake, and Bog still insists on rinsing twice "to save water." She hangs his shirts on a line that she strung between two relay pylons. Each one is the size of a tablecloth. She wrings them out with hands that had once twisted a cannon barrel, and doesn't let herself think about that.

**12:00 PM.** Lunch, second helping requested. Her body is rebuilding itself on his protein, and rebuilding is expensive, as she tells herself every time. Bog says, "That's the third today." She says, "It's logistics, you idiot." He laughs so hard he knocks over the beer.

**1:00 PM.** The reservist's paperwork. Bog sits at a table covered in Dominion forms and does it badly. Nia leans over his shoulder and fixes his mistakes, because the alternative is Tau Ceti, and Tau Ceti is a swamp. She learns his unit's schedule, his platoon leader's name, and which officers drink. She files it all away. *Reconnaissance,* she reminds herself.

**3:00 PM.** The market run, with Bog carrying the bags. She walks two steps behind him, bells chiming, eyes on the stalls. The preacher is still shouting about judgment. The cultist is still shouting about salvation. A woman in a cube gives her the finger as she passes. Nia returns it, discreetly, with a hand that stays at her hip. The woman's face changes.

**5:00 PM.** The garden. Bog planted tomatoes last week without asking her, in rows so crooked she nearly laughed out loud. She re-seats the seedlings, and tells him that tomatoes need stakes, and spends an hour showing him how. He listens with the rapt, shy face of a boy taking his first lesson. *Tomatoes. Scotch bonnet. Thyme.* There's a small, stubborn pot of thyme on the windowsill now. She hasn't mentioned it.

**6:30 PM.** Dinner. Nothing elaborate, and he calls it exquisite. The ritual again, in order, down to the final prayer. When he says "good girl," she bites him lightly on the knee, and he says it again.

**7:30 PM.** The couch. The bells go quiet while he rests his head in her lap and tells her about his day: the forms, the platoon, a rumor about an outfit run by a supervillain in hiding. She strokes his hair without noticing. She asks careful, offhand questions about the fortress in orbit, and he answers all of them with a sleepy, trusting grin. She thinks, *He's going to get himself killed, and I'm going to let him,* and then, *No, I'm not,* and neither thought makes her happy.

**8:30 PM.** The bath. The Earth-made tub is far too small, so they use the shower. Lather, steam, the sponge of her own body, the slow press of skin. He says, "I love you." She says, "Shut up." He says, "Be my consort." She says nothing, and kisses him until he stops.

**9:00 PM.** The ledger.

In the dark, with his arm across her ribs and his breath slowing at her neck, she runs the roll call, as she has every night for six years.

*Amara. Chidi. Daniel.*

The names come slower than they used to. She waits for each one.

Then she lies awake, and counts the days until the collar comes off, and wonders which she is more afraid of: that it won't, or that it will.
