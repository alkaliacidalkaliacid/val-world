## Chapter 122: For the Record

The monster had chosen its resting place the way it chose everything — thoroughly, and without consulting anyone. It lay along the floor of a broad green valley in the northern Alabama hills, walled in oak and shagbark hickory — the hickories peeling their bark away in long grey plates — with tulip poplars standing arrow-straight out of the cove and kudzu pouring over the rusted bones of a fence line and what had once been a barn. Nothing about it looked built. In the morning sun it read as something the earth had grown rather than anything men or Val had made: a vast dark length of cable and plate gone organic at rest, rising and settling almost like breath, its back a warm plateau of living metal above the grass. A red-tailed hawk drew slow circles over the valley, its fanned tail catching the sun rust-red every time it banked. Down in the kudzu tangle a Carolina wren was shouting its teakettle song, absurdly loud for so small and hidden a bird, and somewhere along the fence line a cardinal whistled, clear and unhurried. Inside the hundred feet of the monster's sound-field the biting insects kept their distance, and the morning inside it was all birdsong, grass-smell, and heat coming up off the metal — and on that back, in the full sun, the three women had been sunbathing.

She had been lying flat on the monster's warm back with her eyes closed and her face turned up to the sun, as peaceful as Rin had ever seen her, and then she sat up, pulled her overalls straight, and went — down the slope of the great back, toward where Bog had disappeared, with the set, unhurried stride of a woman who has remembered an appointment. She did not look back.

Rin watched her go. Priya watched her go. Neither of them said anything. There did not seem to be anything to say that would not have been the same sentence, and neither of them wanted to be the one to say it.

Then Rin's stomach growled.
