## Chapter 27: The Cocoon Awakens


Several small discs hovered around Sonya's face, each one opening onto a different corner of the warehouse. Like the mirrors on a car, they erased her blind spots, and every angle of the hall lay open to her at once. Bog's lunge toward the lockers, the toolkit in Nia's arms, the drones still far down the aisles: she saw it all without turning her head.

A small field gun on a nearby pallet winked out of existence. It reappeared directly above Bog's head and dropped.

"Gah!" He threw himself sideways. The gun struck the floor where he'd been standing and gouged a crater in the plating, and the shock of it rattled his teeth. He lay there for a second, staring at the ceiling, his good arm flung out. "Okay. That's the second time she's tried to kill me with *furniture*."

Nia gritted her teeth. *Damn it.* If it weren't for this collar, she could have torn the woman off her feet by now. The old strength sat somewhere beneath her skin, heavy and sullen, behind a wall that would not give. She could feel it answer her like a limb that had fallen asleep: there, but deaf.

Then the cocoon stirred.

It started as a tremor in the tangled cables, a wet, grinding creak that ran through the stack of supplies piled beneath it. A thread of steel uncoiled. Then another. Then, with a shriek of tearing metal, the whole cocoon unfolded like a flower, and a mass of cable-thick tentacles lashed out across the hall.

Sonya was already moving. She slid into a disc, and the tentacles passed through the place where she'd stood. She emerged forty feet away, coat flaring, and the tentacles wheeled after her without hesitation.

"It's targeting her," Nia breathed. "The strongest non-Val in the room."

*That's what it's built for.*

Sonya understood it too. She opened a wall of discs in front of her, and the first wave of tentacles poured in. They came out the far side as stumps, severed clean, writhing in a heap on the floor. She had done it with ease, and for an instant she looked almost bored.

Then the stumps twitched, and new cable grew from the wounds, thick and glistening, and the creature surged forward on its regrown limbs as though nothing had happened.

"Regeneration," Sonya muttered. Her eyes narrowed.

The monster seemed to calculate. It raked the floor with a tentacle and tore up a long strip of plating, and the metal flowed into its body, rippling along the cables like a swallowed meal. It grew. It grew again, bolting more floor into itself, and the thing that had been a bundle the size of a van became a heaving, shrieking mass the size of a bus. It charged her with a ferocity that rattled the crates on their stacks.

That was when the alarms went off.

A howl tore through the warehouse, a flat metallic wail that pounded at the walls. Red light flooded the aisles. Somewhere in the dark, doors rumbled open. Drones swarmed out from behind the stacks in loose, humming flocks. Heavy security robots rolled into the intersections, their weapons already glowing. And behind them, boots: the heavy, ordered tread of Val soldiers, shouting in their harsh native tongue.

Sonya spat out a string of Russian that made Bog's ears burn, even without a translator.

"Ah," Bog said from the floor. He propped himself on his good elbow and surveyed the carnage: the alarm, the monster, the drones, the line of armored soldiers at the end of the aisle. He grinned. "This. This *chaos*. It's all going according to plan."

*Is that so?* Nia thought, watching him. She hugged the toolkit tighter against her stomach, and the bells gave one small, helpless chime.
