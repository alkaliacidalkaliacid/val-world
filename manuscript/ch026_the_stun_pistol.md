## Chapter 26: The Stun Pistol


Behind the tower of crates, Bog's hand closed around something heavy and cold. He pulled it out of a split carton: a long-barreled Dominion sidearm, still wrapped in a film of centuries-old grease. The grip was warm. A pale blue indicator ticked on along the receiver, and a low whine climbed in the stock.

"Charged," he whispered, delighted. "It's *charged*. Thing's been sitting here since before my grandfather was cradled, and it's still ready to fire."

He thumbed the selector without looking, because even a low-ranking soldier knew the basics. Lethal output would punch a hole through a bulkhead. The lowest setting made the target twitch and sleep. He shrugged and chose the second.

He rose from behind the crates and fired.

A ribbon of blue light cracked across the warehouse. Sonya did not even raise her hand. A disc blossomed in front of her, and the stun wave poured into it like water into a drain.

"Heh," Bog said. "Got her."

Another disc opened at his left, at the level of his shoulder.

The blue ribbon came out of it.

He threw himself sideways, but not quite far enough. The edge of the beam brushed his left shoulder, and the whole arm went numb, dead weight dangling from his body. The gun clattered to the floor. He hit the deck shoulder-first and slid, grunting, into a stack of armor lockers.

"Holy Val," he wheezed. "Could it be…?"

Could it be that she had been doing this since the beginning?

Sonya stepped into a disc on the floor and was gone.

She emerged from another, twenty feet to his right, her long coat swinging out behind her like a battle flag. Her face was calm. Her hand was already lifted, and as it came down, six discs opened in a ring around him, hovering at head, chest, hips, and knees.

"Damn, Six!" Nia shouted.

"Whoa!?"

He didn't stop to think about it. He pushed off the lockers with his one good arm and bolted, shoes skidding on the grimy floor. A disc snapped shut where his neck had been. Another bit off the corner of a locker; the metal fell in neat slabs. A third opened beneath his boot and closed on nothing. Bog yelped and ran faster, with the ungainly speed of something very large trying to be very small.

On the far side of the hall, Nia watched, the toolkit tight against her stomach and the bells silent for once, because she had forgotten to breathe.

*Six,* she thought. *She's holding six.*

She had counted four before. *Four was her ceiling when she wanted precision, or when she needed to keep the sphere up around us. Now she's dropped the sphere.* Nia's eyes went to the woman's throat, where a pulse beat hard. To her temple, slick with sweat. To the way Sonya's shoulders had tightened. *She isn't breaking the limit. She's spending everything she saved, on the one man who made the mistake of pulling a gun.*

*And the sooner he's down, the sooner she turns around.*

Nia's heart hammered against her collar.

"Bog!" she shouted. "Don't run in a line! Zigzag! She can only fire where she can see you!"

"I'm *zigzagging*!" he howled, in a voice cracking at the top. "It's just *very* hard to do while screaming!"

Sonya's lips twitched. It wasn't quite a smile.

Behind her, hidden behind the tangled bulk of the steel-cable cocoon, something shifted.
