## Chapter 54: The Escort


Nia told it the way she would have given a field report, in short sentences, with her eyes on the water stain.

"There was a Val. Low rank, on provisional reserve status. He bought me. He wanted something in the depot, and it happened to be the same thing I wanted." She touched the lid of the case with one finger. "We struck a deal. He incited the Broker's people into hiring a supervillain to raid an orbital depot. The villain turned on us. The depot came apart. I got into a pod with this, and he was..."

She stopped.

The pause went on longer than she meant it to. The bulbs hummed. Somewhere down the corridor a pot lid clattered, and a woman laughed.

"...he was in the other pod," she finished. "The one that was swallowed. He's likely dead."

Darkwing did not move. He did not ask what *deal* meant, or why a Val soldier had bothered to bargain, or what the strange, lingering silence had been holding up. He had spent forty years learning which questions to leave on the table. He simply nodded once, as though she had handed him a coordinate, and let the silence close over it.

"I've heard about the depot," he said. "The collapse is all the contractors are talking about. Their alert level is up, and there's a lot of nervous radio traffic. But the Dominion hasn't sent search parties into North America. Not yet. They're either embarrassed or recalculating." He folded his arms. "Either way, we can't stay here. There have been too many skirmishes in this valley. Tonight's patrol was the third in a month. We'll evacuate by dawn."

"You'd abandon the base?"

"I've abandoned bigger." He said it without self-pity. "Tell me about the villain."

"Pale Murderess. Sophia Ivanova. She had full powers, no collar."

That made him go still in a different way. His eyes narrowed.

"She's surfaced, then." He began to pace the short length of the room, three steps and a turn. "Interesting. She's an assassin's assassin, and she does not come cheap. Only a syndicate with serious money could have hired her, and that's not a band of road raiders. The Raiders are too small to afford her. There is somebody behind the Broker, and I think I know who." He stopped. "The League of Shi. They've been buying talent since the occupation began. It's the kind of thing they'd do."

Nia had heard the name once, a rumor in a prison yard. She filed it away beside the other things she would think about later.

"Still," Darkwing continued, "it's a mercy the Dominion's weapons never reached them." He turned. "Which brings me to the toolkit. How do you intend to use it?"

"A doctor." She said it carefully. "Dr. Hermes, at the Free Clinic's Appalachian branch. He claims he can unlock collars if he has the right tools."

"I know him." Darkwing's mouth twitched. "He's insufferable, and he's not lying."

"Could you do it?"

"My engineer can do it," he said flatly. "With time," he added, muttering, "months, perhaps. We don't have months. Hermes can do the work in weeks because he's been waiting for this for years." He picked up the case, weighed it, and gently set it back down. "You'll take it to him. Rin will go with you."

Nia stared. "Rin?"

"As your escort."

"But isn't she needed here? You'll be evacuating. You'll be exposed. She's your—"

"My sidekick. Yes." He did not blink. "Nia, listen. If we can remove the null-collars from the Bound, we have a bench of heroes that the Dominion believed it had finished. Atlas Reign. Slipstream. Sunspike. Every one of them, with the powers they were born with. We could turn the tide of this war." He paused. "I don't know of a more important mission on this continent. I'm simply making sure that you travel with a stronger protector than an old man who gets winded on stairs."

"You are *not* winded on stairs."

"Fine. I'm a *careful* old man." He picked up his cowl and turned to the door. "She's been wanting an excuse to take the suit on a real trip. It will be good for her. It'll be terrible for the suit."

Nia looked at the case on the cot. The stenciled letters stared back at her. *CLASS 5.* She had carried it through a crash, a camp, and a long night of palm prints, and she had told nobody what it was. She had not expected to *hand it over.*

Behind the bedsheet wall, a muffled thump was followed by a loud, delighted "BOSS! Did I just hear I get to drive to *Florence?*"

Darkwing closed his eyes. It was the only sign of fatigue she had seen on him all night.

"Pack light, Rin," he said, without raising his voice.

"I'M ALREADY PACKED!"

"You are *not.*"

"I'M PACKING!"

Nia leaned back against the wall, hands loose in her lap, and for a moment allowed herself something she had not allowed in six years: she let herself imagine the collar coming off. She imagined her own hands, in her own strength, around a Val's neck.

And, a half-second behind it, uninvited, the picture of a fat green idiot asleep on a couch with his boots on.

*He's dead,* she thought. *Stop it.*

Down the corridor, the bells gave a single, treacherous chime.
