## Chapter 8: The Deal


Bog's back arched against the couch cushions. His fingers closed in her hair and pulled tight, and a long, shuddering groan rolled out of him, low enough to rattle the empty beer bottle on the table. Then his hand sprang open as if the hair had burned him.

"Sorry. Sorry! Too rough." He smoothed it flat with his palm, big thumb tracing the part. "All right, ma'am. Grievance noted. My mistake. Your former consort is a gen-gentle-man." He sounded the word out carefully, like a foreign dish. "I'm not entirely sure what that is, but he sounds like a stand-up guy, regardless. I'll check with Val intelligence and surveillance later and see how he and the cubs are doing. That is, assuming they haven't all gotten drunk and sold the whole system to some humans."

He laughed at his own joke, a warm, wheezing rumble.

Nia went still.

Her cheek rested against his thigh, and she kept her eyes on the floor, on a crumb of dried egg near the table leg. The bells had gone quiet. Her breath was the only sound in the living room.

*Check.*

*He said he'd check. Like checking the weather. Like looking up a bus schedule.*

*Surveillance. The same network that found Baltimore and the same grid that tracked every hidden settlement, every Old Guards camp, every cellar where a mother might hide a thirteen-year-old girl.*

*Alive. Or dead. He could tell me. By tomorrow night, I could know.*

*Don't. Don't reach for it. That's what a hook is for.*

*Because what happens when his little report comes back? Amara is a girl. A healthy girl, in a world where the Elites have a word for girls like her, and a policy, and a ward in every garrison city. All it takes is one clerk with a clipboard writing down a street address, and my whole family becomes inventory.*

*I told him they were somewhere he would never find them. I told him that on the first night, on my knees, shaking, with every bone in me screaming. And he nodded, and he wrote it on a form.*

*He's not malicious. That's the horror of it. He'll ask because he's curious, and he'll bring the answer back like a dog with a stick, wagging, proud, and he'll never once understand what he's done.*

She swallowed, slowly, and lifted her chin until she met his eyes. Her voice, when it came, was rough and very quiet.

"Don't."

Bog blinked. "Don't what?"

"Don't look for them." Her jaw tightened until the scar went white. "Please."

It was the first time she had ever said that word to him.

Bog shrugged. "Fine, then. Let's make a deal: if you serve as a proper, devoted consort and help me manage my life as a reservist, I'll take off your collar, and let you go find your family on your own—provided, of course, that once you're collar-free, you don't smash me to bits like an overgrown cactus. How's that sound? We can keep our arrangement a secret from the others from either Earth or Val. Don't feel like answering? If it's a deal, give me one long slurp; if not, give me two. Deal?" He then patted her on the head.

---

*He's offering me the door.*

*Don't take it. Don't look at it. Look at the hand that's holding it open.*

*"A proper, devoted consort." That's the price. Not the collar for the bells, not a swap, but a purchase: everything I'm already doing, and a new word for it. I'm not his property anymore, I'm his partner. The leash gets a ring on it and a ceremony, and he gets to call it love.* *Shlup.*

*And look at what he's actually done. He didn't say he'd look for them. He said I could go and find them myself. After I asked him not to. He listened. The overgrown pickle listened to me, and then he carved out a promise that fits the shape of the thing I begged for.*

*That's either the stupidest kindness in the galaxy or the most careful trap.* *Slrp.*

*Think. Collars like mine aren't taken off by privates. They're locked and unlocked by the Elites' own technicians, working from manuals none of them can read, and the Free Clinics have spent years failing to open a single one without killing the wearer. Bog is a low-ranking soldier who rewired his own refrigerator. What key does he think he's holding?* *Glurp.*

*Maybe he doesn't think. Maybe he just believes that if he asks nicely, the universe will comply.*

*And the secret. "From the others, Earth or Val." He's afraid of them as well. He's afraid of his platoon leader and the Elites and every neighbor who might sniff out his strange soft arrangement. For the first time since the market, I'm holding something he doesn't want anyone else to see.*

*Leverage. Not much. A crumb of it. But I know what a corridor looks like when it's finally open.* *Slop.*

*If I say no, nothing changes. The bells, the routine, the praise, the ledger. Another year of this, and another, until the kitchen is the whole world, and I forget the taste of anything else.*

*If I say yes, I get time. I get a household to learn from the inside. I get his schedule, his comrades, his codes, the shape of his fear. I get the one thing a prisoner can never find in a cube.*

*Access.*

*It isn't surrender. It's reconnaissance.*

*...Isn't it?*

*Don't answer that. Answer him.*

*One long slurp for a deal. Two for no.*

*Breathe in. Hold the line.*

*Amara. Chidi. Daniel.*

*I'll be home before you finish the song.*

*Slurrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrp.*

She let it go on a very long time. She didn't look up while she did it.

---

Warm water, steam on the mirror, the sharp mint of Earth toothpaste. Then Bog's lap, a seat as wide as a bench, and Nia astride it with her knees sunk into the couch cushions and the bells chiming whenever she shifted. His hand moved over her without hurry, broad enough to cover the whole of her lower back, and the kisses came in between sentences, small and absent-minded, like a man salting soup.

"You know about the Free Clinics, right?" he said against her temple. "I siphon a bit of medical supply off to their Appalachia branch. They love Val gear. They pay a fortune."

Nia went still. It was the kind of stillness she'd learned in the corridor, when a sound in the dark might be a friend or a patrol.

"One of the doctors there is some kind of mutant genius," Bog went on cheerfully. "Can't shoot beams or fly or crush a tank, so the Dominion ignores him. But he's obsessed with the collars. Says he could unlock them if he had the right tools. Typical human overconfidence, right?" A kiss landed on her jaw. "He asked me for help. I said, *maybe, if the chance comes up.*"

*A doctor. Unlocking collars.*

*Don't breathe differently. Don't let your pulse show in your throat.*

"I did some digging among the arms traffickers on the southern bases," Bog said. "The tools are in a fortress in geostationary orbit. Out of reach for any disgruntled Old Guard with a rifle, ugh. But I heard there's an outfit out there with a supervillain in hiding behind it, desperate to get their hands on Val weapons. If I leak the right route to them, there'll be chaos, and in the middle of it I could slip in and take the gear myself." He chuckled, a low, wheezing, proud sound. "Heh, heh."

The hand at her back squeezed once, fond and thoughtless.

*Look at him. He's just told me the one thing the Clinics have been dying to know for six years, and he said it between kisses, in the voice of a man describing his fishing plans.*

*And he doesn't even see what he's said. He's selling out his own garrison and handing an outfit the keys to a massacre, and he thinks it's a clever errand.*

She kept her voice low and a touch dry. "You'd get a whole outfit killed over a box of tools."

"They'd get themselves killed. I'd just be nearby." He frowned, as if the point had never occurred to him. "That's bad, isn't it? Hm."

"It's bad."

"I'll think about it." He kissed her forehead, satisfied, as if thinking about it counted as doing it.

Nia let her head rest on his shoulder, so he couldn't see her face.

*A fortress in orbit. A supervillain in hiding. A doctor in the mountains who's been waiting for exactly this.*

*Don't say a word about it. Don't ask for more. Let him keep talking.*

*Every time he opens his mouth, he hands me another piece of the map.*

*Amara. Chidi. Daniel.*

*I'm going to need him to say "heh, heh" a lot more.*

---

"I don't take these kinds of risks just for profit," Bog said. His voice had dropped, low and almost shy. "It's for you too, Nia. You're willing to pay your own price for freedom, aren't you?"

*Price.*

*Of course. Nothing in this house is free. Not the apron, not the bed, not the lavender soap. He's just finally said it aloud.*

Before she could answer, his hand slid up to cradle the back of her head, and he drew her in and kissed her.

It was a deep kiss, slow and unhurried, with a taste she knew too well. His tongue sought hers, patient and thorough, and the bells at her chest gave a soft, trembling chime as her breath hitched against his mouth.

*Wait. This is new.*

*Four hundred and twelve men, and not one of them kissed me. Not once. They never wanted my mouth for anything but the thing it was good for. A kiss means you want the person. A kiss means you're looking at her.*

*And he's looking. His eyes are closed, but I can feel it, in the way his thumb strokes my cheek, in the careful, almost reverent way he holds my jaw. He's kissing Nia Okonkwo, not the Black Huntress, not the inventory.*

*That's what makes it unbearable.*

*Don't kiss back. Don't you dare kiss back.*

*But if I don't, he'll notice. He'll pull away and frown, and wonder if the deal has gone sour. He needs to believe the deal is real. The fortress, the doctor, the borrowed chaos. It all hangs on a stupid green giant believing that a woman on his lap is slowly coming around.*

*So. A move. Just a move, like any other in the field.*

*Move your lips. Soften your shoulders. Let him feel it.*

*This isn't surrender. This is a hand in the dark, feeling for the latch.*

Her fingers curled into the front of his shirt, and she didn't let herself ask why they trembled.

*Daniel's mouth tasted like coffee and mint. I will not forget that. I will not.*

*Amara. Chidi. Daniel.*

*Hold the line.*

*And when it opens, Bog, I'll be the one deciding what price gets paid.*
