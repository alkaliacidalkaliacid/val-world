## Chapter 14: Warm Towel


In her "daily life", naturally, there were times when her stomach or digestive system would act up. For an Earth woman kept by Val, that was a difficult ordeal.

Bog's household robot rolled in on its squeaky wheel, a steaming towel folded across its tray like a butler's napkin. He took it with a nod of thanks and sat cross-legged on the rug beside her, the whole room smelling faintly of lavender soap and hot cloth.

"Hold still, squeeze candy. Experience pays off, remember?"

"Don't♥ you dare♥ call me that, you overgrown♥ *ngh*, ugly, green♥..." She curled tighter around her stomach, bells chiming with every shudder. The sound was far too lovely for the language it accompanied.

"Mm-hm. Lovely diction." He pressed the towel low against her belly and began circling it clockwise, slow and wide, the way he'd seen on a stray page of a prenatal pamphlet. His palm was the size of a dinner plate, and the heat sank in all the way through the dense muscle and the softness laid over it. "You know, the old Earth magazines say warm compresses help. They also say drink water and eat fiber. Which, ah. Isn't really in the cards for you now."

"Shut♥ up. This is your♥ fault. Your *diet*."

"Sure is." He didn't sound sorry in the slightest. He sounded delighted. "Best thing I ever did."

She wanted to snap back. Instead a long, rumbling gurgle rolled through her intestines, and her jaw clenched as she breathed out through her nose. Her eyes watered. The tears ran down into the rug, clean and faintly sweet, and Bog watched one slide off the bridge of her nose with great interest.

*Amara. Chidi. Daniel. Hold the line.*

It was a ridiculous thing to hold the line against. She had held a Baltimore corridor for nine days. She had not once, in nine days, been defeated by her own bowels.

"Breathe out slow," Bog said. "Don't fight it. I'll be right here."

"I'm *not♥ fighting*..." She hissed, because the towel had moved lower and the warmth had found exactly the knot that had been tormenting her since the morning. Her toes curled. The bells shivered. "...Left. A little left."

"Here?"

"*Yes.* Ugh♥. Don't♥ stop."

"Wouldn't dream of it."

Far off across the wasteland, a transponder blinked on the wall console. The supervillain, or villainess, nobody had been sure which, had been waiting at the rendezvous for twenty minutes already. Bog glanced at the blinking light, then at the woman on the rug with her forehead creased and her mouth set in a thin line of pure hatred, and then back at the light.

"Robot," he said. "Message to the meet. Tell them the plan is postponed. Family emergency."

The robot beeped in confusion. "Which family?"

Bog thought about it for a moment. The towel kept up its slow, patient circles.

"The one that's lying on my rug," he said. "Just tell them that."

Nia opened her mouth to say something terrible. What came out instead, as another wave rolled through her, was a long, shaky, undignified sigh. Her hand slid out from her stomach, searched blindly across the rug, and closed around his wrist, not to stop him, only to hold on.

"...Don't tell anyone," she muttered, "that you saw this."

"My lips are sealed," Bog said solemnly, and went on rubbing.
