## Chapter 29: The Pods


"Um…" Nia began.

"Look, now that things have gotten this far, the Dominion will deal with that thing. Probably." Bog was already struggling to his feet, wincing as his dead arm swung against his hip. "And we can't do any 'hero work' with you in that collar anyway. Come on, move! Before the pod gets eaten by the monster, or wiped out by that psycho!"

"Bog, I can walk…"

"You can't run in bells!"

Before she could argue, he scooped her up with his one good arm, hooked the numb one under her knees like a hammock, and bolted. Nia clutched the toolkit to her chest and clenched her jaw as the world bounced past. Every stride set her bells jingling, a bright, ridiculous music, and the alarms swallowed it whole.

"Put me down!"

"Later!"

They ducked through a gap in a wall of crates, skidded around a corner, and vaulted a smoking drone. By some miracle, the glowing arch of an emergency bay appeared at the end of an aisle, and inside it a row of squat, bullet-shaped escape pods sat in their cradles, hatches hanging open.

Most of them were already gone — not launched, eaten. Tentacles were coiled thick around the cradles, and two pods were half sunk into the creature's glistening mass, drawn in like swallowed meals. But one pod still sat untouched in its cradle, hatch open and waiting.

"Ha! Told you!" Bog crowed.

Then a soldier stepped out from behind it.

He was a young one, pale green, with his helmet askew and a rifle slung across his chest. He had clearly had the same idea. He turned, saw Bog charging toward him with a half-naked human in his arms, and his eyes went wide with the particular outrage of a man who had done everything right and was about to be robbed.

"Hey! That's…"

A tentacle lashed across the bay ahead of the creature's advance — a blind, sweeping thing, part of the thrash of its coming — and caught him in the ribs as he stepped into its arc.

He rose into the air like a cork out of a bottle. A long, thin, mournful wail trailed behind him as he sailed across the warehouse, over a stack of crates, and out of sight. Somewhere in the distance, something big fell over.

Bog skidded to a halt. Nia twisted in his arms.

"Is it attacking a Val?"

"Legend says..." Bog said. "It's just a bit clumsy."

The creature was already closing in. It tore across the bay on a carpet of ripped-up flooring, thick as a freight train now, its cables woven into a huge shuddering mass, and a dozen tentacles stretched toward the untouched pod like fingers about to crush a nut.

"It'll swallow it!" Nia shouted.

"I know!" Bog dropped her unceremoniously onto her feet and leaped for the hatch. "Hold on, hold on, I've done this drill in training, it's just a couple of switches…" He flung himself at the open hatch, his good hand reaching for the lever inside.

A disc opened.

It was enormous, and it swallowed the pod whole, hatch and all, an instant before he reached it. His lunge carried him through the space where it had been, and he landed hard on empty plating — a clean circle of bare metal that smoked faintly at the edges.

Bog was sitting in the middle of it.

He blinked up at the ceiling. Then he turned his head slowly toward the aisle.

Sonya stood at the mouth of it, gasping for air. Her sweat-soaked hair clung to her forehead, her long coat hung in tatters, and the racy costume beneath it had been torn to a degree that went well beyond unnecessary. A trickle of blood ran from one nostril. In the red flashing light she looked like something dragged out of a furnace and not at all pleased about it.

She let out a stream of Russian, low and fast and vicious. Bog didn't understand a word of it. He understood the tone perfectly.

She glared daggers at him.

Bog let out a long, heavy sigh.
