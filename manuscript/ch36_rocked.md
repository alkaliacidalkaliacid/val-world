## Chapter 36: Rocked


At Bog's command, the tentacles changed their tempo.

They tickled, stroked, kneaded, and rocked her, slow and rolling, the way one gentles a skittish horse. Rows of small suckers brushed her skin and let go with soft, wet smacks, leaving a trail of damp kisses down her ribs, across her shoulders, behind her knees. Whenever one found a spot that made her flinch, the others converged on it at once, circling and teasing with tireless patience, until the flinch became a shudder.

Between the bursts of laughter, a few sounds slipped out that she had never intended to make: breathy, broken, stifled the instant she heard them. Her back arched off the cable floor without her permission.

*Val.* Even through the fog, a cold thread of thought held on. *They really do know how to break a human woman. Every one of them, every rank.* But she was the Pale Murderess. She was not some collared American heroine who had let a green brute lead her around on a string. When her strength came back, when the portals came back, she would take this thing apart one cable at a time, and then she would take *him*…

A tentacle slid along her flank, and her body bucked.

"Hah… ah… *ahahaha!* No…"

Her thoughts scattered like startled birds. She curled, uncurled, thrashed, and the tentacles followed every motion and lost nothing. She laughed until her breath ran out and then laughed without breath, mouth open, a thin wheeze of sound. Tears streaked her face and mixed with sweat and spit and the rest of the mess of it, and somewhere in the chaos her bladder gave out too. She registered the shame of it distantly, like a pain in another person's body. The tickling did not stop. It never stopped. It had no reason to.

And her powers did not return. She could feel them, a taut ember under her sternum, but every time she tried to gather them the next wave hit and the thread snapped. There was no way to hold a portal open while laughing herself inside out.

Outside, through the cracked viewport of the little pod wedged in the creature's gut, the glow of re-entry had turned the horizon orange. Bog lay back in his seat with his numb arm across his chest, listening to the distant shrieks of laughter with a look of deep and unbothered satisfaction.

"See?" he said to the collar. "Much better than killing her."

"Affirmative, Commander," said the voice. "Ground contact in two to the second barium half-lives."

Bog folded his good hand behind his head and sighed. A long way down, a pod carrying a toolkit and a very angry woman in bunny ears was falling through the same sky. He pictured her face when she found out how it had all ended, and found that he could not stop smiling.
