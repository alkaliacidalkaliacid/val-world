## Chapter 121: Four Hundred and Thirteen

She did not get up.

His hand was still in her hair, stroking, and the prayer was still warm in her throat, and she was supposed to rise now and be finished — and instead she turned her face, traitor, traitor, and took him into her mouth again.

*Four hundred and thirteen.*

*I stopped counting at four hundred and thirteen. A woman should know when a number is finished. The number is finished♥.*

*The collar comes off. That is first. Say it in order, Nia. The collar comes off, and then I find my family. And there is a way now — that is the thing I keep having to say twice, because the first time doesn't survive the taste of him. There is a way. The clinics carry names along their routes, lists of the missing, lists of the found. The Old Guards keep records — mountains of records, six years of them, names and camps and work details, and nobody, not even the Boss, knows how much of the whole it is. A haystack the size of Appalachia, with no index and no promises. But Daniel's name could be in it. Amara's name could be in it. Priya has put her whole network at my feet and Rin has opened the Boss's files, and I have connections, I have an opening, I have everything I have spent six years saying I needed.*

*Shlup. Shlup-shlup♥.*

*And I am on my knees in the grass, in the shade of a tree, with my overalls around my knees and my mouth full of the enemy.*

*I am Daniel's wife.*

*Say all of it. I am Daniel Okonkwo's wife. I am Amara's mother. I am Chidi's mother. There are three people in the world whose names are written on the inside of my ribs, and not one of them is green.*

*I am the Black Huntress. I walked into a burning building when I was a girl who had no business walking into anything, and I have been walking into them ever since. I am a protector of humanity. Humanity. I should be driving the Val out of this world one green throat at a time — I should be the thing in the dark that their soldiers check for twice — and instead I am the thing on her knees in the grass, and the grass is warm, and so is he.*

*Glurp♥.*

*And he doesn't consider any of it. That is the worst of him, and I have a list of the worst of him that goes around the world twice. He doesn't consider it. Not my husband, not my children, not the six years, not the fact that I am supposed to be his people's calamity. It has never once crossed that blunt green forehead. He sat on his log and let me prosecute him like a — like a wife scolding a husband home from the tavern, and he stroked my hair through all of it, and answered me about divas♥.*

*The bastard.*

*Shlup.*

*You overgrown pickle. You swamp-smelling, tusk-faced, barracks-bred obscenity. A mouth on you like a warthog in a fairy tale, a brow like a slab of pavement, and those eyes — those big, stupid, patient eyes, watching me work like a man watching weather he personally ordered. Bastard. Pickle. Green disaster. Mine♥—*

*No. Cross that out. Cross it out in the ledger.*

*And this. Let the record show that I condemn this. The shape of it — thicker than my wrist, and long, God, the length of it, down and down past every human proportion, a two-handed weight that keeps coming like a road in a bad country. And the shape is wrong. Let the record be honest about that as well. Not a man's shape scaled up — an alien's. The ridges stand off it like the rings on a horn, hard under my tongue. The head of it flares like nothing human ever flared, a menacing crown that locks behind my teeth and has to be coaxed back out. The vein runs the length of it fat as a finger, pulsing slow against my lips like it has all the time there is. It looks like a weapon that decided to be an organ. The heat of it — it has its own weather, this thing, its own climate, and my mouth is just a country it passes through. The texture — the ridging my tongue reads like print for the blind, every ridge known, every ridge memorised, four hundred and thirteen times memorised. The taste — salt and musk and the green animal underneath everything, thunder in a locker room, stink and glory. The smell of him at this range isn't a smell, it's a place. I have a home in it now. That is the condemnation. I condemn it thoroughly.*

*Chu♥. Chu.*

*I condemn it again.*

*And what it gives me. Let that be condemned too, while the court is sitting. The weight of it arriving on my tongue — hot, alive, saltier than the sea and sweeter than it has any business being, thick enough to have intentions. The way it settles. Down and down, a coal in a stove, ballast in a ship, and the whole listing vessel of me goes quiet and level and warm. It heals, that's the obscenity under all the others, it actually heals, the aches go out of my knees and the shaking goes out of my hands and I can feel my own body deciding, traitor, traitor, that this is food.*

*Jupo♥ — jururu —*

*More.*

*There. It can live in here, where nobody has to hear it in my actual voice. I want more of it. Not the ration. Not the liturgy's portion, measured out by a custom written by dead aliens who didn't understand their own — I want my stomach full of it. Full♥. I want to be heavy with it, the way I was heavy with —*

*Tomatoes. Scotch bonnet. Thyme.*

*Amara. Chidi. Daniel.*

*Hold the line.*

*...The line runs through here, apparently. Through the grass. Through my knees. Through the middle of the enemy's lap, with his hand in my hair and his beads knocking slow at the door of me, knock, knock, unhurried, the way he's never unhurried about anything except me. The line holds. That's the thing nobody tells you about a line. It doesn't hold somewhere else, in the clean place. It holds where you are kneeling.*

*Ask, then. The form asks. Even now. Especially now.*

*I surfaced, and my mouth was shining, and I looked up at him through my lashes with the whole obscene weight of him resting on my tongue, and I asked with my eyes, the way the liturgy taught me, the way the collar taught me, the way he taught me — and the bastard, the pickle, the green disaster, nodded.*

*Shlup♥.*

*Gulk.*
