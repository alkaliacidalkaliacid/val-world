## Chapter 10: Fuel


The apron came off first. She folded it over the back of the chair, neat and square, the lemon facing out, and the bells chimed as she lowered herself to the floor.

Forehead to the tiles. The petition in perfect Val, the glottal catch in the right place, and her own brief insult on the end, like a thorn on a rose. A kiss pressed to each green toe, because the liturgy asked for it. Permission came in that warm, pleased rumble, and she rose onto her heels and followed the sequence she had memorized, step after step, with the same economy she'd once used to clear a stairwell.

After it was done and the weight had settled in her stomach, she pressed her arms against her sides and offered the prayer of thanks. Every syllable was exact.

Then she sat there, breathing, hands on her thighs.

*Still hungry.*

*Of course I am. He keeps bouncing me all day long, and I do it in a body that's been rebuilding itself on his protein for weeks. Regenerating tissue is expensive. The Free Clinics would have a name for it. Caloric load. Metabolic demand. It's not appetite, it's physiology.*

*Fuel. That's all it is. A tactical necessity.*

*Ask. Don't be shy. A soldier who won't request supplies is a soldier who starves.*

She lifted her head. Her face was perfectly calm.

"Second helping," Nia said, flat and level, the way she'd have called for ammunition. "Please."

Bog blinked. Then he threw back his head and laughed, a big, helpless, delighted sound that shook dust off the rafters.

"Hey, hey, hey. That's the third time, isn't it?" He wiped one eye with a thumb, still chuckling. "A lot of work for one little housewife. All right, all right. Permission granted."

*Housewife.*

*Don't. Don't even think the word.*

She bowed her head, so that he wouldn't see her expression, and went back to the beginning.

*Third time.*

*He's counting. He's actually keeping a tally, the way I keep mine.*

*Except mine ends in a collar coming off. His ends in a doctor, a box of tools, and a heist he's too cheerful to see is suicide.*

*We're both waiting for something, aren't we? Two accountants, working the same ledger from opposite ends.*

*Hold the line.*

*...Fuel.*

*It's only fuel.*

The bells chimed, once, clear and sweet. She did not notice that she had stopped saying the roll call.

"You said my 'meal' was the worst among all the Val soldiers, didn't you? You're like a sommelier. Wait—"sommelière"—that's the right term, isn't it? Anyway, I suppose that makes it something special—so I'm honored."

He patted her on the head.

She made a disgruntled slurp—or perhaps a sound feigning displeasure.

*Shlup.*

*Sommelière.*

*He's right about the word, and that's the infuriating part. I can't even correct him.*

*"The worst among all the Val soldiers." I said that. I said it as an insult, with everything I had, and the overgrown pickle turned it over in his big green hands, found the other side, and pinned it to his chest like a medal. A sommelière rates the vintages. Which makes me an expert, and an expert's opinion is the highest honor there is.* *Slrp.*

*Four hundred and twelve data points. He's done the arithmetic and concluded that I'm a professional who has chosen him as the finest in a very long line.*

*That is not what I meant. That is not remotely what I meant.* *Glurp.*

*"I'm honored." Honored! Look at his face, half-lidded and beaming, all that blunt jaw gone soft with pride. His palm rests on my head and he's patting it in a slow, contented rhythm, the way you pat a cat that has just brought you a bird.*

*Don't smile. Absolutely not. If this sound comes out sounding pleased, I'll never hear the end of it.*

*So make it a different sound. Make it sour. Make it a sulk.* *Mmph. Slop.*

*There. Disgruntled. Aggrieved. Exactly the noise of a woman who would very much like to bite him and is, for tactical reasons, refraining.* *Slrp-slrp.*

*Why does it come out so wet and eager, then?*

*It's the angle. It's the acoustics. It's the apron, hung on its hook, watching me.* *Shlup.*

*Fine. Let him have his honor. Let him tell the whole platoon about the sommelière in his kitchen, if he's dumb enough, and watch what happens to a man who confesses that he's collecting a Bound's review.*

*I'll hand him a better one when the collar is off.*

*One star.*

*Amara. Chidi. Daniel.*

*...Amara. Chidi.*

*...Daniel.*

*Hold the line.*

*Glurp.*
