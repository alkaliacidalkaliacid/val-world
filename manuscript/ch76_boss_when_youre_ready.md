## Chapter 76: Boss, When You're Ready

"Ugh! A Val soldier!"

Rin said it the way other people say *a cockroach*, and all her good manners went with it. The suit wrenched itself out of the cracked jaw, pried the school-bus-less shoulder free of a tooth that was still trying to bite, and Rin, with an inelegant wriggle, shoved the hatch open and scrambled out through the fissure in a clatter of goggles and elbows. She landed in a crouch on the gravel, a wrench in each fist.

Nia stayed where she was.

She could see it all through the broken glass. A green giant stood in the open below the ledge, seven and a half feet of him, shoulders like a doorway, in a soot-streaked soldier's coat that had seen better decades. The overhead light was a ghastly violet and made his skin look like pond water. He held a fat, ugly, ill-made Val laser in both hands, and the barrel was glowing the color of a stove coil.

A stone giant, huge and furious, lumbered at him out of the dark.

He lifted the laser, aimed with all the care of a man swatting a fly, and pulled the trigger.

The giant came apart in a pillar of white. A thousand little faces, a thousand tiny shrieks, flew outward and went to gravel. A rain of cooked pebbles pattered over the ledge. The Val soldier looked at the smoking weapon, said "Oof, hot, hot, *hot*," shook his hand, and, with an air of staggering, casual indifference, threw the thing over his shoulder into a ravine.

"That's enough of *that*," he muttered.

It rang off the rocks for quite a long time. Nia listened to it go.

How *ridiculous*, she thought. How absurd. She was thirty-nine years old, she had held the Baltimore corridor nine days, she had a litany for Amara and Chidi and Daniel, and she was sitting in a wrecked toy with her chin trembling and her eyes stinging because of a *Val*. A man who had bought her at a slave market. A man who had put her in a pod and *launched* it. A man who, according to the most reasonable half of her mind, had been dead for four days and eaten by a tentacle monster.

The other half of her mind had stopped speaking altogether. It was busy being a very small, very loud kettle.

"Nia?"

Rin had climbed back up to the shattered glass. Her face, smeared with dust, hung inches from Nia's, wide-eyed and worried. "Nia, are you okay? You look weird. You went all quiet. Did the teeth get you? I can fix teeth, probably. Mostly I fix *knees*, but..."

"Yeah," said Nia. Her eyes had narrowed. "I'm fine."

"You sure? Because the Val is, like, *right there*, and I really think I should clobber him, I'm just waiting for you to say..."

"Wait."

"Wait?"

"*Wait,*" Nia said again, quietly, in a voice with grass in it.

Because the Val soldier, having disposed of his weapon, had not come toward them. He had turned the other way.

Behind a nearby boulder, in the lee of a long slab of fallen cliff, someone was crouched with her head down and her arms over it. A woman. Plump and soft-shouldered, with dark hair coming loose out of a braid, and wearing next to nothing. A black-and-crimson something, a short and *very* generous costume, cut high at the hips and low across the chest, that did not look like it had been sewn so much as *happened* to her. She looked as though she had been caught in the rain, and the rain had been fire.

The Val strolled over, bent down, and lifted her bodily into the air.

Something went quietly white inside Nia.

He settled the woman onto his shoulders like a sack of rice, one big green hand curled around her plump thigh to hold her steady, and, without the faintest sign of hurry, began to jog toward the suit. The woman clung to his head with both hands. Her braid bounced. The rocks shuddered behind them as two more stone giants, one after the other, stood up out of the scree.

"Who," said Nia, "is that *woman*?"

"Dunno," said Rin. "Why? Is she a hero?"

"I have no idea. I don't *care*. It's just..."

It was just that the Val soldier, her Val soldier, her overgrown cactus, her *pickle*, did not have a free hand to hold a woman's thigh with. He had no business having *any* thighs on his shoulders. He had business being dead, and being mourned, and being *sorry*.

"It's just a strange thing," Nia finished carefully, "to carry. That's all."

"Right," said Rin, with total faith.

The giants closed in behind the running Val. The first raised a fist the size of a car. The soldier did not turn his head. He simply said, to the woman on his shoulders, in his flat, greasy, unbothered voice, "Boss, when you're ready," and the woman, who had been sagging and shivering over his skull, lifted her head.

Her face was not the face of a frightened nurse.

She raised both hands.

Nia saw her skin change. It began at the fingertips and rolled inward: a deep blush, a flush of copper, then red, then a red so deep and clean it looked like the heart of a forge. It poured up her arms, across her shoulders, down the front of that outrageous costume and into the very hair on her head, and a light came out of her with it. Not violet. Crimson, rising in a long ringing sheet that swelled and spread and struck the stone giants full in their chests.

The purple screamed.

It did not fade. It *recoiled*, curling away from the red like paper from a flame, and the walking mountains stopped in their tracks with their stony hands raised and a thousand tiny faces wrenched, for a single astonished second, toward the light.

The bells on Nia's body, every last one, went absolutely silent.
