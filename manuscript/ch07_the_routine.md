## Chapter 7: The Routine


1. *Shlup. Shlup-shlup.* "You overgrown pickle, you smell like a swamp and you've got the table manners of one too." *Glurp.*

2. *Slrp! Slop.* "Four hundred and twelve men before you, and you're still the ugliest." *Slop-slop.*

3. *Schlurp-schlurp-schlurp.* "Is this the lemon apron's fault, or are you always this proud of yourself?" *Sloosh.*

4. *Slurrrp...* "Tau Ceti would have been an upgrade. At least the brittle stars have taste." *Shlup.*

5. *Gulp-slurp.* "Go ahead, tell me I'm a good girl again. I'll wait. I've got all day to hate you." *Slrp, slrp, slrp.*

6. *Sluuurp!* "You hold your beer like a man holding a grudge. At least the beer's got something going for it." *Glurp.*

7. *Shlup. Slop.* "You couldn't hold a candle to the dumbest man in Baltimore, and he's been dead six years." *Chime. Slop-slop.*

8. *Slurrrp... shlup.* "Your Elites can't even find the leak in their own cradles. And you think *I'm* the broken one?" *Gulp-slurp.*

9. *Schlurp. Slrp. Slop.* "Pat my head one more time. Go on. I'm counting every finger, Bog, and I'm very good at arithmetic." *Sloosh.*

10. *Glurp-glurp-glurp.* "Stinker. Rougher and uglier than any soldier I've ever met. And the absolute worst." *Sluuurp! Gulp-slurp.*

---

*Don't stop. Say something. Anything. You were mid-sentence, you had the next insult loaded, and now there's nothing, just my own throat closing like a fist, and my breath coming ragged where he can hear it.*

*He's heard it. Of course he has. His hand stopped moving in my hair.*

*Don't let it be a tremor. Call it anger. Call it the collar. Call it anything but what it is.*

*There's that thread again, hanging between my lip and him, shining like something precious. I pulled away and it came with me, long and thin and stubborn, and it doesn't break. Even that won't let go of me.*

*Look at it. Six years ago I would have laughed at this. The woman who, the day her powers first awoke, had carried a collapsing apartment building on her back, kneeling on a kitchen floor with a lemon on her chest, losing a fight against her own windpipe.*

*Stop. That's the wrong story. I'm not losing. I've kept the count. I've kept the names. I've kept every insult I owe him, and I'm still counting.*

*So why did my voice break?*

*Not from fear. I know what fear feels like, and this isn't it. It's the routine. Three times, four, five times a day, and the strange, awful truth is that my body has stopped bracing for the blow. It waits for the pat on the head instead.*

*That's what's trembling. Not my courage. My expectation.*

*He's watching me with those big, stupid, patient eyes. He isn't angry that I stopped. He's worried. The overgrown pickle is worried about me.*

*Don't let him see that you noticed.*

*Wipe your face. Straighten your back. Find the next word, the sharpest one you've got, and put it in your mouth like a blade. You'll hate him tomorrow too. You have to. If you stop hating him, there's nobody left holding the corridor.*

*Amara. Chidi. Daniel.*

*Breathe.*

*"Stinker," I'll say. "Rougher and uglier than any soldier I've ever met."*

*Just as soon as my voice comes back.*

---

*Don't swallow. Hold it.*

*He said it so softly. Like a teacher asking a child to wait for the bell. No order in his voice, no edge, just that low, patient rumble, and my body is already obeying before my mind has finished hating it.*

*Why? What does he want? Is it a game? A test? Some Val custom I was never told about, some ritual the barracks never bothered to teach me because nobody cared whether I understood?*

*No. Look at his face. He's not performing. He wants me to stay here, in this moment, and to notice it. To take my time.*

*That's worse. The barracks never asked me to take my time.*

*I can feel every second now. The weight of it, the heat, the seconds stretching out while he watches my face with those enormous, tender, idiot eyes. Nobody has ever looked at my face before. Four hundred and twelve men, and every one of them stared past me at the wall.*

*I should spit. I should do it right now. Let it land on his boots and see what that gentle voice does then.*

*But he said please, in his way. And my mouth is still shut, and I don't know why.*

*Don't mistake this for obedience. I'm only waiting. I'm only watching. A hunter holds still because she's learning the animal, and every second he gives me is another piece of him I get to memorize.*

*His breathing. The slackness in his hand. The way his shoulders have dropped, like he's finally, finally relaxed.*

*Your guard is down, Bog. Do you know that?*

*Good.*

*Keep telling yourself I'm tame.*

*Amara. Chidi. Daniel.*

*Hold the line.*

---

*Hold it. Move it. Stir it.*

*He says it like a man teaching a child to rinse after brushing. Patient, careful, a little proud, and I'm doing it. My body is doing it. My tongue is following the instruction while the rest of me stands outside the room, arms folded, taking notes.*

*Don't think about what it is. Think about what it's for. That's the only trick I ever learned in the barracks: find the function, and the rest becomes a task.*

*Task. Hold. Shift. Wait.*

*But he's asking me to notice it. That's the difference, and it's the difference that scares me. Before, nobody cared what I felt. Now he wants me to pay attention. To taste. To stay present for every second of it.*

*And the worst part is that I can. I know this flavor better than I know my mother's cooking. When did that happen? Somewhere along the line I stopped remembering what Daniel's jollof tasted like. I try to call it up now, the smoke, the pepper, the steam on the kitchen window, and there's only this, thick and familiar, where the memory should be.*

*That's what he's really doing, isn't it? Not the meal. The forgetting. One mouthful at a time, making this the only taste I have left.*

*No.*

*I'm still here. I can still list the ingredients. Tomatoes. Scotch bonnet. Thyme. Say them. Say them again.*

*Tomatoes. Scotch bonnet. Thyme.*

*He's watching my cheeks move. His hand is in my hair, and it's so gentle that I want to scream.*

*Don't swallow yet. He hasn't said you can.*

*That's what he's counting on. Not that I'll obey. That I'll wait for permission.*

*I'm waiting because I choose to. I'm waiting because the moment he says "good girl," I'm going to look him in the eye and let him see exactly how much I hate him.*

*Tomatoes. Scotch bonnet. Thyme.*

*Amara. Chidi. Daniel.*

*Hold the line.*

---

*Open.*

*Show him. Hold still and let him look, like a child showing a dentist, like a dog presenting an empty bowl. He leans in with that serious, absorbed face, studying me the way he studied the skewer stand.*

*Don't look away. If you look away, he wins something.*

*So look at him instead. Memorize the shape of that brow, those blunt, kind, stupid features. In six years I have never once been able to put a face to any of them, and now I have one. I'll know it anywhere.*

*"Good girl." His thumb brushes my cheek. "You can swallow."*

*Permission. He's actually granting me permission.*

*And I do it. Not because he said so. Because there's nothing else left to do.*

*It goes down heavy and warm and familiar, and settles low in me like a stone dropped in a well, and I feel it settle. That's the worst part. I feel it settle, and some traitor in my body goes quiet and satisfied, the way a belly does after a long day.*

*I'm not hungry. I haven't been hungry in six years.*

*I'm not grateful. I will never be grateful.*

*But I'm full, and he can see it in my face, and he's smiling like I've given him a gift.*

*Smile, Bog. Smile while you can.*

*Every day I'm still counting. Four hundred and twelve. Four hundred and thirteen. Each one a name in the ledger, and yours is at the top.*

*Amara. Chidi. Daniel.*

*I haven't forgotten the taste of jollof. I haven't forgotten the taste of home.*

*Tomatoes. Scotch bonnet. Thyme.*

*Hold the line.*

---

Bog murmured something in the old Val tongue, a low string of syllables that rolled like gravel in a barrel. Then he switched back to English, his voice gentle and a little shy.

"After you've eaten your fill, you're supposed to offer a prayer of thanks to the one who provided it." He scratched the back of his neck. "But do you remember the words? Did the fools at the front, or in the markets, actually teach you properly?"

Nia drew her arms in against her chest and hunched her shoulders, wearing the sullen, bored air of a gifted delinquent facing a new teacher who is far too friendly. She stayed in her squat, knees apart and bells still, and looked up at him from under her brows.

Then she recited it.

Every syllable, every drop and rise of the old tongue, came out perfectly. She had the cadence of the third line right, the glottal catch before the closing phrase, and the hushed, reverent drop at the end. It rang through the little kitchen like a bell struck cleanly.

*Of course I know it.*

*They drilled it into me at the front. Wrong stress, wrong breath, wrong pause, and the collar answered. After the first week I could have recited it in my sleep. I did, some nights. Four hundred and twelve repetitions, each one a lesson in precision.*

*I never forgot a single word. Isn't that funny? I can't recall the lullaby I sang to Chidi, but I know this by heart.*

Bog's whole face lit up. His blunt brow lifted and his wide, tusked mouth stretched into a smile that was pure delight, like a teacher watching his worst student ace the exam.

"Perfect," he breathed. "That's perfect! Not a single mistake." His big hand came down over her hair, stroking it slowly. "You're a clever girl, aren't you? Cleverer than any of those idiots deserved."

*Don't. Don't be pleased. Don't let your spine soften like that.*

*It's a hymn. It's a hymn to a man who bought me at a market. I said it because I had no choice, and he's treating it like a gift.*

*Even now, even in his joy, he doesn't hear what the words really say. He thinks they're gratitude.*

*They are, aren't they? That's the poison in it. I did say thank you. In perfect Val. To him.*

Her jaw tightened until the scar along her cheek went white. She kept her eyes locked on his chest, because if she looked at his face, she would see how proud he was, and that would be the worst thing of all.

*Tomatoes. Scotch bonnet. Thyme.*

*Amara. Chidi. Daniel.*

*Hold the line.*

---

*The bells go first. They always do. I fold down onto the floor and they chime ahead of me, a clear, lovely little announcement, and I hate that I no longer have to be told to make them ring.*

*Forehead to the tiles. Back bared. Wedding-ring tan still on my finger, like a stripe of old paint, and the back that Daniel used to trace with his thumb on the nights when I couldn't sleep, offered up to a thing I once knocked flat with a single punch. I remember the way they fell. I remember the sound.*

*Now the words.*

*The petition, in perfect Val, with the glottal catch before the last line. And then my own little line tacked on at the end, sharp and mean, so he knows who is saying it. It's the only part of the liturgy that belongs to me.*

*A kiss pressed to each green toe, because the liturgy asks for it. That step, too, my mouth takes without being told.*

*"Good," he says. "Beautiful pronunciation. And I like how you tuck your chin."*

*He likes how I tuck my chin.*

*Nobody told me I was doing it. Some animal part of me worked out the angle that makes the bells sing and keeps the collar quiet, and now a green giant is applauding it as if it were a talent.*

*Look up. Do the rest. I know the order of it by heart, the way I know the order of a rescue: approach, assess, commit. It's a drill. It's only a drill.*

*But Nia, listen to your own hands. When did they stop shaking?*

*That's what I can't forgive. Not the collar. Not the cube. Not the bed. The quiet. The ritual is so smooth now that my body goes through it like a river through a channel it carved itself, and the part of me that should be screaming has learned to sit in the corner and watch.*

*He praises every gesture. Every one. Even the ones I do with my teeth clenched. Especially those. He says "clever girl" and "so devoted" and "you've gotten so good at this," and every word is a pebble dropped into a well, and one day, if I'm not careful, the well will fill up.*

*So I count the pebbles instead.*

*Four hundred and twelve. And then the new ones, every day. A different ledger now. One he doesn't know I'm keeping.*

*Tomatoes. Scotch bonnet. Thyme.*

*Daniel's face. I can still see Daniel's face. Round cheeks, tired eyes, that crooked tooth he refused to fix.*

*Amara, with her textbook open on the stairs. Chidi, asleep with one sock on.*

*That's what the ritual cannot reach. It can take my voice and my knees and my taste for jollof. It cannot take the roll call.*

*Say it again, Nia. Out loud, under the chiming, where the bells will cover it.*

*Amara. Chidi. Daniel.*

*I am still the one who holds the line.*

---

"Your kitchen is a crime scene," Nia said, pulling away just long enough to tilt her head and glare up at him. The bells gave a small, sour chime. "Dish rack next to the fridge. Knives in the cupboard with the cereal. The sink is at chest height for me and the stove is somewhere near my chin. Whoever organized this had never seen a house." *Shlup.*

"I thought it looked homey," Bog said.

"You thought *wrong*." *Slrp.* "Nothing matches. You've got a toaster from Ohio and a rice cooker from Osaka, and the outlets are all wired to something that hums like a dying wasp. I can't cook in here. I can't clean in here. I couldn't *stand* in here for a week without wanting to burn it down."

"You've been cooking for three days."

"Out of spite." *Glurp.* She paused, ran her tongue around her lips, and let the next sentence come out slow and flat. "My husband would've fixed it in an afternoon. He never waited to be asked. He'd see a pile of dishes and just start. Every night. Even after a double shift. Even when I told him to sit down."

Bog's hand stopped moving in her hair.

"He sounds like a good man," he said, after a moment, in a quiet voice. Then he cleared his throat and added, brightly, "Okay. I'll help."

Nia's eyes narrowed.

"I'll do the chores with you. We can turn on the helper robots. I've got three of them, they're in the closet." He scratched his jaw with the other hand. "I only wanted to watch you work because you looked so cute in the apron."

Silence.

She stared at him. The bells didn't move. A faint, furious color crept up the side of her neck.

Then she snorted, a short, disgusted huff of air, and turned her head away, her whole body radiating contempt. She looked at the wall, the floor, the lemon apron on its hook, anything but his face.

*Shlup.*

*Shlup. Slrp.*

She focused on her meal.

---

*Shlup.*

*Everything about this thing is an insult. The heat of it, like a stove left on all day. The stink, which is a swamp and a locker room and a rusted engine, all of it fused together. The weight of it on my tongue, the sheer, stupid bulk, as if the whole species had been built to the wrong scale as a joke at my expense.* *Slrp.*

*Daniel never needed any of this. Daniel was gentle and funny and he got the proportions right. He never once made me feel like I was being fed through a hole in a fence.* *Glurp.*

*And Bog is proud of it. That's the stupid part. He sits there like a man showing off a prize marrow at a country fair, stroking my hair, waiting for me to be impressed. As if this overgrown cactus were a talent he'd earned.* *Slop. Slop-slop.*

*Hate it. Hate every inch. Hate the way he sighs like it costs him something, the way his thigh shivers against my shoulder, the soft, grateful noise he makes when I do the thing with my tongue that I only learned because the barracks taught it with a collar and a clock.* *Schlurp.*

*Hold on to the hatred. It's the one thing in this room that's mine.* *Shlup.*

*Because here's what nobody has bothered to notice. The day this collar comes off, I could break this thing. One flick. One finger, the way I used to snap a car door off its hinges. A Val's body is armor everywhere but the places he forgot to guard. And he forgot, didn't he? Of course he did. He's got a lemon apron on a hook and a refrigerator he rewired for love.* *Sluuurp.*

*The collar says no. The collar says I'm a pretty ornament with a bell on every hinge. But the collar is a machine, and machines have seams. Slipstream told me that once, before the corridor, over a bad cup of coffee. "Every cage has a door, Nia. You just have to be patient enough to learn when the guard is looking at his own feet."* *Gulp-slurp.*

*So I'll be patient. I'll learn the rhythm of his breathing and the weight of his hand. I'll learn the tell in his shoulders when his guard drops. And one day, in a kitchen full of mismatched appliances, with a beer going warm on the table and nobody looking...*

*Slrp, slrp, slrp.*

*Just one flick. That's all I'd need.*

*Say it again.* *Slop.* *Tomatoes. Scotch bonnet. Thyme.*

*Amara. Chidi. Daniel.*

*Hold the line.*

---

*Shlup. Slrp.*

*Too much. That's the first complaint, and it's the biggest. Who portions a meal like this? Any sane cook would serve a woman an honest helping. Bog ladles it out like he's feeding a platoon and expects me to clear the plate.* *Glurp.*

*And it's thick. Thick as wet cement, as paste, as something that wants to set before it gets all the way down. I have to work at it. I have to take my time, and he calls that devotion.* *Slop. Slop-slop.*

*The smell, too. Pungent doesn't cover it. Pungent is a word for cheese. This is a mineral swamp that moved into my sinuses and put its feet up. And the flavor! It arrives like a brass band, loud and sweet and metallic all at once, so that I'm chewing the air for an hour afterward.* *Schlurp.*

*Then the weight of it. Every single time. My stomach sits like a bag of wet sand, and I can feel it when I stand, a slow, dumb, heavy fullness that follows me around the kitchen. The soldiers at the front never did that to me.* *Sloosh.*

*That's the part that offends me most. Four hundred and twelve of them, and not one served anything so excessive. The worst of them were brisk. Sloppy, vile, forgettable, but brisk. Bog, on the other hand, thinks he's an artist. He's actually proud of being a better pickle than the barracks pickles, as if he's mastered a craft.* *Slrp!*

*He is, without a doubt, the absolute worst.*

*And now the hand. There it is, right on schedule, a slow pass over my hair, the broad palm settling like a blanket. "Such a good girl. So eager today."* *Shlup.*

*Don't. Don't you dare.*

*...But I'm smiling. The top half of me is smiling. I can feel my cheeks lifting around my mouth, and my shoulders have loosened, and I'm working harder now, faster, with more enthusiasm than he's earned.* *Slurrrp. Shlup-shlup-shlup.*

*It's a ruse. Obviously. Look at him, eyes half-closed, breath going slow, every muscle in that vast green body gone slack. A man who thinks he's safe. That's what the smile is for. Let him melt. Let him sink into the cushions. Make him comfortable enough to forget what I am.*

*That's all this is. A trap, built carefully, one pat at a time.*

*Surely.*

*...Surely so.*

*Gulp-slurp.*

*Amara. Chidi. Daniel.*

*Tomatoes. Scotch bonnet. Thyme.*

*Hold the line.*

---

*Talent.*

*He said it with such warmth. A man handing out a participation ribbon. "A true calling." I put a Val tank on a satellite and I held Baltimore for nine days, and this is the line he picks for the plaque.* *Shlup.*

*Hold it in. Don't give him the barb. He's earned a dozen of the good ones, the ones I've been sharpening since the market, and every one of them is sitting behind my teeth right now like a loaded round.*

*Not yet.*

*A change of environment. Develop strengths I didn't know I had. He's talking about me the way Daniel's cousin used to talk about his tomato plants.* *Slrp.*

*And then Daniel. He had to bring Daniel in. As if my husband were an old appliance that happened to break. "What a waste." "A shame he won't get another chance." "That's life."* *Glurp.*

*That's not life, Bog. That's you, standing in my kitchen in my husband's place, patting my head and calling it philosophy.*

*Daniel never asked me for this. That's the whole answer to your little question, you overgrown cactus. He never asked, because he never needed to. He'd have been horrified to see a thing like you in the same room. And I'd have been horrified to learn that anyone could take what we had and turn it into a kitchen-floor curiosity with a chime attached.* *Slop.*

*Don't say it. Don't say any of it. If I open my mouth now, it'll come out as a scream, and he'll just pat my head again and murmur "such spirit," and that will be the end of me.*

*So I'll take the insult and swallow it, the way I swallow everything in this house, and I'll keep it down until it turns to iron.*

*But I'm not letting this one pass without a reply.*

*Let him have his gentle hand and his proud little speech. Let him lean back and sigh. I'm going to answer in the only language this idiot has ever respected.*

*Breathe in.*

*Every ounce of contempt I've got.*

*Every night I've lain awake counting.*

*Every four hundred and twelve.*

***Slurrrrrrrrrrrrrrp.***

*There. Choke on that, pickle.*

*Amara. Chidi. Daniel.*

*Hold the line.*
