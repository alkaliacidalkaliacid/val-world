## Chapter 96: Buried

The red light came down the dark the way dawn comes up it — except that nothing in the cavern had ever seen a dawn, and this was not that kind of light.

It found them where the dark had left them. Bog sat cross-legged on the stone, seven and a half feet of Val folded down into a posture that should not have been comfortable and plainly was, and Rin slept against him with her cheek laid along him like a pillow she had been given. The red light walked over them both and lit them up like a stage it had caught mid-performance.

Bog's face, in that light, was a map of the fight with the suit. One side of it had gone the deep plum of a bruise ripening; his nose sat at a slight, thoughtful angle to the rest of him and had bled at some point and stopped; the red glow showed the mottling all down his ribs where the possessed armor had pummeled him like a man tenderizing meat. He showed no sign of any of it. He never did. He sat in the red light with his hands loose on his knees and grinned — not the grin of a man defying a cosmic power, but the much smaller, much worse grin of a man who has been caught doing exactly what he looks like he was doing, and has decided, on balance, to be pleased about it.

Rin woke.

She woke the way she did everything lately, all at once and by touch first: warmth, a heartbeat against her cheek, the familiar salt of him still in her mouth. She rubbed her eyes with the heel of one hand, yawned enormously — and sat up.

Bog's cock, which had been pressed warm and heavy along her face the whole of her sleep, slid away from her cheek with the movement and settled against his thigh.

There was a silence in which Rin understood everything.

"—♥" What came out of her was not a word. It had the shape of a word the way a dropped tray has the shape of music. Her face went red so fast and so completely that in the red light it was almost invisible, which was the only mercy available, and she clutched her cheeks with both hands and held them, as if her face might otherwise leave. "I — I was — that's not — I didn't *mean* to fall asleep on—"

"Good morning," said Bog, comfortably, from above her, as if she had woken up at a breakfast table.

The Red Mediatoress looked at them.

It was a fleeting glance, no more — the red eyes passed over the bruised Val and the girl with her hands on her face the way weather passes over a roof, noting the shape of the thing without stopping for it — and then she went on, deeper into the cavern, the sash streaming behind her in its private wind, and the red light went with her, and left them in a dark that felt darker for having been lit.

Inside her, Priya was screaming.

Not aloud. Aloud was no longer available to her; her mouth belonged to the errand now, and her feet, and her hands folded so calmly at her sides. But in the small submerged room of herself, Priya Raghavan — nurse, wife of nineteen years, a woman who had bandaged half of Florence and buried a good share of the rest — had seen what the light had shown her, all of it, in the half-second of that passing glance: the girl. The *girl*, asleep with her face against that man's— and Bog, Bog the benefactor, Bog who had kept the clinic alive, sitting there *grinning* like a boy caught with his hand in— She could not finish the thought. She could not finish any thought. Her horror and her shame and her furious, useless embarrassment on Rin's behalf chased each other around the inside of her skull like things in a sack, and none of it reached her face, and her feet carried her on into the dark, unhurried, in a straight line, toward the thing she had come down here to finish.

The cavern narrowed, and then opened.

Bog and Rin caught up from behind — Bog unhurried, strolling in the red glow like a man out for an evening constitutional, Rin trotting to keep up with a stride twice hers, her embarrassment already dropping away behind her, because what was in front of her had got hold of her eyes and would not give them back.

At the heart of the cavern, where the backlash had come through — where the thing that had worn her suit, and then worn *her*, had first torn its way into the world — the stone was wrong. It cupped inward like the inside of a held breath, and in the cup of it, growing out of the rock at an angle no crystal should have grown, hung the singularity: a shard of purple gemstone the size of a man's forearm, lit from inside by a slow pulse, violet and patient, brightening and dimming like something asleep that was dreaming about waking.

Rin stopped breathing for a moment. She had seen its light all her life, in a sense — the purple pillar on the mountain, the glow that had split and sung — but she had never been this close to the root of it, and the root of it was beautiful, and the root of it was the worst thing she had ever seen, and both of those arrived in her at the same time and neither gave way.

Bog, beside her, reached out — casually, without looking, the way a man reaches for a railing — and made a grab at her backside.

Rin dodged. Nimbly. Without thinking about it at all: a skip, a twist, clean as a cat coming off a fence, and Bog's hand closed on cavern air. She blinked down at herself, astonished. Now her body answered her like it had been tuned. Whatever Bog had fed her down here, in the dark, under the liturgy — it was in her now, working, and some small scientific corner of her mind lit up with the thought even as the rest of her flushed: *it really does heal. It really does restore—*

Bog, thwarted, accepted defeat with the serenity of a man who had other options. He folded his arms, settled his weight, and turned his attention to the view the cavern was actually offering: the Red Mediatoress, bent to her work over the purple shard, and the generous, swaying architecture of Priya's backside, presented to him at working distance in crimson and black, utterly unguarded, while its owner finished the fate of the world. He admired it the way he admired sunsets and other people's dinners. Leisurely. A connoisseur denied a grape, consoling himself with the vineyard.

The Mediatoress raised one hand over the crystal.

The void opened the way it had opened at the mountain — no, not opened. The world *thinned*, in a shape around the shard, and the purple light went strange at the edges, and the crystal sank. Not down. Not in any direction that had a name. It sank *away*, into the thinning, its pulse slowing as it went — slower, and slower, and dimmer — the way a heartbeat settles in a body going under anesthesia, the way a light goes down a well. The stone cupped emptier and emptier around nothing at all. Rin's ears ached with a pressure that was leaving. Somewhere very far inside the rock there was a sound like a door, in a house the size of the world, being pulled gently shut.

The last of the violet went out.

The cavern was red now, only red, and then the red began to ebb out of the air as the sash settled and the burning of her eyes banked down to coals.

"Buried," said the Red Mediatoress.

She turned around. The red eyes found them — the bruised Val with his arms folded and his attention only just withdrawn from inappropriate places, the girl still flushed from dodging him, both of them standing in the new, ordinary dark of a cave that was only a cave again — and she looked at them the way she had looked at Nia on the mountainside: as an account about to be rendered.

"Next," she said, "it's time to deal with the humans."
