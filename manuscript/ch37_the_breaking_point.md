## Chapter 37: The Breaking Point


Sonya fought.

She kept fighting long after the laughter had hollowed her out. She was a warrior born of the Родина, and her body was a weapon that had been broken, rebuilt, and tempered by men who did not believe in surrender. She clawed at the cables. She bit down on the inside of her cheek until she tasted blood. She dug her heels into the floor and tried, again and again, to reach the ember beneath her sternum.

It did not matter. The tentacles had learned her too well.

They found their way past every defense she had left. They came as fine as threads, so fine she did not realize what was happening until the first one had already slipped inside her, and by then the second had followed. Inside, the threads twined together and fused, and they thickened as they went, slow and patient and thorough. Nothing about it was cruel. That was the worst part. It was *efficient*, a procedure carried out by a machine that had been told the goal was *entertaining*.

They drew out and carried away whatever waste her body held, and in its place sent a warm, thrumming rush of nutrients that made her limbs go heavy and her skull go light. Other tentacles worked the grime from her skin in long, slow strokes, over her shoulders, down her spine, behind her ears, and they never stopped tickling her. The tickling underlay everything, a bright, dizzying current that no thought could cut through.

A stifled laugh broke in her throat. It tumbled into something else halfway out, a high, ragged note that she did not recognize as her own voice.

Her back bowed. The world went white.

For an instant there was nothing at all, no cold, no cable, no collar-voiced Val bastard in her ears. Then the blackness came, soft and absolute, and she slid into it gratefully.

It did not last.

She woke to the same hollow, the same slow rocking, the same impossible patience, and a tentacle already tracing the arch of her foot as if it had only paused for her to catch up. A sound escaped her before she could stop it. Her hands closed on empty air.

*No,* she thought, very faintly. *Not again. I won't…*

The second time, she did not even have the strength to be ashamed.

---

In the pod wedged in the creature's belly, Bog had begun to hum. The laughter echoing through the walls had grown quieter and stranger, and he wasn't sure whether it was still laughter. He tilted his head, frowned, and decided it was none of his business.

"How long?" he asked.

"Ground contact in two to the first barium half-lives, Commander," the voice said.

"Is she still alive?"

"Affirmative. Vital signs elevated but stable."

"Good." Bog stretched his good arm behind his head and watched the glowing sky rush up to meet them.
