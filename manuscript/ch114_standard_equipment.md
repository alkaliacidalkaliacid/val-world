## Chapter 114: Standard Equipment

The questions came from all over the compartment.

Nia first. She had the toolkit case across her knees — the case she had carried over a mountain, the whole length of the road, to a dead man. "My toolkit," she said. "Is it any use at all?"

"Any Val can use a Class 5 toolkit to remove a system, or to alter its settings," said the monster.

"Then teach *me*."

"Only the Val can use the toolkit," said the monster. "You cannot. Not yet."

Nia's jaw tightened. She let it pass, because the rest mattered more, but she let it pass the way a person sets down something heavy in a place they mean to come back to.

"So the powers aren't gone?" Rin said. She was sitting up straight now, all attention, a mechanic who had just heard an engine turn over in a machine everyone else had given up for scrap. "They're just — off?"

"Superpowers can be restored by adjusting the settings, without removing the collar," said the monster. "They can also be amplified. A burst can be triggered. At present, the system is in accumulation mode."

"Accumulation," Priya said. Her voice was the clinic voice, level and exact. "The power it has accumulated — will it short-circuit?"

"It will not cause a short circuit," said the monster.

"Right," said Bog. "Fine. Let's get it working, then." He took the case from Nia — she surrendered it with both hands and visible reluctance — and opened it on his knees. "Talk me through it."

The monster talked him through it. Bog listened with the expression of a man being read a contract in a language he had once known a man who spoke. Panels. Clasps. A sequence that had to be done in order and undone in a different one. He got the second step wrong twice.

"Here," Rin said. She had heard perhaps four instructions. She leaned across and put her hands over his, unhesitating, the way she did everything with a machine. "This one first. Then it wants *this*. See? It's obvious."

The toolkit unfolded.

It came apart and came together in the same motion, segments flowing like water finding a level, and snapped shut around Bog's forearm — a broad, close-fitted wristband of Val make, seamless, final.

Bog looked at it. He turned his arm over. He shook it, gently, the way a man shakes a sleeve with something in it.

"Ugh," he said. "This is annoying. Tell me how to take it off."

"No," said the monster.

Bog blinked at the wall. "Huh?"

"There is no way to remove it," said the monster. "However, a Class 5 toolkit is standard equipment for a Commander, so it is not an issue."
