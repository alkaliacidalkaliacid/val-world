## Chapter 106: From Above

A bell chimed in the darkness — one of hers, swinging as she moved.

Nia went down the way she had gone down for what felt like forever: gasping for air in the thin cold, one hand on the stone, feeling her way forward a half-step at a time. The seam had narrowed to a path, and the path to a suggestion, and still she climbed down it, because below her was Rin, and Bog, and whatever had worn Priya's face down there.

There had been light, once, to climb by. The purple light had come up out of the depths and shown her the stone. Then, without any fuss, it had changed its mind and turned red. Then the red had gone out, the way a lamp goes out, and left her with the dark and her hands.

She should have turned back the moment it vanished. Any sensible woman would have climbed up into the daylight, sat down, and waited for somebody to come and explain the world to her.

Instead, she inched onward.

Her fist slammed into a stalagmite. It burst apart under the blow — a crack, a clatter, fragments tumbling away down into the black and falling for a long, long time before they stopped being audible. She stood there breathing hard, stung and furious, with her knuckles smarting.

How powerless she had become.

There was a time when she had only to call, and the mountains had answered. The spirits had shown her the path the way you show a path to family — gladly, and before she finished asking. A time when a cliff in the dark was a road to her, and the stones themselves had opinions about where she ought to step.

All of it gone. All of it the fault of one overgrown cactus.

He had reduced her to this. He had let her believe him dead — buried him in her heart, grieved him, carried it up a mountain — and then he had come back, alive, grinning, entirely unbothered, and promptly leapt back down into the abyss after a runaway girl, as if the abyss were a place he kept an appointment.

Everything—

She gave up on caution altogether, and the mountain took its fee for that at once. Her foot found nothing where the path had been. She went over flailing, hands snatching at stone that wasn't there, and then she was falling in earnest, down through the black in a tumble — and training older than the collar took her over: she curled, tucked, and spun in mid-air, neat as a cat dropped off a fence.

*Oh, for heaven's sake…*

Far below her, small and green and steady, a chemical glow burned in the dark.

Strong arms caught her.

They took her out of the fall the way a wall takes a thrown ball — completely, and with a low, guttural groan from somewhere down in a chest that no human ribcage had ever been built around.

"Holy Val," he remarked, conversational as a man greeting weather. "This cave isn't half bad. A stunning beauty just fell from above."
