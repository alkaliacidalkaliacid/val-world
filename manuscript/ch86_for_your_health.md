## Chapter 86: For Your Health

Bog was being driven into a corner, and it was an embarrassing feeling.

He had been in a great many fights. He had survived the front, a tank division's worth of angry superheroes, and a Val drill sergeant with a grudge, and he was fairly sure he had never been outclassed this thoroughly by a girl with a ponytail. The possessed suit hit like a falling building. One steel hand closed on his coat and *lifted* him, and he saw the cavern wall rush at his face, and the world went white and rattled. Then the other hand caught him, and he was thrown the other way. Again. A wall. A boulder. A heap of rib bones, which cracked and scattered under him like kindling. His nose burst. He tasted green blood, hot and salty, running over his lip.

"*Clobber!*" the doubled, trebled, tin-bell voice roared. "*Clobber! Clobber!*"

Another wall. His ribs gave a warning creak.

*Any more of this,* thought Bog, hanging in the air with his boots a meter from the floor, *and I'll start coming apart.*

The suit had its cockpit exposed. It was, in fact, the most exposed thing about it, a clean pane of cracked glass in the middle of its chest, with a small, soft, warm human body inside. Any Val soldier of any rank or breeding would have seen it in an instant. One fist through the glass. One squeeze. And the whole thing would stop, and the problem would be solved, and the paperwork would be minimal.

Bog looked at the glass.

He looked at the two glowing violet eyes behind it, and under them the small stubborn jaw, the oversized goggles pushed up into a lopsided ponytail, and he remembered the way she had laughed on the updraft.

*Nope,* he decided.

It was not noble. He would have been the first to admit it. Other Val might not have hesitated, but he, Bog, a provisional reservist, a man with a firm grip on his own limitations, simply could not do it. It was like being asked to eat a puppy. The thought made his stomach turn.

The suit dropped him.

He hit the floor on his hands and knees, spat a mouthful of blood, and groaned, "Right. Right. Okay." He forced one leg under him. Then the other. He rose, swaying, a great green silhouette in the glare of violet light, with his coat in rags and his fists loose at his sides.

If only he had a weapon. He had thrown the laser into a ravine. He had left the rolling pin in Florence. He patted his belt, hopefully, and his fingers brushed against something cold and smooth and curved.

The collar.

One of the two he had lifted from Hermes's shed. A heavy gray band of alien alloy, dead weight, not a weapon at all, merely a thing that had once made a great many people very sad.

*Holy Val,* thought Bog. *All or nothing.*

The suit came at him again, arms spread wide. He did not back away. Instead, at the very last instant, he did a thing no sensible Val had ever done in the history of the Dominion. He *dropped*, flat on his belly, and slid. He slid under the swinging steel arms, under the pistoning knees, through a spray of gravel and a gust of purple heat, and came up beneath the great chest of the machine, inches from the glass. He put one hand on the shattered pane. He tore it away. And in the same movement, with the speed of a man who has spent six years practicing exactly this on other people's necks, he reached in and snapped the collar around Rin's throat.

*Click.*

The suit collapsed.

It did not fall so much as *fold*, in one long gasp, the lights going out in its seams, the plated limbs drooping, the great frame sagging to its knees like a puppet with its strings cut. Bog stepped back, panting, one hand pressed to his ribs.

*Success,* he thought.

But Rin came out of the cockpit.

She came out like something fired from a gun, in one long leap, her hands curled into claws and the collar gleaming at her throat, and she hit him square in the chest and bore him to the floor. Her eyes were the wrong color. Her teeth were bared. Her breath came in short shrieking gusts. She was *shaking*, he realized, head to foot, as if two very large and very angry hands were pulling her in opposite directions, and one of the hands was made of metal and the other of light. The collar was struggling to smother her. The purple was struggling to keep her. And between them was a twenty-year-old girl who weighed about as much as a sack of flour.

"*Clo... bber...*" she said, through her teeth. And then, thin and high and desperate, in her own voice, "*Lucky Val... help...*"

Bog growled low in his throat.

It was a bad contest. He could see it. The collar was strong, but it was only a collar; the purple was a hundred thousand years of dead things, and it was winning. If this went on much longer, she would not hold. Her heart would give out, or her mind would. He could *feel* the thin little bones of her wrists beginning to buckle in his grip.

*Then,* thought Bog, *I'll just have to tip the scales in the collar's favor.*

He did not know quite why he thought so. Something he had read, long ago, in the dusty back pages of a Dominion handler's manual: *in cases of collar instability, bond-calm by direct contact.* Hands, preferably. But his hands were full. He had a vague, hazy, unreliable memory that there was a second method, in a footnote, for emergencies. He was almost certain it had involved a diagram.

He rolled over, pinned her firmly, gently, face-up against the cold stone floor, with both of her wrists in one large palm and the other hand cupped behind her head so that it would not strike the rock. He looked down into her blazing violet eyes. "Sorry about this," said Bog, quite sincerely. "It's for your health."

Then he leaned in and kissed her.

It was not a long kiss. It was brief and awkward, the kiss of a man performing an unfamiliar maneuver from a manual he had half read. He held her there, with his nose bleeding down onto her chin, and counted slowly in his head to five.

The collar flared. A ring of cold white light ran round it, from the clasp to the nape and back, and for one long, aching second, nothing happened.

Then the purple *screamed*.

It came out of her mouth. It poured from her nose and her ears and the corners of her eyes in long, thin, writhing threads of violet smoke, shrieking, thrashing, tearing loose from her like a nail from a plank. It fled across the cavern in a long streak and plunged into the dark, and the light in her eyes went out. Her body went slack. Her head fell back against his palm.

Bog lifted his face.

Rin lay on the stone beneath him with her mouth open and her eyes closed, breathing, a pale thread of blood across her cheek. She looked very small.

"...Hm," said Bog, and wiped his nose with the back of his hand. "I think it worked. I think that's what the diagram was."

Her eyelids fluttered.

And then, very slowly, with a long, shuddering breath, they opened. They were brown. Wide brown eyes, bleary and bewildered, staring up at a bleeding green face a hand's breadth from her own.

"...Lucky Val?" said Rin, in a very small voice. "Did you just kiss me?"
