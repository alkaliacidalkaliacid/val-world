## Chapter 64: Test First, Verify Afterward


The bear gave Bog no chance to strike another pose. A single sweep of its forepaw slammed him flat against the floorboards, and the boards cracked under him. One heavy paw settled onto his chest and pressed down, as though it were stamping out a cigarette.

"Oof. Okay. That one's on me."

Priya came skidding back into the room with the laser in both arms. It was a squat, ugly thing, a hand-welded jumble of Val casing and hospital cabling, with a strip of masking tape across the side that read DO NOT USE in a neat engineer's hand. She did not stop to explain. She pushed it into her husband's hands and ran for the girls.

Arjun held a doctorate in engineering from Georgia Tech, where his specialty had been the design of advanced medical devices, and in the course of that education he had acquired one habit above all others. When confronted with the unknown, a man tested first and verified afterward. He did not stop to wonder whether the situation was a proper occasion for testing. He turned the output dial to its maximum, put the emitter against his shoulder, and sighted along the barrel, whose safety systems were still undergoing repairs.

"Hypothesis," he muttered, "is that this will work."

He fired.

The beam was a thin white line, and it passed through the bear's torso with a sound like tearing silk. A hole the width of a coin opened behind the shoulder. Purple light burst from the wound in a rush, like steam out of a ruptured pipe, and the aura came off the animal's back in thick, shrieking ribbons. The bear lurched forward with a bellow that was now clearly two voices, one of them a dying animal's, and it threw itself across the room at the man holding the weapon.

Arjun did not run. His knuckles went white on the grip.

"Second trial," he said.

The second shot took the animal through the chest. The third took out the knee. Paws that could have taken off a human face skidded across the floor, scattering plates and curry, and stopped a yard from Arjun's bare feet. The great body sagged and collapsed onto the ruins of the dining table, and then it lay still.

A last wisp of purple rose from the open wound and thinned into the air.

Under the bear's paw, Bog drew a long breath.

"Does anybody," he wheezed, "want to get this thing off me?"

Arjun looked at the laser. The casing was too hot to hold, and a thread of smoke was curling up from the grip. He lowered it carefully to the floor.

"Results," he said, a little dazed, "were consistent with expectations."

Then he sat down hard in the middle of the broken room and started to shake.
