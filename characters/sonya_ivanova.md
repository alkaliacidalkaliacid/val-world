## Core (does not change)
- Role: Main cast. Supervillain (Pale Murderess), 29, Russian. Former operator, FSB Alpha Special Operations Unit; defected after murdering her superior officer.
- Desire (what they want across the whole story): [NOT STATED in her documents - she is pragmatic and serves no cause; in captivity her competitiveness has latched onto the monster's leaderboard and the "secondary consort" title, which she outwardly rejects.]
- Lack / wound: Not a wound, and not rootlessness: a mismatch of premises. Other people treat this world as the one unshakeable thing and build their lives on rooting themselves in it; having known the other side since infancy, Sonya has never shared that premise, and cannot organise her values around it — hence, in part, her lack of loyalty to heroes or villains alike (see Background).
- Strength, and how it backfires: Elite special-forces training (firearms, hand-to-hand, infiltration, assassination, reconnaissance, tactics) plus superhuman strength, durability, speed, agility, reflexes and senses - and dimensional portals (below). Her betrayal of Bog and Nia at the depot is what delivered her to the monster.
- Her rage at Bog and Nia during the depot fight (user, 2026-11-17): her killing intent is already escalating before any pod is taken from anyone - a grudge, and a misplaced one, since she is the one who betrayed them. The pod-vanish in ch29 is therefore double-edged: she secures her own escape *and*, in the same act, snatches the pod away from in front of them - stranding them to die is itself an expression of her killing intent, not a side effect. And the pod Bog takes from her in ch30 was one she had stolen from them first, so objectively it is a reclaiming, not a robbery; her own sense of having been robbed of it exists but is minor - a small thing next to the rage itself, not its driver. Her ch30 death threat ("Тебя убью!") flows from that pre-existing, escalating rage.
- What they're lying about / don't realize about themselves: She repeats "Не сдамся" ("I won't surrender") while no longer knowing whether it is true. Her slips (humming the anthem, saying "Вторая") are logged - or mercifully not logged - by the monster.

## Background / Personal History
> Reference material — include in a context pack only when a chapter touches her past.
> Built in consultation with the user, 2026-11-19, on four axes: military service, the underworld career, the other-side experience, and her Russianness. All items confirmed by the user, 2026-11-19.

- **Military service.** Born in Novosibirsk, Siberia, to ordinary civilians (father an electrical engineer at a municipal heating plant, mother a schoolteacher). After the portal incident (below) she was raised attending a government research-medical institution for superpower studies, then entered the military and rose to the FSB Alpha Special Operations Unit. Her record there was excellent, and it remains a genuine point of pride. Her superior officer, an unpowered man, insulted her and persistently undervalued her abilities and her record; behind the friction lay her accumulated grievance at being used by the military as a convenient weapon. At the limit, in the middle of an argument, she kicked him away, forced his head into a portal, and closed it. She defected immediately after.
- **The underworld career.** She became an assassin working the underworld of the former Soviet sphere, from Eastern Europe to the Far East, occasionally travelling to Western Europe and America on jobs. She keeps several professional partners, all ex-Soviet like her, held in conditional trust and something like friendship: a fixer in Almaty who never sells her for money; a document forger in Minsk who makes all her passports; a former spotter in Vladivostok, the one person she lets watch her back — conditionally. When the Val invaded Eurasia she did what the smart underworld did: ignored the Accord and sat the war out in safe, non-urban strongholds — a decommissioned Soviet underground complex in the Kazakh steppe, a drug syndicate's mountain stronghold in the Pamirs.
- **The other-side experience.** As an infant she was swallowed by a portal during a federal portal experiment in her home city; she came back out by opening a portal herself, and her abilities date from that event. She has gone on experiencing the alien world beyond the portals ever since; its details are deliberately not established. The effect is internal to her, not a product of how anyone treated her: most people treat this world as the single unshakeable ground and devote themselves to rooting in it and living accordingly; she has never been able to share that premise, and she does not experience the mismatch as deprivation. It is why the Val Dominion's overturning of the world does not shake her — her foundations were never laid in this world alone.
- **Her Russianness.** What holds her is the land — her rodina. For a woman whose other-side experience makes her prone to losing her grip on the real world, the Russian land is the anchor that ties her life down: frozen earth, mountains, deep forest — the winter ice and Shaman Rock of Baikal, the Dead Forest at Tolbachik, the empty Ukok Plateau, the Putorana, the Lena Pillars. She does not indulge in nature sentimentally; she simply belongs to it in a way she belongs to nothing human. Russian society she holds at arm's length; the land is exempt, and the Val's destruction of it disgusts her. America, by contrast, means nothing to her — she is there on a contract, and what becomes of it is not her concern. She is not a complete alien: her Russianness lives in the land and in work done well, not in society or the state.
- The thin white scar through her eyebrow is from the Lancet — the same enemy who gave Nia her facial scar, whose cuts refuse healing.
- Private life: A lesbian; her relationships with women are short-term, she sometimes torments her partners — her desire is bound up with hurting, even killing — and she parts without clinging. Her everyday life is stoic; luxury, when she allows it, follows one philosophy — equipment, not display: quality legible only to those who know, no logos, monochrome tailoring (Max Mara for coats, The Row, Jil Sander; a single Jaeger-LeCoultre Reverso, a watch with a hidden second face; Valmont skincare; Russian Oscietra eaten plain; hotel suites chosen with two exits). She watches ballet for the dancers' movement — the athletic side, trained bodies at their limit — not for the stage art or the story.

## Powers: Dimensional Portals
- Creates glowing disc-shaped portals to another dimension; she freely designates entrance and exit, and the direction of emergence. Transfers matter, energy and any physical phenomenon.
- Selective transference: she decides what each portal passes or blocks - radio waves and radar, light and radiation, magnetic fields, bullets, objects, energy attacks.
- Spherical portal field ("stealth mode"): a sphere of countless small portal discs bends detection and attacks around her (spatial redirection, not invisibility). The discs are small and form a single field; the "four / six" figures under Limits count only the additional portals she runs on top of this field, and those figures are Nia's estimate, not a measured cap. The transit tunnel in ch22 is an application of stealth mode: a moving field that sheds Val surveillance and cosmic radiation while air bleeding through from the other dimension supplies pressure and thrust. (Ruled by the user 2026-11-17, ch22 review.)
- Uses: short-range teleportation, passing through barriers, ambushes, redirecting attacks, severing objects by snapping a portal shut, dropping objects on targets, mirror-discs to remove blind spots, sound-muffling discs.
- Presentation note (user, 2026-11-17): her menace is portal-based only. She does not crumble or disintegrate matter by proximity/aura - a paper-to-dust effect in an early draft of ch19 was a leftover from before her ability was finalized, and was deleted at the user's direction.
- Limits: portals open only in her immediate surroundings (no long-distance teleport). The upper limit of portal size is unknown - deliberately left undetermined (user, 2026-11-17). There is no person-size cap: in the tentacle-monster battle she opened a single disc wide enough to cleave the creature nearly in half (ch28). What scales with size is the cost - larger portals demand more effort, and size and count strain her stamina and concentration; exhaustion (sweat, nosebleed, trembling) can collapse them. A large spherical field is especially demanding. The figures "four (usual) / six (extreme)" are NOT a confirmed limit: they are Nia's in-story estimate of how many additional portals Sonya can handle while maintaining her stealth-mode field (ch26, Nia's interior: "Four was her ceiling when she wanted precision, or when she needed to keep the sphere up around us. Now she's dropped the sphere."). The stealth field's own small discs are effectively countless and are not part of that count (in the tentacle-monster battle she opens portals beyond counting). Record the numbers as Nia's conjecture, never as canon fact. (Clarified by the user 2026-11-17, ch22 review.)
- The null-collar suppresses her portal abilities. Her portals could not find a "core" in the tentacle monster.
