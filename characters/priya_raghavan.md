# Character Card: Priya Raghavan (Avatāra of the Red Mediatoress)

> Created 2026-11-17 from `archive/red_mediatoress.md` and the Bog & Nia summary.
> Current situation lives in 02_current_state.yaml, not here.

## Core (does not change)
- Role: Main cast (newest). Human, 43, of Indian descent. Nurse at the Free Clinic in Florence, Alabama - in truth closer to a doctor/PhD; her specialty is superhuman medicine ("hero medicine"), trained at Emory University, and some of that knowledge applies to Val physiology.
- Family: Husband Arjun (PhD in engineering, Georgia Tech, advanced medical devices; helped Dr. Hermes analyze Val medical gear). **Married 19 years** (canon, ruled by the user 2026-11-18; Priya married at 24). Two teenage daughters, Kavya (younger) and Meera (elder). Mother-in-law Dadi, openly hostile to Bog.
- Desire (what they want across the whole story): Before the manifestation, she researched heroes and her own lifelong "sight," unfinished. Now: carry out the Mediatoress's mission (below) and keep it secret.
- Lack / wound: She has seen things since childhood - shapes that were not there, people in empty rooms - and was teased for it; she long wondered whether she was losing her mind or had a power. The war interrupted her research before she reached a conclusion; the materials were lost in Atlanta, and she has since let the question go (ruled 2026-11-19).
- Strength, and how it backfires: Timid, nervous, stuttering in ordinary conversation, with unexpected intensity about spirits - and brave when it counts: she fought to pry Dadi's fingers off her daughter, and stopped the Mediatoress from erasing Bog because he had brought medicine to the children.

## Background / Personal History
> Reference material — include in a context pack only when a chapter touches her past.
> Built in consultation with the user, 2026-11-19 (user rulings and adopted
> proposals, as noted), and organised on three axes: (1) the woman who sees,
> (2) the medical life, (3) family and two lands. Cutting across all three is
> her empiricism of persons — she judges people by what they do, not by the
> category they belong to (see Politics, axis 3).

### Axis 1 — The woman who sees
- **The sight, from earliest memory (user ruling, 2026-11-19):** She has seen
  strange things for as long as she can remember — no onset, no first incident
  (this fixes the manuscript's "since I was a child," ch058). She is, in fact,
  the Red Mediatoress's born Avatāra (see The Red Mediatoress, below); the
  sight is that nature showing. As a small child she was taken for an
  imaginative, fantasy-prone girl; as she grew, the sight combined with her
  stutter and she came to be regarded as a little abnormal, and the devout in
  her community offered karma and such explanations. Her parents, in a world
  where superheroes exist, had her assessed at a hospital — but a power that
  lifts a car can be measured, while a thing only she can see and hear cannot
  be judged either way. The assessment settled nothing: neither illness nor
  power was ever established.
- **School (adopted, 2026-11-19):** Quiet, top of her class, a library child.
  Oral presentations terrified her; early on she decided to win on paper
  instead. By middle school she had learned to hide the sight — her strategic
  silence about what she sees, choosing when to speak, dates from there. Her
  friends were few and deep; she was the one who tutored classmates.
- **Faith (user ruling + adopted, 2026-11-19):** Priya is Hindu, with real
  faith; growing up she took diligent part in the community's festivals and
  rites. Practice is home- and festival-centred rather than weekly and
  congregational — observant, not exceptional, in the Indian-American
  context. Her faith is the lived framework her sight and her Mediatoress
  vocabulary grow out of, not a hobby. The rebuilt Florence house has a small
  shrine; a lamp and a prayer in the morning, before the clinic, are how her
  day begins — her grandmother's pattern. It is why spirits and bhuta are
  not, to her, horrors first: they are beings to be dealt with properly, and
  manners come before fear — the source of her calm when she judges such
  things. She speaks politely to presences no one else can see ("just a
  moment, please"); in her house the sight is part of her system of manners.

### Axis 2 — The medical life
- **The road to Emory (adopted, 2026-11-19):** The inconclusive hospital
  assessment is the hinge of her education. A conventional medical career was
  open to her — the grades, the family (physicians and pharmacists on both
  sides; see axis 3), the expectation — and she took the family's medicine in
  the one direction that might answer her own case: superhuman medicine,
  trained at Emory, in her home city. Her research into heroes and awakenings
  (how they awakened, what they had in common, whether any of them saw as she
  does) was the personal side of the same question. Her undergraduate
  institution is deliberately not specified.
- **A life inside universities (adopted, 2026-11-19):** Her whole adult life,
  until the invasion at thirty-seven, was spent inside universities —
  undergraduate, graduate work, then Emory research and clinical staff. She
  never had a non-academic job until the Florence clinic: a woman formed by
  institutions and peer review, dropped into improvised refugee medicine.
- **The research is gone (user ruling, 2026-11-19):** Her research materials
  were lost in Atlanta, and she has let the question go — her interest is no
  longer there. The clinic and the household are where her attention lives
  now. (The "unfinished research" is abandoned-by-her, not merely
  interrupted.)
- **The Free Clinics (canon + adopted, 2026-11-19):** The Clinics are not a
  founded organisation so much as something that spread on its own — medical
  people, in town after town, trying somehow to help the refugees (00_canon).
  Priya's own path into the Florence clinic is deliberately not specified
  more finely; Hermes was her colleague there, Arjun helped him analyse
  captured Val gear, and that working triangle is all the card fixes. She is
  exceptionally good at remembering people's names and family structures,
  and keeps her own notebook of Florence's patient families: a person who
  counts people and does not drop them.

### Axis 3 — Family and two lands
- **Origin (user ruling, 2026-11-19):** Third-generation Indian-American,
  raised in Decatur (DeKalb County, the Atlanta metro's oldest South Asian
  community, near Emory). Her natal family is comfortably well-off — affluence
  expressed as education, a paid-for house in the Decatur / Druid Hills area,
  and community standing, not display. Her natal family's jati is higher than
  Arjun's family's; that relative ranking is all that is established, the
  specific jatis are not named.
- **Grandparents and parents (adopted, 2026-11-19):** The grandparents are
  the immigrant generation, arrived in the late 1960s / early 1970s in the
  first professional wave around Georgia Tech and Emory — grandfather in
  engineering / university research, grandmother the keeper of the
  household's religious and community life. Her parents (American-born):
  father a physician in the Emory orbit, mother a pharmacist, later
  part-owner of a pharmacy — medicine as inheritance on both sides, hero
  medicine her one deviation.
- **Sisters (adopted, 2026-11-19):** Four sisters in total, Priya the second
  daughter. The others are socially fluent in the way she is not — the eldest
  the family's polished representative (law / corporate), the younger sisters
  quicker, funnier, earlier to marry within the community. Priya out-scored
  all of them at every stage and was still the one adults had to wait for to
  finish a sentence — the family's pride and its embarrassment in one person,
  and the root of her habit of apologising before she speaks. Names and exact
  careers are left open until needed.
- **Her natal family now (user ruling, 2026-11-19, as corrected the same
  day):** Her parents, at least, are alive. They joined the Free Clinics, and
  it is through that network that Priya and Arjun know they are safe. Her
  three sisters' fate is not established and remains open.
- **Meeting Arjun (user ruling, 2026-11-19):** They met through their
  professors — her Emory hero-medicine professor and Arjun's Georgia Tech
  medical-devices professor were close friends, their labs in regular contact
  (real-world grounding: Emory and Georgia Tech run biomedical engineering as
  a single joint department, Coulter BME, est. 1997). The working occasion: a
  prototype that needed a clinician who understood superhuman patients to
  test it. An academic introduction, not a community arrangement — which is
  how a marriage across the jati gap came about through two doctoral students
  working together. Married at 24, nineteen years ago (canon, 2026-11-18).
- **Arjun and Dadi (user rulings, 2026-11-19):** Arjun married her in
  disregard of the reputation her stutter and her strange sense had given
  her in the community; what he fell for was her intelligence and her
  character. Dadi at first was against the match when she heard Priya
  stuttered, but meeting the woman changed her mind, and Priya is now a
  daughter-in-law Dadi truly treasures. The respect runs both ways. Dadi's
  hostility to Bog is not only that he is Val: she dislikes, on principle, an
  unrelated man coming into the house and being on familiar terms with Priya,
  and she has heard how the Val treat women (authorial note: her insight on
  this point is, in fact, one hundred percent correct). She accepted his
  visit at all only because Priya had brought him home as the Free Clinic's
  benefactor — Dadi bent her own rule out of respect for Priya. The two women
  also oil each other's hair: their respect in its unspoken form.
- **The "good Val" question (authorial stance, 2026-11-19 — deliberately
  unresolved):** Whether Priya is right to see Bog as a "good Val" is a hard
  question and is not to be settled either way. He is plainly unlike other
  Val, and in his own way he works for humanity — but he is still Val. What
  is not in doubt is the fact underneath her view: his contribution did, in
  actual fact, save Florence (the clinic kept alive on his supplies; the
  bhuta night).
- **Memories with Arjun (adopted, 2026-11-19):** Three fixed points. The
  prototype he tested on himself, and her genuine fury — the incident that
  set the pattern of their marriage. The day she understood what kind of man
  he was: he had learned exactly how sweet she takes her chai — courtship in
  his language, by act, not words. And the first Diwali after the wedding,
  the exhausting double round of her higher-jati family and his, and the
  turning point when it became something they could laugh about.
- **Cooking and the household (user ruling, 2026-11-19):** Priya has no real
  philosophy of cooking. Before the marriage, she and Arjun were alike in
  this — two graduate students eating whatever, carelessly, both of them.
  Dadi was somewhat exasperated, but a daughter-in-law who knew nothing was a
  blank slate to teach, and the teaching never became a conflict — if
  anything, the blank slate suited Dadi. To this day Priya is busy at the
  Free Clinic and Dadi carries much of the running of the household.
- **The daughters (adopted, 2026-11-19):** Meera, the elder, is the reserved
  one — wary, sparing with words, her gestures doing the talking (in ch060 she
  slides the raita an inch toward Bog without looking at him, which in her
  language is a treaty); she watches over the younger. Kavya, the younger, is
  blunt and direct — fork held like a weapon, the point-blank "Do you have a
  mother?", then bursting out laughing at his belch; the child's
  unguardedness is still in her. Both were small children when the family
  fled Atlanta, so their memories of it are fragments; Florence is, in
  effect, their hometown. In Atlanta Priya took them to Your DeKalb Farmers
  Market (tastings as small adventures), the dinosaurs at Fernbank, the
  Georgia Aquarium, Stone Mountain, library story times, and walks on the
  Emory campus. Meera drew and built things; Kavya climbed and lived for
  water. At Diwali they helped make the sweets and played at wrapping
  themselves in her saris. And the core of her motherhood: she read aloud to
  them — a woman with a stutter, reading slowly at home, and nobody hurrying
  her. Home is the place where her sentences are waited for, which is the
  same thread as Arjun.
- **Refugees (user ruling, 2026-11-19):** The Raghavan household are
  themselves among the refugees: Priya, Arjun, the daughters, and Dadi were
  displaced by the thorough destruction of Atlanta and resettled in Florence
  (world facts in 00_canon.md). The improvised household Bog dines with in
  ch060 — a door laid across sawhorses for a table — is a refugee family's
  rebuilt home, not an old Florence family's.
- **Dress (adopted, 2026-11-19):** In Atlanta she divided it by setting:
  Western dress (blouses, trousers, lab coat) at the university and hospital;
  the sari at home, in the community, and for festivals. The sari became her
  everyday dress in Florence through the double pressure of a refugee's
  limited wardrobe and a choice — not letting displacement decide who she is.
- **Screen and music (adopted, 2026-11-19):** The films of her youth are the
  1990s Bollywood she watched with her sisters (the DDLJ generation); she
  still rewatches them for comfort and cries at the crying scenes without
  embarrassment. American medical dramas she watched professionally and
  complained about — the hospitals are wrong. Tamil films are a later layer,
  acquired through Arjun's family. As a child she was made to take Carnatic
  vocal lessons; they never took, but the ear stayed. Bhajans are her
  grandmother's layer in the house. Her own private taste is old film songs,
  hummed while she works, and she is broadly omnivorous about sincere
  singing — gospel and soul included.
- **Politics (adopted, 2026-11-19):** Before the war, a moderate professional
  liberal of Atlanta: healthcare access first, and as a clinician sceptical
  of hero regulation. Underneath the positions sits her empiricism of
  persons (see the headnote): why she can see Bog by his record while Dadi
  judges by nature, and why they will never fully agree about him. Since the
  war her trust in grand politics is gone; her remaining measure is who is
  feeding people.
- **Atlanta places (adopted, 2026-11-19):** Her haunts were concrete: the
  weekly family trip to Your DeKalb Farmers Market in Decatur, the Indian
  grocery, the sweets-and-chai shop. Her nostalgia for the lost city lives in
  specifics like the smell of that market, not in abstractions. She has a
  private sweet tooth — very sweet chai, jaggery sweets — with a small stash
  of her own hidden in a clinic drawer.
- **Florence comforts, plants, animals (adopted, 2026-11-19):** Florence is
  a Tennessee River town, and walking the river, watching the water, is her
  small way of recovering herself — the walk home from the clinic is the one
  stretch of the day that is hers alone. Porch evenings, chai, neighbours she
  knows by face: a Decatur community childhood makes "a neighbourhood that
  knows you" medicinal to her. She is quietly grateful the public library
  survived, and she has let the new place's small goods in — peach season, a
  hummingbird feeder. She grows marigolds from seed every year; getting them
  to bloom is a declaration that the festivals happen here too. A tulsi plant
  lives in a pot and comes indoors for winter — her grandmother's household
  layer, carried through the displacement in plant form. Her curry-leaf
  plant is the most fussed-over thing she owns, frail in Alabama winters.
  Jasmine's scent is the trigger for her natal home; honeysuckle on the
  riverbank is Georgia summers. No pets in Atlanta — both families were
  practical about animals. In Florence a stray cat attached itself to the
  clinic and, at the daughters' urging, was half-adopted: the first new life
  the family took in after the flight. She is unafraid of animals, and her
  order of care — food and water first — applies to them as to people. She
  feeds birds, and a cardinal at the feeder is a small, definite pleasure.

## The Red Mediatoress
> Design note (meta, user, 2026-11-19): the Red Mediatoress's image draws
> strongly on **Durga** — the great goddess, red-clad, cosmic feminine power
> (shakti) that is neither simply benevolent nor safely categorised, wields
> the gods' weapons, and descends as avatāra when the world's order breaks.
> This is the author's image source for the entity, not an in-world fact
> characters know.
- **Priya is the Mediatoress's born Avatāra (user ruling, 2026-11-19):** she
  did not acquire or begin hosting the entity at any point in her life — she
  was born its incarnation. Her lifelong "sight" is the innate expression of
  that nature, present for as long as she can remember because it was never
  not there. (This supersedes the card's older "host" framing.)
- A cosmic being that speaks through Priya's own lips; Priya is in a sense part of it. Asked if it is a hero, it answers: neither Dharmatman nor Papatman, neither deva nor asura, nearer to Brahman - then corrects itself to "a cosmic being."
- Manifestation: a red aura erupts when Priya is in extreme distress; her sari burns away, leaving a crimson-and-black costume (gold band at the waist, wide red sash streaming in a wind that is not there); her skin turns red and her eyes burn red while the entity speaks.
- Power shown: burned the purple bhuta haze off Arjun and Dadi in one shockwave; casually attempted to erase Bog from existence (stopped by Priya herself).
- Attitude: treats ordinary lives as insignificant, but respects the will of its incarnation. Ordered Priya to keep it secret.
- Mission and lore: Earth's "transcendent ones" used siddhis and astras against the Val, exposing a buried singularity; a recent conflict fought with siddhis - in a land the entity names only by its sky, "a low-hanging moon over a land where the sun rises high" - was the final blow. A "threshold" is widening from the singularity; the bhutas (spirits of unburied war dead) are only the first leak; cosmic collapse follows. The singularity lies to the west (read as the old Mississippi-Alabama border) and must be buried again.
- The vision: touching possessed Dadi's wrist, Priya saw bhutas spread house to house, possess animals, and end the world in mutual slaughter and total extinction - the moment the red aura first erupted.

### Her terminology (hers alone - no other character uses these terms; manuscript spelling has no diacritics)
- **Dharmatman**: the hero side - those who fight for Earth. (She herself claims to be neither Dharmatman nor Papatman.)
- **Papatman**: villains - the side opposed to the Dharmatman.
- **Rsi**: a superpower user, of either side. First used on the page in ch094, unexplained in the text.
- **Astra**: a great weapon or loosed power-attack of the transcendent ones. Earth's transcendent ones used siddhis and astras against the Val, exposing the buried singularity. In ch094 she classes Rin's suit blast (ch081) as a new astra - her framing, delivered abstractly and not explained in the text.
- **Siddhi**: a transcendent power or ability. A recent conflict fought with siddhis was, in her account, the final blow that widened the threshold.
