## Core (does not change)
- Role: Protagonist. Low-ranking Val soldier, on leave on Earth, provisional reservist. (No sergeant rank: "Sergeant" for Bog was a Claude invention - Claude kept inflating the protagonist - and was struck by the user's ruling of 2026-11-18; see the ch54 review. His exact grade is not established beyond low-ranking.)
- Height: **7.5 feet** (user ruling, 2026-11-18). Already stated in ch62 ("seven and a half feet of bone and muscle"); ch76's "eight feet" was corrected to match. ch01's "built for people a good two feet shorter than he was" is consistent with 7.5 against ordinary human height.
- Phallus (established across the ritual chapters; length and non-human contour made explicit at the user's direction, 2026-11-20): beyond human scale in both girth and length — thicker than Nia's wrist, a two-handed weight, long in proportion to his 7.5-foot frame (no number is ever stated in prose; sizes are always relative). Its contours are distinctly non-human and menacing rather than merely large: heavy ridging the tongue can read, a vein running the length of it fat as a finger, a shape no human mouth was consulted about (Rin can take the tip only; Nia takes him deep). His testicles are likewise enormous, heavy and pendulous, the skin over them finer and softer.
- Desire (what they want across the whole story): To settle on Earth. In the ending direction he builds the ultimate harem and finds happiness, with Nia as his number one.
- The house dream: He is dissatisfied with his current home (the repurposed relay station) and dreams of someday living in a fine, comfortable Earth-style house where he can truly relax. This is what ch01's "a house. A real one, with walls" means. (User setting, 2026-11-17.)
- Earth attachment: Since coming to Earth he has grown thoroughly attached to Earth culture, and to women. His knowledge of Earth culture, however, is heavily skewed and strange - pieced together from markets, occupation life and fragments, and confidently off in ways he never notices. Played for comedy: he is an enthusiast, not an expert. (User setting, 2026-11-17.)
- Lack / wound: A large old scar and a thin-plated spot under his left shoulder blade (origin [UNKNOWN]). As a Val he has no mother; he was grown in a cradle.
- Strength, and how it backfires: Cunning beneath a haphazard, easygoing surface; physically a Val (far stronger than humans). His improvisations keep succeeding beyond his rank - and keep escalating his exposure (the depot raid, commanding a sealed Dominion weapon).
- What they're lying about / don't realize about themselves: He presents his devotion to Nia as mood and appetite ("Oops, I said that to set the mood"), while acting on it consistently at real cost. He does not frame his own risks as self-sacrifice; his species has no such concept.

- Speech characteristics: Crude, easygoing, lewd, haphazard; quietly cunning. Oblivious deadpan against other people's horror. (The "That's a lot of paperwork" deadpan formerly noted here was a Claude tic, struck 2026-11-19 — see 04_style_guide.md, Banned Patterns.)
- Words they use often / never use: [UNKNOWN]

"Pickle" is never Bog's word for Nia - his own coined endearment for her is "squeeze candy" (first use ch14; the ch14 draft's "pickle" in his mouth was ruled a Claude hallucination by the user and replaced, 2026-11-17).

## Notes
- Home: a squat, repurposed relay station in the Wasteland, on the edge of a dead freeway (established ch04 - he takes Nia straight there after the purchase; it is already his dwelling). What he dreams about in ch01 is a different thing: "a real house, with walls." Do not write him as homeless or quarterless. (Confirmed in the ch01 review, 2026-11-17.)
- Home layout (accumulated from the prose, recorded 2026-11-17 at the user's direction, ch09 review): a basement with a Val water processor that could fill a lake (ch09, ch13); a stone shower cubicle at the back of the house, big enough for him and cramped for two (ch09); a kitchen of all-Earth, mismatched gear inventoried with pride in ch05 - toaster from Ohio, rice cooker from Osaka, dish rack next to the fridge, knives in the cupboard with the cereal, sink at chest height for Nia and stove near her chin, outlets wired to something that hums (ch07) - on a power system Bog rewired himself because "the wiring was ridiculous" (ch05); a living room with an Earth-made couch "comfortable beyond all reason" (ch05); a closet holding the two unused helper robots (ch07).
- Superior: his Platoon Leader, who contacts him by arm communicator.
- His household includes helper robots: he owns three in total. One - the squeaky-wheeled, tactful one that obeys Bog - is in regular service; the other two stay in the closet, unused. (Ruled 2026-11-17, ch07 review: this reconciles ch07's "three of them, in the closet" with the single household robot of ch14-17 and the minor cast.)
- He secretly diverts Val medical supplies to the Free Clinics' Appalachian branch. It is paid business, not charity: the Clinics pay well for Val gear (ch08: "They pay a fortune"), and in ch56 he corrects Priya's "volunteer" with "Business, actually." (Recorded 2026-11-17, ch08 review. This is the Clinics channel only; whether Bog profits from the black market itself remains deliberately ambiguous per the ch01 ruling.)
- Under the standing deal, he has NOT looked up Nia's family, at her request.
- Left arm: at the orbital depot (ch26) his own stun shot was redirected through Sonya's portal and brushed his left shoulder, numbing the whole arm. It stayed numb through the fall (ch28-36), began to tingle at landfall (ch39), and he was still working the numbness out of the bad shoulder in ch40. Ruled 2026-11-18 (ch55 review): by Florence it is only a light, lingering numbness - "still faintly numb from the depot stun" - not a disabling injury, and not something he favors or cradles. In ch56 Priya, an excellent clinician, spots the faint awkwardness in the arm's movement and treats it (ruled 2026-11-18, ch56 review).
