## Core (does not change)
- Role: Major cast. An ancient autonomous Dominion weapon modeled on a real space beast - a cocoon of steel cables, found in the orbital depot's Sector Delta.
- Nature: Regenerates instantly; has no core (Sonya's portals could not find one); absorbs floor plating, weapons and supplies to grow. By default it targets the strongest non-Val in range; the "legend" that it never attacks Val holds as targeting - it does not aim at Val - but its rampage hits them when clumsy (the soldier launched in ch29 is collateral, not an attack).
- Control: Bog clamped a spare null-collar onto a tentacle, and the collar acts as a **General Control System**: the monster obeys Bog as "Commander," takes orders literally (asks to "define" vague terms), can speak through collars, shape tentacles into lifelike forms, and synthesize compositions from samples.
- The depot's emergency charge: the bomb stored at the depot (the "run-of-the-mill" planetary-core-destruction bomb in ch23's Sector Delta inventory) was an emergency device emplaced to destroy the monster if it got loose. Sonya activated it after severing the monster in ch30; it had degraded with age, so it burned away the parts of the creature still tangled in the depot and kicked the severed mass Earthward instead of destroying it. She then teleported into an escape pod the monster had absorbed and fought on from inside it. (User, ch32 review 2026-11-18; reported in ch32 in the monster's own mechanical register.)
- History (from its own records): The ancient Val sealed it away because its first deployment produced three days of unconditional surrender from an entire fleet; eleven admirals asked for its composition and were denied; the order to seal followed when the fleet requested an encore.
- Personality: Warm, tactful and courteous; never mocks. Keeps logs and maps of everything - vocabulary counts, reaction maps, training manuals. Designs training "modules," "reward phases" and a holographic leaderboard (Nia on top). Composes and plays music. Continues "internal expansion and adjustments" of its burrow.
- Running gag: "In about one Carbon-11 half-life" = about twenty minutes.
- Loyalty (General Control System): **300%** (author-given, 2026-11-20; not yet displayed in prose). For the scale's meaning see lore/val_devices.md — at 100% a wearer no longer wants the collar off; above 50% they almost certainly never betray the handler.

## Voice
- Flat, literal, formal machine voice: "Awaiting orders, Commander", "Please define '...'", "Report", "Logged as...", "Vocabulary count: N". Insults and numerals are logged as praise / cough / tactical analysis depending on context. "Duplicate entries are not recorded."
- It reckons time in its own units (e.g. "approximately one Carbon-11 half-life"), not Earth units. An Earth-minutes gloss in its mouth at ch32 ("Approximately twenty minutes") was a Claude addition, cut at the user's instruction (2026-11-18) - minutes are an Earth unit and the monster would not suddenly speak in them. Nuance (user, 2026-11-18): with the General Control System integrated, the monster also learns new languages and concepts rapidly, so Earth units in its mouth become defensible later - but at ch35 a precise minute-by-minute countdown was still too much, too soon. Resolved at the ch35 review (user's device): ch35's announcement now gives ground contact as "two to the third barium half-lives" (Ba-137m, ~2.55 min each; 8 of them = ~20.4 min, the same span as one Carbon-11 half-life), with Bog nodding along pretending to have converted it. The user then extended the device to the whole descent (2026-11-18): ch36 is "two to the second barium half-lives" (~10.2 min) and ch37 "two to the first" (~5.1 min) - a halving countdown in the monster's own logarithmic unit, replacing the original Earth-minutes figures (12 and 8). Its rapid-learning point stands as a general trait, but these announcements stay in its own units.
- It calls Sonya "Sophia Ivanova" or "the subject"; in its titles, Nia is Bog's "future primary consort" and Sonya may become "secondary consort."
- Val lore it has supplied: Val soldiers come from artificial cradles and have no mothers ("Cradle Vault Seventeen," dormant); the Quiet Blight is what makes cradles fail; among the Val, a woman who bears a child is rare and precious - the root of Bog's wish for hybrids.
