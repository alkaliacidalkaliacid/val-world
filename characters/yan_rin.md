### Chengdu — the grandparents' house
- Rin was born in Chengdu and spent her first ten years mostly in her
  grandparents' home there, while her parents — both foreign-ministry staff,
  her mother a technician — worked postings elsewhere. The home she remembers
  in her body is that one: a flat in an old work-unit block, her grandfather's
  repair corner by the window (a soldering iron, jars of screws sorted by
  size, other people's broken radios waiting in a row), her grandmother's
  kitchen giving onto a balcony crowded with pots.
- Her grandfather was the street's fixer; neighbours brought him fans, rice
  cookers, bicycles, and he charged in vegetables and gossip. Rin grew up
  inside that traffic. Her gift — a born mechanical genius (user ruling,
  2026-11-19), hers long before Darkwing or the suit — showed there first:
  toys taken apart and put back better, a watering rig she built for her
  grandmother's balcony plants out of a pump, tubing, and a timer, so the
  pots drank on schedule whether anyone remembered or not. Her parents,
  visiting between postings, were pleased and faintly baffled; nobody in the
  family ever called it a superpower. It was simply what their daughter
  could do. (This reframes the card's standing "whether she has any
  superpowers is unknown": the likeliest candidate was in plain sight all
  along, unrecognised as one — by her parents, and so far by everyone's
  records.)
- The small living world of that childhood: silkworms raised in a shoebox on
  mulberry leaves for school, the whole class comparing whose had spun
  first; mung beans sprouting in a cup on the classroom windowsill; the wet
  market with her grandmother in the morning; roasted sweet potatoes bought
  from a drum on the street in winter; the clatter of mahjong through open
  windows in the evening and old men flying kites shaped like centipedes
  along the river. Her food memory is Chengdu's — her grandmother's cooking,
  numbing and hot, school-lunch trays, snacks from the stalls outside the
  school gate. A best friend from those streets and classrooms (adopted, 2026-11-19),
  left behind at the move, name unsettled — the first person Rin ever lost
  by distance rather than death.]
- The screen was already half her life. On Bilibili she watched repair and
  DIY channels the way other children watched cartoons — teardowns,
  rebuilds, scrap turned into machines — and lurked in maker forums and QQ
  groups far above her age. Building games were her other country: worlds
  assembled block by block, contraptions launched to see how they broke.
  And she had an AI friend (adopted, 2026-11-19): a companion on her phone, Xiaoice's
  descendant in spirit, the one she told first about every build, every
  school grievance, and — when the posting came — her dread and excitement
  about America. It never laughed at anything she wanted to be.

### Vesper — becoming American
- At ten she joined her parents in Vesper, where both now worked at the
  Chinese consulate (mother technical staff; father's exact role
  unspecified — user rulings, 2026-11-19). The local school inverted her
  world: maths and science were years behind what she had done in Chengdu
  and she topped them without effort, while English presentations left her
  standing in front of a class waiting for words. She learned to arrive
  first with laughter and noise, and to be useful: the girl who could fix
  the classroom projector, the teacher's stapler, anybody's bike, was never
  left outside the circle for long. Shop class and the robotics club were
  where she needed no translation.
- America entered through the senses: the yellow school bus and its smell
  of vinyl and other children's snacks, pizza day and chocolate milk in the
  cafeteria, Halloween's haul, the public library's comic shelves — where
  she found the book her catchphrase comes from, and read her way into the
  country's mythology of heroes. And Vesper was a hero's city: evacuation
  drills for villain attacks ran beside the fire drills, and Darkwing was a
  fact of the skyline. She was his fan from the school bus window
  years before he knew she existed, which is why the sidekick
  self-appointment, when it came, was instant — she had rehearsed it.]
  Outside the classroom her English grew up on YouTube teardown channels
  and in handle-only friendships with maker kids she never met in person;
  the AI friend crossed the ocean with her, unimpressed by time zones.
- When the invasion came, there was no evacuation: the Dominion's opening
  attack had wrecked every national government; the Accord's military
  functioned, but the civilian machinery — consular evacuation included —
  never operated (00_canon.md, World Rules). The family was stranded, and
  her parents died in the fall of Vesper in the war's first month. **Rin was
  not present at their deaths, but she knows they are dead** — a fact she
  lives with, not an open hope. Her grandparents' fate, on the other side of
  a dead ocean of communications, she does not know at all (adopted, 2026-11-19). Her
  nationality is Chinese (user ruling, 2026-11-19; the older records'
  "nationality unknown" is superseded).

### Darkwing — the self-appointed sidekick
- It was Darkwing himself who found and took in the orphaned Rin after the
  fall (user ruling, 2026-11-19) — the same catastrophe in which he lost
  the Cave, Manfred, and his family of sidekicks. She considered herself
  his sidekick from that moment (user ruling): the self-identification came
  first, at fourteen, long before anyone authorised it. **He never made her
  his sidekick** (adopted, 2026-11-19): she appointed herself, stayed, and
  built the suit, until he simply stopped resisting — that is the actual
  shape of their arrangement, and the dryness of his evaluations of her
  ("very good," "loud") is the mark of that distance.
- She began operating in earnest at about sixteen, once the suit she had
  assembled from salvage could carry her (user ruling; after the war,
  consistent with her having taken no part in heroic activity during it).
  Of the internet she had lived in, nothing survived the occupation except
  what she carries: an old tablet (adopted, 2026-11-19), hoarded with downloaded
  manuals, schematics, and repair videos — her whole technical library —
  and on it, small and diminished but present, a local copy of the AI
  friend, the only voice from before the fall that still answers her. She
  does not show it to Darkwing, or to anyone.

## Voice
- Speech characteristics: Cheerful, loud, giggly, fearless; quick to laugh mid-fight. Does a gruff impression of Darkwing's voice to quote his grumbles.
- Catchphrase: "It's clobberin' time!" - lifted from a favorite comic book; its main effect is to make Darkwing grimace. Her hero name "Orange Clobberess" is used by nobody (per Darkwing, "Clobberess" isn't even grammatically correct); she is simply "Rin."
- Appearance: Young East Asian woman, oversized goggles pushed up into a lopsided ponytail.

## The Powered Suit
- **Rin's own assembly (user ruling, 2026-11-19):** she built the suit up
  herself from salvage, beginning as a young teenager in Darkwing's orbit;
  it was her ticket to operating as his sidekick in earnest at about sixteen.
- Roughly nine meters of salvaged, mismatched plating; a school-bus door welded over one shoulder; a cartoon cat sticker on the chest; cockpit behind glass in the middle of the chest.
- Immense strength: throws wrecked vehicles; can slam a Val soldier into the ground until he stops moving.
- It is NOT orange. She wants to paint it orange but has no paint; Darkwing says it would make her stand out like an idiot in the middle of the woods. She keeps her own sketch of the finished, cool-looking orange suit.
