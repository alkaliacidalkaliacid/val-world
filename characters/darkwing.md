## Core (does not change)
- Role: Supporting cast. Billionaire hero, about 60, no powers, still moves like a man of thirty. Field commander, strategist and spiritual pillar of the Old Guards' Appalachian branch, and one of the Old Guards' powerful sponsors — his surviving Thorne Group physical assets, whose locations he carries in his head, quietly underwrite the organisation (see Background).
- Background: Former protector of the ruined megalopolis of Vesper; in the first month of the war he lost the Cave, the butler Manfred, and his family of sidekicks. Both the old US government and the Val had him on their lists; everyone assumed he was dead. He knew Nia from the Earth Accord's first planning summit, and knows Dr. Hermes ("insufferable, and he's not lying").
- Desire / lack / self-deception: Asked what he wants after the war, the answer would be retirement (user ruling, 2026-11-19) — and it would even be sincere, as far as it goes. In fact, if the Dominion were driven out tomorrow, he would hunt criminals until his body stops moving, and possibly past that point; the war changed his enemy, not his occupation. The retirement is real the way his young heir's façade was real: a part he could perform, and will never be asked to.
- Strength, and how it backfires: Experience, discipline, preparation (pre-planted wires, decoys), and dry patience. Fights Val hand-to-hand with tethered boomerang-cables and sneeze-powder grenades, vaulting off Rin's suit.

## Background / Personal History
> Reference material — include in a context pack only when a chapter touches his past.
> Built in consultation with the user, 2026-11-19. The user ruled the shape:
> **essentially Bruce Wayne's story, renamed** — what follows applies that
> ruling to this world's facts; where the manuscript already differs (the
> sidekicks' end, Rin), the manuscript governs.

- **The house:** Elias Thorne, heir to Thorne Industries, the diversified
  industrial conglomerate his family built with Vesper — steel, shipping,
  construction, later electronics and medicine; the Thornes are one of the
  city's founding fortunes, and the Manor on the hill above Vesper was the
  family's seat. His parents were murdered in a street robbery when he was
  a small boy; he inherited the company, the Manor, and the name, and was
  raised in that big house by the family butler, Manfred. Manfred served
  the house all his life; by the time of the invasion he was an old man
  living in a care facility (user ruling, 2026-11-19), which is part of
  what Darkwing counts among his losses.
- **The making:** As a young man he vanished from Vesper's public life for
  years and spent them learning how the world works from underneath —
  martial arts, criminology, chemistry, tracking, disguise, the sciences a
  man with no powers would need. He came home and waged his one-man war on
  Vesper's crime from the Cave he built under the Manor, as Darkwing, the
  Knight of Vesper. In public, the young Elias Thorne kept the façade of
  the charming, flirtatious young heir — and kept it properly, with the
  education and the set of fine tastes the part required, all of which he
  was genuinely capable of enjoying (user ruling, 2026-11-19). The company
  ran itself under hired executives, and nobody connected the heir to the
  figure on the rooftops. That façade, and the man who could enjoy it, are
  long since irrelevant.
- **The family he built:** Over the decades he took in trainees — young
  people with nowhere else to aim their damage — and made sidekicks of
  them; the Manor was rarely empty, and Manfred kept its table full. It
  ended the way such families do: one by one the earlier ones grew up,
  quarrelled, or left, and the last of them were still with him when the
  Val came. In the first month of the war he lost all of it at once —
  Vesper, the Manor, the Cave, Manfred, and the trainees who had stayed.
  Everyone on both sides' lists assumed he had died with his city.
- **After:** He surfaced in Appalachia with the Old Guards and rebuilt a
  smaller, poorer version of his war: a branch to command, an excellent
  engineer (Hermes) borrowed from the old networks, prisoner exchanges
  conducted with distasteful pragmatism, and salvage where the Manor's
  stores used to be. And into that reduced household walked the orphan
  Rin, who appointed herself his sidekick; he never appointed her, and in
  the end he stopped resisting (see yan_rin.md). She is the first one who
  stayed.
- **The money is gone; the things remain (user ruling, 2026-11-19):** Of
  the Thorne fortune as money, nothing is left — and Darkwing is not
  troubled by it. What survives is the Thorne Group's **physical assets**,
  scattered here and there: warehouses, depots, facilities, stockpiled
  materials and goods, still legally his property even in a world where
  nobody cares about ownership any more. He carries the location of every
  one of them, and how each is got into, **in his own head** — no ledger,
  no map anyone could take. The Thornes were never in the weapons
  business, so none of it is armaments: it is supplies — materiel,
  equipment, provisions — which he draws on directly or trades away by
  barter. More than that: this is what makes him **one of the Old Guards'
  powerful sponsors** (user ruling, 2026-11-19) — the asset network quietly
  underwrites the organisation well beyond his own branch's logistics and
  its side of the prisoner exchanges, and he is one of a small number of
  backers on that scale.
- **No private life (user ruling, 2026-11-19):** Paradoxically for a
  character built this way, Darkwing has no personal life at all, and never
  really had one behind the façade: training, planning, command, and combat
  are not his duties — they are his life, entire. He eats, sleeps, and
  maintains a sixty-year-old body that moves like thirty only as logistics
  for the next operation. If anything stands where a private life would be,
  it is his contact with orphans: the trainees he took in, the strays the
  war keeps producing, Rin. The household of the parentless that he builds
  around himself, again and again, is the closest thing to a personal
  existence he permits.
