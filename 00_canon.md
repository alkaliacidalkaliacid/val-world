## World Rules (must never be broken)
- In 2041 the Val Dominion invaded Earth. The war lasted eleven months; the Earth Accord was decisively defeated at the Battle of the Meridian Line. The occupation has lasted six years at the story's present.
- The Dominion's opening attack struck the national governments of every country hard (user ruling, 2026-11-19). The Earth Accord — the military alliance — functioned, but the non-military machinery of government was left in ruins: civilian administration, records, and services such as consular evacuation never effectively operated. (This is why, e.g., foreign consulate staff and their families in fallen cities were stranded rather than evacuated.)
- The Val are an all-male humanoid species, 7'2"-8'6", green-skinned, and physically far stronger than unenhanced humans. An ordinary Val infantryman can overpower several humans; uncollared superheroes are a strategic threat capable of threatening even Val warships.
- Superhuman abilities are real and varied. Every captured superhero is fitted with a null-collar, which suppresses or sharply limits their powers.
- Null-collars also monitor vital signs, broadcast the wearer's location, and can deliver incapacitating pain pulses via authorized control units. They resist ordinary tools and conventional weapons. Escape with one on is difficult, but not physically impossible.
- No reliable way to disable or remove a null-collar exists yet — **on the human side**. Free Clinics research has documented symptoms and fragments only; a wrong intervention can permanently injure or kill the wearer. The Dominion's own method exists (a Class 5 toolkit, operable only by a Val), but no human knows it (see the revealed-truth rule below).
- Revealed truth — the General Control System (ch112–116): a null-collar is not a mere suppressor. Each collar carries a **General Control System** whose basic functions are communication, sensory synchronization, remote control, loyalty monitoring, power conservation, storage, amplification, bursts, and resonance. Suppression is only its visible face: settings alone can restore, amplify, or burst a wearer's powers, and removal done correctly (Class 5 toolkit, Val hands) does not explode — its cost is losing access to the System. Full detail in `lore/val_devices.md`. **Knowledge restriction (provisional, as of ch116): apart from the tentacle monster, only Bog, Rin, Nia, and Priya know any of this.** Everyone else — Sonya, Darkwing, the Free Clinics, all other Val and humans — still knows the collar as a power-suppressor only. Update this rule when someone else learns in-story.
- The Val do not reproduce biologically. Their population is sustained by artificial gestation chambers (cradles), an inherited technology they maintain by ritualized procedure and barely understand - the same is true of the null-collars.
- The cradles are failing because of an unexplained contamination, the Quiet Blight. Its cause is unknown to the Val and it cannot currently be reversed. The Dominion faces slow extinction without a replacement.
- Val elites classify humanity as a reproductive resource, because humans reproduce naturally. Dominion policy forbids killing human females ("reproducible individuals"); lower-ranking Val generally observe this, but it is a policy, not an absolute.
- Natural reproduction is a rediscovery, not a living Val tradition. After ages of total dependence on the cradles, the Val relearned natural reproduction only through contact with humanity. The Elites hastily assembled the consort institution from ancient texts, and it shows: the system is earnest but off-kilter, its terms and rituals not quite fitting the reality they govern (decided 2026-11-17).
- The flying-lizard view (user ruling, 2026-11-19): the ch03 merchant's proverb — catch a superior wild female, ideally one that already has a mate and offspring; she'll fight hardest because of her loyalty, but once tamed she'll give that same devotion, maybe more, and her breeding record is already proven — is not only an alien trader's animal lore. The same thinking is held by some Val about women: a wife and mother faithful to her husband and children resists most fiercely at first, but once she yields she will give her new master the same loyalty she gave her former family, or more — and her fertility and her ability to build a household are already proven. Bog heard the proverb from the merchant at Nia's purchase and shrugged it off; it has never been a thought he acts on.
- The Val have no compunction about taking married Earth women as consorts (user ruling, 2026-11-19). A woman's Earth marriage is no obstacle and no transgression in Val terms — Bog bought Nia, Daniel's wife, without a thought. Whatever friction a collaring creates sits on the human side (the woman, her husband, her family) and with entities like the Mediatoress, never in Val conscience.
- Consort physiology (user-supplied 2026-11-17; it had been missing from the records): an Earth woman who becomes a consort by ingesting a Val's semen changes gradually. Her sweat, tears, saliva, mucus, arousal fluid, urine and feces all become clean and fragrant. Used as fertilizer, her waste makes plants flourish - but it slowly terraforms the local ecosystem toward the Val homeworld. Her feces become jelly: normally elastic, rounded lumps, but constipation forms one long continuous cylinder the sphincter cannot sever, which she must struggle to push out. Excretion itself is pleasurable for her - the stimulation of the intestines, and likewise of the urethra when she urinates.
- Human resistance has no unified command, no secure territory, and no reliable communication between cells.
- A brand marks a person as the permanent property of a specific Val under Dominion law, comparable to a marriage contract. It is not applied casually.
- Buying and selling human captives is permitted under Dominion law. At a purchase, the buyer swears an oath before a registrar, under the glowing seal, to keep the human healthy and well. This purchase oath is a general Val custom and is entirely separate from the Brand. Once a person is branded, they can no longer be bought or sold - the Brand removes them from the market permanently. (Ruled by the user 2026-11-17, ch01 review.)
- The Val have a strong natural resistance to many drugs: most human narcotics have no effect on them at all. They are not invulnerable, however - alcohol affects them fully, and some substances can still harm them. (Ruled by the user 2026-11-17, ch01 review.)
- The Val language has no word for self-sacrifice. This is a fact about the language and culture, and it is how Bog frames his own conduct: he denies the concept outright. Whether Bog's actions are in fact self-sacrificing is deliberately left undecided - so far, all that is established is his denial. (User's original instruction, restored at the ch30 review 2026-11-17 after the original Claude inverted it in the prose.)

## Basics of Time and Geography
- Era / setting: Earth, six years into the Val occupation (invasion: 2041). The story's present is provisionally 2047 [PROVISIONAL - the user has agreed to treat 2047 as the working year, subject to retroactive revision if a contradiction appears].
- Many cities are shattered shells; some are rebuilt as Val garrisons, labor hubs, or administrative centers.
- Florence, Alabama (user ruling, 2026-11-19): Florence was attacked during the invasion, but its strategic importance was low and it was never as thoroughly destroyed as Atlanta. Its people scattered, then gathered again and rebuilt the town; it now also shelters refugees from Atlanta and Nashville. Atlanta, by contrast, was hit thoroughly.
- National governments have dissolved, fled into exile, or survive as tightly controlled local administrations. The United States led the Earth Accord coalition during the war.
- Electricity, medicine, transport, and communications are scarce outside occupation zones.
- Climate (user ruling, 2026-11-20): the war with the Val Dominion has left Earth's climate abnormal. Seasons no longer reliably bring the weather they should — unseasonable heat, cold, rain or calm are common everywhere, and a scene's weather often does not match its nominal season. The season bands in 05_timeline.md are therefore nominal: they fix the broad time of year, but scene-level weather may deviate from them freely under this rule.
- Most survivors live in ration districts, agricultural compounds, underground settlements, or scattered communities beyond regular patrol routes. Abandoned suburbs and industrial belts are dangerous corridors of feral machines and scavengers.

## Prohibitions / Continuity Checks
- Naming (decided 2026-11-17): the resistance alliance is **The Old Guards** - "Ash Guard" is an abandoned earlier name and must not be used. The highway raiders are **Raiders** - "the Cinder Kings" is abandoned and must not be used. The smuggler network is **Traders** - "the Gilded Hand" is abandoned and must not be used. The cults have no proper name, deliberately. **The Uncrowned does not exist** - the concept was abolished; former supervillains belong to various separate groups (e.g. the League of Shi), not one organization.
- Do not give the resistance a unified command or safe territory without a story event that changes this.
- Do not let any character explain the underlying science of cradles or collars; in canon, almost no one understands them.
- Do not remove or disable a collar easily or safely. For humans, no method exists yet; the only known method is a Class 5 toolkit operated by a Val (ch112–114).
- Do not let any character outside the knowing circle (the tentacle monster; Bog, Rin, Nia, Priya — as of ch116) use, reference, or act on the General Control System's functions. The restriction is chronological: characters learn only in-story, on the page.
- Do not treat the Val as uniformly stupid; their technology is inherited, but their military organization and strategy are real.
- Do not resolve the Quiet Blight or the Val reproductive crisis casually; it is the Dominion's central long-term pressure.
