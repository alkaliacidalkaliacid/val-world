## Resistance

### The Old Guards
> Naming decided 2026-11-17: unified as "The Old Guards". "Ash Guard" was an earlier concept name that still appears in older drafts; it is to be unified away, not used.
- A loose alliance of former soldiers, local defense groups, escaped prisoners, and civilian fighters.
- Activities: ambushes, sabotage of supply lines, stealing ration shipments, protecting settlements from raiders and patrols.
- Some units include non-powered former crime-fighters and vigilantes who rely on training, investigation, disguises, and local knowledge.
- Strength: intimate local knowledge. Weakness: cannot coordinate beyond their own territory. Internally divided between careful defense and aggressive reprisals.
- Field identification: a white armband marked with a red cross and a number, worn to claim lawful-combatant status under the Laws of War.
- Bases (user ruling, 2026-11-21): the Appalachian branch holds several bases on the Mississippi side of its broad region; Darkwing's bunker (northern Mississippi) is one of them — abandoning it would not end the branch. Geography in lore/geography.md.
- Information: they hold a great deal of accumulated data — records on people the Val have taken, on camps and work details, built up cell by cell over six years. **How comprehensive any of it is remains unclear** (user ruling, 2026-11-20): coverage is deep in their own territories and patchy to nonexistent beyond them, records are unverified and unindexed across cells, and nobody — including the Old Guards themselves — knows what fraction of the whole their data represents. Keep this vagueness in prose: a large haystack is established; that any given needle is in it is not.

### The Free Clinics
- A network of doctors, nurses, pharmacists, and volunteers working from basements, abandoned hospitals, and hidden rooms.
- Treat injuries, deliver babies, preserve scarce medicine, and hide fugitives when they can.
- Secretly researching the null-collars: symptoms documented, collar behavior compared, fragments from malfunctions studied. No working removal method yet.
- Core principle: research must never come at the expense of a captive's life. This sometimes conflicts with impatient resistance fighters.
- Information: their routes carry more than supplies — word travels with them: names, and lists of the missing and the found (this is how Priya's parents were confirmed alive). The network has accumulated a great deal of such information over the years, but **how comprehensive it is remains unclear** (user ruling, 2026-11-20): it reaches only where their routes reach, lists are partial and secondhand, and nobody knows what share of the missing their records actually cover. Keep this vagueness in prose: the network is a real route for a search, never a guarantee of an answer.

## Third-Party Factions
Neither resistance nor Dominion allies. Loyalties are local, transactional, or ideological, and shift with the balance of power.

### Raiders
> Naming decided 2026-11-17: unified as "Raiders". "The Cinder Kings" is an abandoned name that still appears in the older source and in draft prose; a unification pass over the prose is pending as a separate later task.
- Operate along abandoned highways and freight corridors.
- Strip vehicles, attack isolated settlements, sell captured supplies to whoever pays.
- Avoid direct battle with Val patrols, but sometimes trade guides, prisoners, or intelligence for weapons and protection.

### Cults [no proper name - confirmed deliberate, 2026-11-17]
- Interpret the invasion as divine judgment and the Val as instruments of a new age; preach that surrender to Dominion rule is a sacred duty.
- Some chapters collaborate openly with occupation officials, identifying hidden settlements and denouncing resistance members. Others focus on ritual, communal living, and apocalyptic prophecy.

### Traders
> Naming decided 2026-11-17: unified as "Traders". "The Gilded Hand" is an abandoned name.
- Smugglers, black-market brokers, and former corporate fixers.
- Trade in medicine, forged identity papers, ammunition, alien components, and information.
- Will work with the resistance one week and sell a route map to a Val quartermaster the next. Regarded as dangerous but sometimes indispensable.

### Former supervillains [The Uncrowned was ABOLISHED on 2026-11-17 - do not use it]
- There is no single supervillain organization. Former supervillains belong to various separate groups (e.g. the League of Shi), or operate alone.
- In the source they survived by hiding, abandoning public identities, and letting the Val believe they died in the invasion. They distrust one another nearly as much as they distrust the heroes.
- The figures below are individuals from the source. Their group affiliations are not yet established.
- Known figures:
  - **Mister Ruin** - demolition expert; weakens the structural integrity of materials; sells sabotage to the highest bidder.
  - **Madame Morrow** - former information broker; extracts fragments of memory through physical contact; keeps a private archive of secrets.
  - **Gravemind** - telepath who can influence crowds but not reliably control disciplined individuals; leads a small fearful following in a ruined industrial district.
  - **The Jackal** - non-powered master thief and escape artist; trades stolen alien equipment and changes identities.
  - **Saint Voltage** - disgraced energy manipulator, formerly claimed to be a hero; supplies power cells to Val officers in exchange for protection.
- Some could help undermine the occupation; others are predators. None are dependable allies.
