## Null-collar
- See `00_canon.md` for rules: power suppression, vital-sign and location monitoring, pain pulses, resistance to ordinary tools.
- Each collar is a standalone system: it carries its own control system on board and does not tie back to any central network. This is why a spare collar clamped onto the tentacle monster's limb in ch031 could engage a General Control System on the spot. (User ruling, ch031 review 2026-11-18: "ties back to" was changed to "equipped with" in Bog's reasoning for exactly this reason.)
- The monster holds collar stock of its own: depot inventory it absorbed in orbit. It collared Sonya from that absorbed stock (ch038) and fitted a small detached cluster of itself with one (ch131). No count was ever set for the absorbed stock and nothing records it as exhausted. Distinct from Bog's own spares — two collars from Hermes's shed, spent on Rin (ch086) and Priya (ch098), the last one on his belt gone at ch098.

## General Control System (expanded ch112–116)
- The collar's on-board system is the General Control System. The tentacle monster wears a collar fitted on one of its tentacles and can instruct in the System's use, given a toolkit of Class 2 or higher (ch112). It teaches only on Bog's order (ch113).
- Removing a collar, done correctly, does not explode. What removal costs is access to the System's functions (ch113).
- The functions, in the monster's own list — which it calls "basic functions": communication, sensory synchronization, remote control, loyalty monitoring, power conservation, storage, amplification, bursts, and resonance (ch113).
- Confirmed by the monster (ch113, on Rin's deduction): the System can amplify a hero's power and can trigger a burst. The collar is therefore not a mere nullifier; suppression is only the face the party had seen.
- Settings alone can restore a wearer's superpowers without removing the collar; the collars are currently in accumulation mode, and the accumulated power will not cause a short circuit (ch114).
- The toolkit: a Class 5 toolkit can remove a System or alter its settings, but only a Val can use one — a human cannot ("not yet," in the monster's unexplained wording, ch114). Once set up, the toolkit transforms and bonds onto the user's forearm as a wristband, and there is no way to remove it; a Class 5 toolkit is standard equipment for a Commander (ch114). Bog now wears Nia's. Toolkit control is not absolute in principle: Rin's superpower at maximum could override and hijack it (see "Rin's interference" below — authorial truth, unknown in-world).
- Loyalty rating: on command, the System displays a loyalty rating as a hologram above the wearer's collar — Val script by default, convertible to an Earth language on command (ch115). ## Piercing-rings and bells
- Ornamental devices fitted at designated body piercings on some captives; small bells with a clear, unusually beautiful tone. Use is not universal among the Val.
- Functions: the rings can vibrate in controlled patterns (persistent sensory stimulation that breaks concentration); placement can restrict movement, especially combined with a collar or restraints; the bells make the wearer locatable by sound; they can be tuned or remotely activated by authorized handlers.
- The pleasant tone is deliberate; many captives find the contrast with the coercive purpose especially unsettling.

## Intestinal bead-chain
- A more invasive device used in some compounds: a string of large beads with mechanical and sensory systems, remotely controlled or run on automated cycles. Depending on the model it can rotate, vibrate, change texture, expand and contract, change temperature, and generate electrical currents.
- The Val describe it as a sanitary and disciplinary appliance: they claim it processes waste in the intestines and converts it into a clean, transparent, fragrant gel that can be collected through the apparatus. Dominion technicians cannot fully explain the underlying processes.
- Some models can be fitted with a faux tail made from the wearer's own shorn hair, regarded by the Val as a fashionable sign that a captive has been incorporated into an owner's household display.

## Leashes
- Made by braiding hair cut from the collared person. No technological function; significance is symbolic and practical.
- Leashes made from superhero hair are especially prized: superhuman hair is often exceptionally durable, and such leashes are prestigious trophies among status-conscious Val.

## Brands
- See `00_canon.md`: a permanent ownership mark under Dominion law, comparable to a marriage contract, establishing an enduring household bond and exclusive ownership.
- Among the Val, receiving one is considered a profound honor and a public declaration of selection. Some humans who have embraced Val customs share this interpretation and actively seek such bonds.
- Once a person is branded, they can no longer be bought or sold. The Brand takes them off the market permanently - which is one reason it is never applied casually. (Ruled 2026-11-17.)

## The purchase oath (custom)
- Buying and selling human captives is ordinary, permitted commerce under Dominion law.
- At a purchase, the buyer swears an oath before a registrar, under the glowing seal, to keep the human healthy and well. The Elites are strict about it; the oath is a general Val custom, sworn by ordinary soldiers as a matter of course.
- The oath is entirely separate from the Brand: the oath governs a purchase, the Brand ends all purchases. (Ruled 2026-11-17.)

## The household liturgy (custom)
- Recorded 2026-11-17 at the user's direction (ch007 review), from the ritual established in ch007 "The Routine". Documentation only; it fills the canon gap flagged in that review.
- A Bound's meal in a Val household follows a fixed liturgy, performed in Bog and Nia's household three to five times a day:
  1. The bells go first: she folds down onto the floor, forehead to the floor, back bared (prostration).
  2. The petition, recited in archaic / old Val, ending with the glottal catch before the closing phrase. Nia tacks on one short, sharp line of her own at the end - the only part of the liturgy that belongs to her; Bog accepts it and praises her pronunciation.
  3. A kiss pressed to each of Bog's green toes, because the liturgy asks for it (shown in ch010; a short beat was added to ch007's prostration section at the user's direction, 2026-11-17, ch010 review, so the step appears where the liturgy is first laid out).
  4. The meal itself under instruction: hold, move / stir, hold still and open to show, and swallow only on permission ("Good girl. You can swallow.").
  5. After she has eaten her fill, the thanksgiving prayer: the eater offers a prayer of thanks, in the old Val tongue, to the one who provided the meal - perfect cadence, the hushed, reverent drop at the end.
- The prayer and the forms are not Bog's invention: they were drilled into Nia at the front, where a wrong stress, wrong breath or wrong pause was answered by the collar. After the first week she could recite it in her sleep; she knows all 412 repetitions' worth by heart.
- Bog treats correct performance as a talent to be praised ("clever girl", "so devoted", "you've gotten so good at this") and does not appear to hear what the words mean to her.

## Slang: "the Polished"
- Ordinary Val soldiers' mocking name for the Val elites: **the Polished** - polished hides, polished seals, polished speeches; people who have never been scuffed by field work, mud, or an actual battlefield.
- Used among grunts and in a soldier's private thoughts. To an elite's face, a careful soldier says "the Elites". (Coined 2026-11-17 at the user's request, ch001 review.)
