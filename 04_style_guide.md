# 04_style_guide.md - Style and Anti-Repetition

> Migrated 2026-11-17 from Part D of `archive/source_bog_nia_story_context_summary.md`
> ("Writing conventions observed in this project").

## Style
- Point of view: Third person.
- Tense: Mostly past tense.
- Language: Story prose in English.
- Typical scene length: The user often provides bullet-style plot beats; expand them into full scenes of about 500-900 words and end on a small hook.
- Desired texture: Dark comedy with slapstick beats. Bog's obliviousness and Nia's dry fury drive the humor. Quiet Bog humor against other characters' sincerity or horror.
- Background specificity (ruled 2026-11-20, from the ch122 work): subtly incorporate background elements and props that convey a scene's atmosphere and lived-in reality, and name them specifically rather than generically — e.g. not "a tree" but "a shagbark hickory" for a scene set in northern Alabama; not "an old vinyl disc" in a jazz enthusiast's home but "a slightly worn red record with 'COLUMBIA' printed in white — a legacy to having survived the war" (an allusion to Miles Davis's *Kind of Blue*). The specifics should be correct for the place and the person, and stay background — atmosphere, not exposition.

## Figurative Language Policy (ruled 2026-11-17, ch04 review)
- Expressions in which Nia's words "land", or the bells hum / respond to the force of her speech, are FIGURATIVE - figures for her vehemence and presence, not literal supernatural effects. While collared, Nia works no curse or power with physical force; her spiritual side operates through the spirits' own rules (e.g. mate recognition), not through spoken curses. (This is the same policy under which the literal curse ending of ch03 was deleted in the ch03 review.)

## Character Voice Rules
- Swearing (ruled 2026-11-17): Val speakers — Bog at minimum — swear by "Holy Val" (e.g. "Holy Val," he murmured). Earth humans keep Earth expletives ("damn", "Damn it", "damn him"). A manuscript-wide sweep applied this to Bog's lines on 2026-11-17 (log in `reviews/terminology_log.md`). One adjective case (ch71 line 586, "the whole damn place") could not convert and was resolved by dropping the intensifier ("the whole place") — see the log.
- The "♥" symbol (ruled 2026-11-17, ch03 review): "♥" in captive women's speech is a general cartoon symbol of Val domestication/conditioning - an erotic, ridiculous piece of notation, sprinkled throughout the work wherever captive women speak under arousal, fluster, or conditioning. It is NOT unique to Nia or Sonya. What distinguishes Nia's is that hers is fueled also by her growing genuine affection for Bog, which she finds difficult to control.
- "♥" typography (ruled 2026-11-17, ch14 review D2): a heart always attaches directly to the preceding word with no space before it ("word♥ next word"), never as a standalone spaced token. The whole manuscript was normalized to this form on 2026-11-17 (612 hearts, count unchanged; details in reviews/terminology_log.md).
- Nia: "♥" appears between her words when she is aroused or flustered, fueled also by her growing affection for Bog, which she finds difficult to control.
- Sonya: Short, laconic lines; Russian curses and thoughts in Cyrillic; "♥" appears in her speech too, even while cursing. Name in narration: **Sonya** is fine and is the standard form (ruled by the user 2026-11-17, ch22 review A1 - the closed chapters ch19-22 all use Sonya in narration). "Sophia" is her full name and appears in formal/record contexts (e.g. the monster's scoreboard, "SOPHIA IVANOVA") and in later-arc prose where it already stands; do not sweep either form across chapters. Never "Sofia" / "Sofiya".
- The monster: Flat, literal, formal machine voice ("Awaiting orders, Commander", "Please define '...'"). Its reports read like clerical minutes ("Report", "Logged as...", "Vocabulary count: N"). It never mocks; it is courteous and literal.

## Recurring Beats (use, but rotate)
- The monster's logged vocabulary count; "Please define '...'"
- Sonya's denials logged as "cough" / "tactical analysis"; her "Не сдамся"
- Bog sneezing off-screen during the monster's scenes
- Old Guards scenes: Rin's loud, cartoonish energy against Darkwing's dry, laconic command; the formal armbands; Rin's "Boss" impressions

- Seasons (ruled 2026-11-20): prose seasonal cues should broadly match the season bands in 05_timeline.md, but the war with the Val Dominion has made Earth's climate abnormal (00_canon.md), so weather that does not fit the nominal season is normal in this world and needs no justification. Still no month names or calendar dates.

## Story Time (ruled 2026-11-18, after the ch96 "three days ago" invention)
- In-story time is tracked in `05_timeline.md`, anchored at Day 1 = the day Bog bought Nia (ch01); the current position also sits in the timeline block of `02_current_state.yaml`.
- Never state an elapsed duration in prose ("three days ago", "a week later", "that night", when it implies a specific gap) unless it can be derived from `05_timeline.md`. If it cannot be derived, do not write the duration at all — not even vaguely. Invented durations are canon errors, on the same level as invented ranks or names.

## Banned Patterns / Hard Rules
- The "paperwork" deadpan (ruled 2026-11-19): Bog's reflexive "That's a lot of paperwork" as a stock reaction to revelations was a Claude tic, not a trait the user set — it recurred until it annoyed. Do not use "paperwork" as Bog's canned deadpan. The word itself is fine in literal contexts (forms, Dominion administration, ch13's paperwork scene). Existing instances in closed chapters (ch67; ch79, including its title) stand as written — the user's ruling was to fix the guides only, not to reopen the prose.
- Florence-session convention: new characters and plot threads are added **only when the user specifies them**. Do not invent extras.
- No notes at the end of replies unless asked.
- Refinement requests: output only the polished text, no commentary or note at the end.
- Sexual and degradation content: as bold, compelling, and unique as the work's established tone (see 00_canon.md theme), handled per the project's existing practice.
